Case Scenario 1 · Mehrotra Saxena & Co. (5 MCQs × 2 marks)

Mehrotra Saxena & Co. (MSC) is a Lucknow firm of Chartered Accountants with six partners: CA Arvind Mehrotra, CA Kavya Saxena, CA Tarun Bhatia, CA Imran Qureshi, CA Pallavi Dubey and CA Nidhi Rastogi. CA Nidhi is a part-time practising partner. On weekdays she teaches at a university under the Council's specific permission. The firm celebrated its silver jubilee in July 2025 and placed a notice of the celebration in a Lucknow daily. The managing partner, CA Arvind, has also just published a book on GST litigation.

MSC is the statutory auditor of Gomti Polymers Ltd, a listed company with a turnover of ₹420 crore for FY 2025-26. The statutory audit fee is fixed at ₹60 lakh. In October 2025, Gomti's audit committee offered further work:

  • (a) A cost-optimisation advisory project for MSC at a fixed fee of ₹40 lakh.
  • (b) A management consultancy project in data analytics, at ₹28 lakh, for Avadh Insights Pvt Ltd. This company renders management consultancy services. CA Kavya's husband is one of its two directors.
  • (c) Representation before the GST Appellate Authority, at ₹35 lakh, for MSC.
  • (d) The tax audit under Section 44AB, at ₹12 lakh, for MSC.

MSC is a member of "Saxena Bhatia & Affiliates", a network registered with ICAI. Another member of that network, Rastogi Dubey & Co. (no common partner with MSC), has been approached by Gomti's CFO for its internal audit.

CA Tarun has been a director simplicitor of Gomti Holdings Ltd, the holding company of Gomti Polymers, since 2024. He attends only Board meetings and receives sitting fees. CA Arvind is a non-executive director of Kanpur Resins Ltd. MSC has been offered the statutory audit of Kanpur–Kansai Coatings Pvt Ltd, a joint venture of Kanpur Resins. CA Imran has registered as a Registration Authority for issuing digital signatures to clients. CA Pallavi is a member of the Board of Management of Lucknow Urban Co-operative Bank. She has no part in day-to-day operations, is not an auditor of the bank and receives only sitting fees.

For FY 2025-26, MSC has so far accepted 260 tax audit assignments under Section 44AB. It has also accepted 45 audits under Sections 44AD and 44ADA. No partner accepts tax audits in an individual capacity or through another firm. Separately, MSC is leading the formation of a new network with firms from Kanpur and Varanasi and a consulting entity in Dubai.

1. How many more tax audit assignments under Section 44AB can MSC accept in FY 2025-26 under the Council General Guidelines, 2008?

  • (A) 100
  • (B) 40
  • (C) Nil, as the limit is already exceeded
  • (D) 55

2. Which of the following correctly states the position under Chapter IX of the Council General Guidelines, 2008 for the work offered in (a) to (d)?

  • (A) All four may be accepted, since each assignment is individually below the statutory audit fee of ₹60 lakh.
  • (B) Items (a) and (b) together, totalling ₹68 lakh, exceed the statutory audit fee, so both cannot be taken by MSC and its associate concern while MSC remains statutory auditor. Items (c) and (d) are outside the ceiling.
  • (C) Only (a) may be accepted, because (a), (c) and (d) together exceed ₹60 lakh, and (b) is excluded because Avadh Insights is a separate company.
  • (D) The ceiling counts only fees payable to MSC itself. Item (b) is ignored, so (a), (c) and (d) may all be accepted.

3. Regarding the internal audit offered to Rastogi Dubey & Co. and non-audit work by network firms for Gomti Polymers, which statement is correct?

  • (A) Rastogi Dubey & Co. may accept the internal audit. It is a legally separate firm with no common partner, and registration of the network is deemed client consent.
  • (B) Rastogi Dubey & Co. cannot accept the internal audit. The other firms of the network, collectively, may receive non-audit fees from Gomti of up to three times the statutory audit fee, i.e. ₹1.80 crore.
  • (C) Rastogi Dubey & Co. cannot accept the internal audit. The other firms of the network, collectively, may receive non-audit fees from Gomti of up to the statutory audit fee, i.e. ₹60 lakh.
  • (D) Rastogi Dubey & Co. may accept the internal audit because Section 144 of the Companies Act, 2013 restricts only the auditor and its holding or subsidiary companies.

4. Which of the following positions is or are NOT permissible?

  • (i) CA Tarun continuing as a director simplicitor of Gomti Holdings Ltd while MSC audits Gomti Polymers Ltd.
  • (ii) CA Imran acting as a Registration Authority for digital signatures for clients.
  • (iii) CA Pallavi being a member of the Board of Management of Lucknow Urban Co-operative Bank.
  • (iv) MSC accepting the statutory audit of Kanpur–Kansai Coatings Pvt Ltd.
  • (A) (i) only
  • (B) (i) and (iv) only
  • (C) (i), (iii) and (iv) only
  • (D) (iv) only

5. For the new network being formed, which statement is INCORRECT?

  • (A) ICAI shall approve or reject the name applied for in Form A within 30 days of receipt. Approval of the name alone does not entitle the network to practise in its own name.
  • (B) The approved name is reserved for three months. The network must register in Form B within that period, failing which the name stands cancelled.
  • (C) Because the arrangement includes the Dubai entity, the Indian member firms must file a declaration in Form D within 30 days of entering into the network arrangement.
  • (D) MSC may also join the new network while remaining in "Saxena Bhatia & Affiliates", provided it reports the change in Form C within 30 days.

Answers: 1 (B): CGG 2008 Chapter VI allows 60 tax audits per partner, but a part-time practising partner is not counted, so the limit is 5 × 60 = 300. Audits under Sections 44AD, 44ADA and 44AE are not counted, so 300 − 260 = 40. (A) wrongly counts CA Nidhi (360 − 260). (C) wrongly counts the 44AD and 44ADA audits. (D) makes both errors. 2 (B): Chapter IX applies to listed companies. Fees for other work payable to the auditor and its associate concerns together must not exceed the statutory audit fee. Avadh Insights is an associate concern because a partner's relative (husband) is a director in it and it renders MCS. Audit under any other statute (the tax audit) and representation before an authority are excluded. (D) is the trap: associate concerns are added. 3 (B): Networking guidelines, ethical compliance: if one network firm is the statutory auditor, no network firm may accept internal audit or book-keeping. The ceiling for other firms of the network collectively is three times the statutory audit fee. (C) applies the ceiling meant for the auditing firm. (A) misuses "deemed consent", which relates only to the client's consent to the network itself. 4 (B): ESB decisions: the auditor of a subsidiary cannot be a director of its holding company (independence). A non-executive director of a company, or his firm, should not accept the statutory audit of that company's joint venture. A Registration Authority for digital signatures is permitted. Membership of the Board of Management of a primary (urban) co-operative bank is permitted, being similar to a director simplicitor. 5 (D): A firm is allowed to join only one network, and firms with common partners join only one network. Form C is for a change in the constitution of a registered network, not a route to dual membership. (A), (B) and (C) state the 30-day, 3-month and 30-day rules correctly. The silver jubilee notice and the book are distractors. A silver jubilee notice is permitted, and the book may carry "Chartered Accountant" and the firm name without attainments.

Case Scenario 2 · Narmada Agro Foods Ltd (5 MCQs × 2 marks)

Narmada Agro Foods Ltd (NAFL) is an unlisted public company in Indore, incorporated in March 2018. It processes soya and wheat. Since 2020 it has been the subject of three bank consortium meetings about restructuring. Its audited financial statements for FY 2025-26 show accumulated losses of ₹38 crore. Its entire net worth, before those losses, is ₹35 crore (paid-up capital plus free reserves). Joshi Pandit & Co. (JPC) have been its statutory auditors since FY 2021-22. Their audit fee of ₹4.5 lakh for FY 2024-25 appears as a provision in accounts signed by both NAFL and JPC. It is still unpaid. NAFL says it will pay "after the restructuring". JPC's FY 2024-25 report contained a qualification on inventory valuation. The Managing Director said in a board meeting that the auditors "do not understand the agro business".

The AGM for FY 2025-26 was fixed for 26 September 2026. On 15 September 2026, a shareholder, Mr Harish Vyas, gave special notice of a resolution to appoint CA Neha Kulkarni & Associates (NKA) in place of JPC. NAFL sent the notice to members on 18 September 2026. NAFL sent a copy to JPC by speed post and kept the certificate of posting.

NKA sent its Clause (8) communication to JPC in four ways:

  • (i) by ordinary post under a certificate of posting;
  • (ii) by e-mail to JPC's e-mail address registered with ICAI, from which an acknowledgement e-mail was received;
  • (iii) by Registered A.D., returned with the endorsement "office found locked";
  • (iv) by courier to an old address of JPC (different from the address registered with ICAI on that date), returned with the remark "no such office exists at this address".

The CFO of NAFL offered NKA a certificate signed by him stating that "all requirements of Sections 139 and 140 have been complied with". JPC submitted a representation for circulation to members. It set out how JPC had acted independently and conscientiously during its term and stated its willingness to continue. It also attached a two-page note titled "Value we delivered", listing ₹2.3 crore of cost savings identified by JPC and a testimonial from NAFL's former CFO.

6. Looking only at the dates of the special notice and of the notice to members, which conclusion should NKA draw?

  • (A) The procedure is valid because NAFL sent the notice to members at least 7 days before the AGM.
  • (B) The procedure is defective because the special notice must reach the company not earlier than three months but at least 14 clear days before the meeting. NKA should not accept until compliance is ascertained.
  • (C) The procedure is valid because the 14-day requirement applies only when an auditor is removed before the expiry of his term.
  • (D) The procedure is valid as long as a copy of the special notice was sent to JPC forthwith.

7. Ignoring the defect in Q6, can NKA accept the appointment while JPC's fee of ₹4.5 lakh remains unpaid?

  • (A) No. The fee is undisputed because it is provided in accounts signed by both, and Chapter VII of the CGG 2008 bars acceptance until it is paid.
  • (B) Yes. NAFL has been registered for at least five years and its accumulated losses equal or exceed its entire net worth, so it is a sick unit and the bar does not apply.
  • (C) Yes, but only if JPC issues a no-objection certificate.
  • (D) Yes, because a dispute about fees is not a valid professional reason for declining an audit.

8. Which of NKA's communications provide positive evidence of delivery, or are deemed delivered, under the Council's view on Clause (8)?

  • (A) (i), (ii) and (iii)
  • (B) (ii) and (iii) only
  • (C) (ii), (iii) and (iv)
  • (D) (ii) only

9. Which of the following is NOT something NKA is required to verify or obtain before accepting, as per the Council's guidelines under Clause (9)?

  • (A) A true copy of the special notice given by the member.
  • (B) Evidence that the special notice was effectively served on JPC, for example by hand or Registered A.D. NAFL's certificate of posting is not enough.
  • (C) A copy of the minutes of the general meeting at which the resolution is passed, verified by the Chairman.
  • (D) A no-objection certificate from JPC consenting to the change, in place of the CFO's certificate.

10. Evaluate JPC's representation.

  • (A) The whole representation is permissible, because Section 140(4) gives the retiring auditor a right to have it circulated.
  • (B) The statement of independent and conscientious conduct and the willingness to continue are permissible. The note "Value we delivered" with the testimonial amounts to canvassing and breaches Clause (6) of Part I of the First Schedule.
  • (C) The willingness to continue and the note both breach Clause (7) of Part I of the First Schedule.
  • (D) Only the willingness to continue breaches Clause (6), as it is a direct request for reappointment.

Answers: 6 (B): Cl (9) guidelines: the special notice under S.140(4) must be sent by members not earlier than three months but at least 14 days before the meeting, excluding the day of the notice and the day of the meeting. 15 Sep to 26 Sep leaves only 10 clear days. The 7-day rule in (A) concerns sending the notice to members, a separate requirement. Without compliance the incoming auditor should not accept. 7 (B): Chapter VII proviso and its Explanation 2: a sick unit is one registered for not less than five years with accumulated losses at the end of any financial year equal to or exceeding its entire net worth. NAFL was registered in 2018, with losses of ₹38 crore against ₹35 crore. (A) ignores the proviso. (D) is wrong because the fee is undisputed; the "fee dispute" principle applies only to disputed fees. 8 (B): Positive evidence comes from Registered A.D., by hand, an acknowledgement from the e-mail registered with ICAI, or UDIN. "Office found locked" on an A.D. is deemed delivered. A certificate of posting is not sufficient (J.S. Bhati). "No such office exists" is deemed delivered only if the address is the one registered with ICAI on the date of dispatch, so (iv) fails. 9 (D): The incoming auditor must verify the records himself. A management certificate is not enough. He obtains a true copy of the special notice and verified minutes, and checks effective service on the retiring auditor. An NOC from the retiring auditor is not required: the client can change auditors, and Cl (8) needs only written communication. 10 (B): Cl (6), Council view on representations under S.140(4): the letter should set out in a dignified manner how he acted independently and conscientiously and may state willingness to continue. It must not amount to canvassing or securing publicity. Highlighting contributions is soliciting (SM TYK 34). The MD's remark and the qualification are distractors. A qualified report is a reason to ascertain the full facts, not an automatic bar.

Case Scenario 3 · Kothari Iyer & Co. (5 MCQs × 2 marks)

Kothari Iyer & Co. (KIC) is a four-partner Pune firm established in 2001. It has grown quickly in GST and insolvency work. CA Meera Iyer, a partner, is an empanelled Insolvency Professional and an associate member of the Institute of Cost Accountants of India (ACMA). CA Rajat Kothari holds an LLB degree from Savitribai Phule Pune University, but he does not practise as an advocate. In FY 2025-26 KIC plans a "visibility drive" approved at its partners' retreat in Lonavala. The partners spent most of the retreat debating new office furniture and a staff bonus scheme.

The drive has four parts. First, a write-up in a Pune newspaper. The draft contains:

  • (i) "Year of establishment: 2001";
  • (ii) "Winner, Best Emerging Firm 2025, Pune Chamber of Commerce";
  • (iii) "Excellence in Public Finance Audit Award 2024, Government of Maharashtra";
  • (iv) the tagline "Numbers You Can Trust";
  • (v) the firm name in 16-point type;
  • (vi) KIC's FRN;
  • (vii) "Affiliated with Deccan & Affiliates (network registered with ICAI)".

Second, sponsorships:

  • (i) KIC sponsors a seminar of the Pune Branch of ICAI (a PoU), for which the CPE Directorate has given prior approval;
  • (ii) CA Rajat sponsors a CSR tree plantation drive, with banners reading "Sponsored by CA Rajat Kothari";
  • (iii) KIC sponsors a corporate cricket tournament, with the firm name displayed on the boundary;
  • (iv) CA Meera sponsors a CSR blood donation camp, with banners reading "Kothari Iyer & Co., Chartered Accountants".

Third, a new website. It runs on a pull model and includes:

  • (i) a page "Our esteemed clients" listing 25 client names (no regulator requires this);
  • (ii) GST educational videos;
  • (iii) a rotating banner advertisement of an accounting software vendor;
  • (iv) a link to KIC's LinkedIn page with a pop-up "Like and follow us for updates!";
  • (v) secure document log-ins for clients.

Fourth, new visiting cards for partners. Meanwhile, CA Rajat placed a classified advertisement in the ICAI Journal seeking a partnership. In it, he described himself as having "15 years of bank audit experience". He actually has 6 years. He repeated the same claim in KIC's response to a bank's tender for concurrent audit.

11. Which items must be removed from the draft write-up?

  • (A) (ii) and (iv) only
  • (B) (ii), (iv) and (v) only
  • (C) (i), (ii), (iv) and (v) only
  • (D) (ii), (iii), (iv) and (v) only

12. Which of the sponsorships are permissible?

  • (A) (i) only
  • (B) (i) and (ii) only
  • (C) (i), (ii) and (iv) only
  • (D) (ii) and (iv) only

13. Which website features breach the Council Guidelines for Advertisement, 2008?

  • (A) (i) and (iii) only
  • (B) (i), (iii) and (iv) only
  • (C) (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv) only
  • (D) (iii) and (iv) only

14. Which of the following may appear on a partner's visiting card without breaching Section 7 read with Clause (7) of Part I of the First Schedule? (i) "Insolvency Professional" on CA Meera's card; (ii) "ACMA" after CA Meera's name; (iii) "LLB" after CA Rajat's name; (iv) "Tax Consultant" on CA Rajat's card; (v) "Est. 2001" under the firm name.

  • (A) (i), (ii) and (iii) only
  • (B) (i), (ii), (iii) and (v) only
  • (C) (i) and (ii) only
  • (D) (ii), (iii) and (iv) only

15. CA Rajat's overstatement of experience in the ICAI Journal advertisement and in the tender response is professional misconduct under:

  • (A) Clause (2) of Part III of the First Schedule
  • (B) Clause (3) of Part III of the First Schedule
  • (C) Clause (3) of Part II of the Second Schedule
  • (D) Clause (7) of Part I of the First Schedule only

Answers: 11 (B): Advertisement Guidelines 2008, write-up conditions: (F) no awards except those given by the Central or State Government or regulatory bodies, so (ii) goes and (iii) stays. (G) no catch words, so (iv) goes. (I) font not exceeding 14, so (v) goes. "Year of establishment", the FRN (mandatory) and affiliation with a registered network are expressly allowed. Trap (C): the bar on the date of establishment applies to letterheads under Cl (7), not to write-ups. 12 (B): Cl (6), sponsoring: a firm may sponsor only an event of an ICAI PoU with prior CPE Directorate approval. For CSR, members may mention their individual name with the prefix "CA"; the firm name and CA logo are not permitted, so (iv) fails. (iii) is a firm sponsoring a non-ICAI event. 13 (B): Website guidelines: client names only where a regulator requires them; no banner or any other advertisement; a link to a social networking page is allowed, but members must not solicit people to visit or "like" it. Educational videos and client document log-ins are expressly allowed. 14 (A): Clause (7) allows a degree of a university established by law (LLB) and titles of recognised bodies. ACMA letters are allowed, but not the designation "Cost Accountant". "Insolvency Professional" may be mentioned by an empanelled member. "Tax Consultant" is not a degree or a recognised membership. The date of setting up practice or establishment of the firm should not be on letterheads and other professional documents. 15 (B): First Sch Pt III Cl (3): giving information knowing it to be false while inviting work from another CA, responding to tenders, or advertising through a write-up (items (6) and (7) of Part I). (A) is failing to supply information to the Institute. (C) is false particulars in submissions to the Institute or its bodies. A classified advertisement is not a submission to the Institute. The retreat and furniture are distractors.

Case Scenario 4 · Banerjee Ghosh & Associates (5 MCQs × 2 marks)

Banerjee Ghosh & Associates (BGA) is a Kolkata firm. It audits Hooghly Jute Ltd, a company listed on BSE with a net worth of ₹180 crore at 31 March 2026. During the FY 2025-26 audit, the engagement partner, CA Debashis Ghosh, found payments of ₹1.2 crore to a pollution-board consultant that looked like bribes for an effluent clearance. Separately, Hooghly's CFO, CA Ritu Sen, a key managerial person, had found the same payments while reviewing major expenditure. Most of the audit file deals with jute price hedging and an Ind AS 116 lease restatement, which took up the team's time.

In March 2026, BGA's accounts team recorded these client receipts:

  • (i) ₹85,000 received in advance for GST advisory services to be rendered next quarter;
  • (ii) ₹30,000 received for MCA filing fees and stamp paper, to be spent within the week;
  • (iii) ₹6.5 lakh received by CA Debashis as executor of the estate of the late Mr Arun Sen;
  • (iv) ₹4 lakh received from Sundarban Education Trust, to be placed in a fixed deposit "after the trustees decide the bank", expected in about five months.

BGA also issued a certificate to Sundarban Education Trust for utilisation of a ₹2.4 crore government grant. It relied on a summary prepared by the trustees. Vouchers for ₹1.1 crore were not seen, and the certificate did not state its basis, assumptions or limitations. BGA's fee was 0.5% of the utilisation amount certified.

In May 2026, the Trust's donor filed a complaint with ICAI. It alleged lack of due diligence on the certificate and also alleged that BGA had solicited the Trust by e-mailing a fee brochure to its trustees. In an unrelated matter, BGA's partner CA Sourav Banerjee was convicted by a criminal court in Kolkata of an offence punishable with imprisonment of up to two years. He was sentenced to eight months.

16. Which receipts must be put in a separate bank account immediately?

  • (A) (i), (iii) and (iv)
  • (B) (iii) and (iv) only
  • (C) (iii) only
  • (D) (ii), (iii) and (iv)

17. On the utilisation certificate, which statement is correct?

  • (A) BGA is guilty under Clauses (7) and (8) of Part I of the Second Schedule. Charging fees as a percentage of the utilisation amount is permissible as per the Ethical Standards Board.
  • (B) BGA is guilty under Clauses (7) and (8) of Part I of the Second Schedule, and also under Clause (10) of Part I of the First Schedule for the percentage-based fee.
  • (C) BGA is not guilty, because a certificate is only an opinion and the trustees' summary is sufficient evidence.
  • (D) BGA is guilty only under Clause (5) of Part I of the Second Schedule, for not disclosing a material fact.

18. Under the Code of Ethics as applicable in India, which statement about NOCLAR is correct?

  • (A) Section 360 applies to CA Debashis and Section 260 applies to CA Ritu.
  • (B) Section 260 applies to CA Ritu. Section 360 does not apply to CA Debashis, as Hooghly's net worth is below ₹250 crore, though SA 250 continues to apply to the audit.
  • (C) Section 360 applies to CA Debashis only, as Section 260 is limited to directors of listed entities.
  • (D) Neither applies, as NOCLAR in India covers only entities with a net worth of ₹250 crore or more.

19. The donor's complaint alleges misconduct under both Schedules. Where will the Director (Discipline) place it, and what is the maximum penalty if BGA's partner is found guilty?

  • (A) Board of Discipline: reprimand, removal of name for up to 3 months, fine up to ₹1,00,000.
  • (B) Disciplinary Committee: reprimand, removal of name permanently or for any period it thinks fit, fine up to ₹5,00,000. Appeal to the Appellate Authority within 90 days.
  • (C) The Board of Discipline for the First Schedule charge and the Disciplinary Committee for the Second Schedule charge, separately.
  • (D) Disciplinary Committee: removal of name for up to 3 years, fine up to ₹5,00,000. Appeal to the High Court within 30 days.

20. CA Sourav's conviction falls under:

  • (A) Clause (1) of Part IV of the First Schedule: other misconduct
  • (B) Part III of the Second Schedule: other misconduct
  • (C) No misconduct, as the offence is unrelated to professional work
  • (D) Clause (2) of Part IV of the First Schedule only, as it brings disrepute

Answers: 16 (B): Cl (10) Part I Second Sch, Council suggestions: an advance against services is outside Cl (10); money for expenses to be spent within a reasonably short time need not be put in a separate account; money not to be spent within a reasonably short time, and money received as trustee, executor or liquidator, must be put in a separate account immediately. (A) wrongly includes the advance. 17 (A): A certificate issued without seeing vouchers and without stating its basis or limitations shows a lack of due diligence and a failure to obtain sufficient information. The SM cites Cl (7) and (8) for certificates that do not state limitations or assumptions. ESB decision: fees as a percentage of the utilisation amount for certifying an educational institute's spending out of a grant are permissible, so (B) is wrong. A certificate is a written confirmation of facts, not an estimate or opinion, so (C) is wrong. 18 (B): Section 260 applies to senior professional accountants (KMP) employed by listed entities. Section 360 applies to audits of entities that are listed and have a net worth of ₹250 crore or more. (D) wrongly extends the net-worth test to Section 260. 19 (B): Where misconduct falls in the Second Schedule or both, the Director (Discipline) places the matter before the Disciplinary Committee. Its powers are reprimand, removal permanently or for any duration, and a fine up to ₹5,00,000. Appeal lies within 90 days to the Appellate Authority. 20 (B): Second Sch Part III: held guilty by a civil or criminal court of an offence punishable with imprisonment for a term exceeding six months. First Sch Pt IV Cl (1) covers terms not exceeding six months. Other misconduct need not relate to professional work. The hedging and lease work are distractors.

24. Which pairing of a circumstance with the threat it creates, as given in the Code of Ethics, is INCORRECT?

  • (A) An audit team member having a long association with the audit client: familiarity threat
  • (B) A professional accountant who accepted a significant gift from a client and is threatened that this will be made public: self-interest threat

Case Scenario 1 — Nirmala Polymers Ltd (SM Ch 17)

Nirmala Polymers Ltd (NPL), a listed manufacturer of PVC pipes in Pune, had a revenue of ₹1,860 crore in FY 2024-25. Its board approved in principle, on 12 June 2025, the purchase of a 74% stake in Ketan Packaging Pvt Ltd (KPPL), Vapi. A non-binding term sheet was signed the same day; the share purchase agreement is expected to be signed in October 2025. KPPL's founder, Mr. Ketan Desai, is 78 and wants to retire. NPL's own strategy team spent April and May 2025 studying whether KPPL's film lines would create operational synergies with NPL's extrusion plants, but its report is still in draft. NPL's CFO wants M/s RSK & Co., Chartered Accountants, to "start the financial due diligence at once and call it the complete due diligence, since money is all that matters".

RSK's preliminary review of KPPL shows: GST show cause notices of ₹4.2 crore that have not turned into demands and are not disclosed as contingent liabilities; a letter of comfort given to a bank for a sister concern's ₹30 crore cash credit; inter-company balances with two group companies that have not been reconciled for 18 months; customs duty on imported resin finalised only on a provisional basis; and quoted shares held as long-term investments carried at cost of ₹6 crore against a market value of ₹1.1 crore. KPPL changed its inventory valuation policy in FY 2024-25, the year it decided to look for a buyer.

Separately, NPL's internal auditor reported that transport payments at NPL's Nashik plant rose 41% in FY 2024-25 while dispatch tonnage rose only 6%. The board engaged CA Leena Shah as forensic professional. Her team extracted ERP payment logs, vendor master change logs and e-mails, but no one recorded who extracted each file, when, or where it was stored. Her draft report, discussed with the logistics manager as the mandate required, ends with: "Mr. Pradeep Kale, logistics manager, is guilty of colluding with two transport vendors."

  1. As per the SM, when is the thorough, detailed due diligence on KPPL typically conducted?
    • (A) Before NPL's board agrees in principle, to decide whether to talk to KPPL at all
    • (B) After the in-principle agreement and preliminary understanding (June 2025), but before the binding share purchase agreement (October 2025)
    • (C) After the share purchase agreement is signed but before completion, so that warranties are already fixed
    • (D) After completion, as a post-acquisition review of what was bought
  2. Which of RSK's findings fall in the SM's list of hidden liabilities? (i) GST show cause notices not matured into demands; (ii) letter of comfort to the bank; (iii) unreconciled group company balances; (iv) customs duty assessed only provisionally; (v) quoted investments carried at cost.
    • (A) (i), (ii) and (iv) only
    • (B) (i), (ii), (iii) and (iv) only
    • (C) (i), (iv) and (v) only
    • (D) All of (i) to (v)
  3. Regarding the CFO's instruction, which statement is in line with the SM?
    • (A) Tax due diligence is always a separate exercise and can never be included in financial due diligence
    • (B) Financial due diligence should be performed after completion of commercial due diligence, and the term "financial due diligence" should not be confused with an overall due diligence unless its scope covers all aspects
    • (C) Commercial/operational due diligence is generally performed by the target's statutory auditor, so RSK should complete it first
    • (D) Since financial due diligence ascertains the financial implications of all other reviews, it is appropriate to call it the complete due diligence
  4. What is the most likely consequence of the way CA Leena Shah's team collected the electronic evidence?
    • (A) None, because a forensic report does not express an opinion and evidence quality is therefore not critical
    • (B) The engagement must be converted into a statutory audit so that sampling can be applied
    • (C) The final report must carry an "except for" qualification on the electronic evidence
    • (D) The chain of custody is broken; the evidence may be challenged in court or even become inadmissible, defeating the objective of the engagement
  5. The last sentence of the draft forensic report is:
    • (A) Acceptable, because the subject party was given the draft for discussion as the mandate required
    • (B) Not acceptable; under FAIS 510 the report shall not express an opinion or pass judgement on guilt or innocence — culpability is for the internal disciplinary or judicial process
    • (C) Acceptable if the evidence is conclusive, because forensic work seeks conclusive evidence
    • (D) Acceptable only in an interim report, since interim reports are outside FAIS 510

Answers: 1 (B) — SM para 2 (p.17.4): detailed due diligence comes after parties agree in principle and reach a preliminary understanding, but prior to signing a binding contract. Trap (C): problems found must still be usable in price negotiation and warranty/indemnity clauses, so it must precede the binding contract. 2 (A) — SM p.17.10 lists show cause notices not matured into demands, letters of comfort (not "guarantees", hence undisclosed) and pending final customs assessments as hidden liabilities; "group company balances under reconciliation" and "investments carried at cost though realisable value is much lower" are in the over-valued assets list (p.17.11) — that is the trap in (B) and (C). 3 (B) — SM para 3 (p.17.5–17.6): financial due diligence should be performed after commercial due diligence; the term should be used with caution and not confused with overall review unless its scope is wide enough. (D) quotes the very view the SM calls "not appropriate"; (A) is wrong because tax due diligence is generally included in financial due diligence; (C) is wrong because commercial due diligence is generally done by the acquirer. 4 (D) — SM para 14 Step 3 (p.17.67) and TYK 18 answer: gaps in chain of custody mean evidence may be challenged or become inadmissible. (C) is the auditor's mindset; a forensic report expresses no audit opinion. 5 (B) — FAIS 510 point 5 (p.17.70). (A) is the trap: discussion of the draft only requires a summary of the subject's responses to be included; it does not permit a finding of guilt.

Case Scenario 2 — Deccan Urja Vitaran Nigam Ltd (SM Ch 15, 16)

Deccan Urja Vitaran Nigam Ltd (DUVNL), a power distribution company, was wholly owned by the State Government until March 2024. After an IPO in August 2024, the State Government holds 58% and the public 42%; the shares are listed on NSE. In FY 2025-26 the company had a turnover of ₹9,400 crore. Its Finance Manager told the board that "now that we are listed and watched by SEBI and the market, private-sector audit rules under the Companies Act will apply and the C&AG has no role left".

During FY 2025-26 an IAAD team under the Accountant General examined DUVNL's smart-meter programme. The team found that 2.4% of meters were defective against a permitted 1%, and that replacements took four to six months against the stipulated one month. It also found that a ₹14 crore divisional office building was completed in March 2021 with all sanctions in order but was occupied only in April 2025. The team asked three questions: (i) whether smart meters were bought at the best price and in due time; (ii) how the cost per new connection released compared with two neighbouring distribution companies; and (iii) whether the 3% fall in distribution losses was due to the smart-meter programme or to other causes, such as a tariff revision.

The State Legislature's financial committees are expected to take up the C&AG's report. One member suggested that the Audit Board that appraised DUVNL "should appear before the committee as an independent body in its own right".

Meanwhile, DUVNL's CFO wants the newly appointed Chief Internal Auditor, CA Rohan Kulkarni, to report to him, because "finance is the main area audited". He also wants CA Kulkarni to run the SEBI compliance-tracking system and correspond directly with SEBI, since "compliance with laws and regulations" is part of the agreed internal audit scope.

  1. Which statement about the Finance Manager's view is correct as per the SM?
    • (A) Correct; once listed, a company is governed only by the audit provisions for private-sector companies
    • (B) Incorrect; DUVNL remains a government company because not less than 51% of its paid-up capital is held by the State Government; its auditors are appointed by the C&AG, who gives directions, may comment on their report and may conduct a supplementary audit — listing is irrelevant
    • (C) Incorrect, but only because the C&AG reviews every company whose shares are listed
    • (D) Partly correct; the C&AG's role continues only for reports laid under Article 151, while members now appoint the auditor
  2. The observation about the divisional office building occupied four years after completion, though all sanctions were in order, is best described as an outcome of:
    • (A) The regularity aspect of compliance audit
    • (B) Propriety audit, which brings out improper, avoidable or infructuous expenditure even when incurred in conformity with rules
    • (C) Financial audit, which is always an attestation engagement
    • (D) The effectiveness aspect of performance audit
  3. Which of the following statements is INCORRECT as per the SM?
    • (A) The Estimates Committee does not comment on a policy approved by the legislature, but brings to the House's notice a policy that is not leading to the desired results
    • (B) COPU exercises over public undertakings the same financial control that the PAC exercises over Government departments
    • (C) The PAC satisfies itself that re-appropriation has been made in accordance with the provisions made
    • (D) Audit Boards are separate legal entities that work independently of the C&AG and present their own findings to the committees
  4. Questions (i), (ii) and (iii) asked by the IAAD team relate respectively to:
    • (A) Economy, Efficiency, Effectiveness
    • (B) Efficiency, Economy, Effectiveness
    • (C) Economy, Effectiveness, Efficiency
    • (D) Efficiency, Efficiency, Effectiveness
  5. Which course is consistent with the SM on CA Kulkarni's position?
    • (A) Report functionally to the CFO, as finance is the main audit area, and manage the compliance system because it is within scope
    • (B) Be positioned outside the functions he audits and report to the Audit Committee (administrative reporting to the MD/CEO is possible if the Board approves and independence is not affected); audit the compliance framework but not manage it or correspond directly with SEBI
    • (C) Manage the compliance system but refuse to correspond with SEBI, since only correspondence affects independence
    • (D) As DUVNL is a government company, the internal auditor must be appointed by the C&AG and report to the Accountant General

Answers: 1 (B) — SM p.15.6 and TYK 5 answer (s.2(45)): not less than 51% held by Government = government company; auditors appointed by the C&AG, who gives directions and has a right to supplementary/test audit; "listing of company's shares on a stock exchange is irrelevant". (D) is a trap mixing Article 151 (Government accounts) with company audit. 2 (B) — SM para 8.1 (p.15.29): the school building used five years after completion is the SM's example of avoidable expenditure; propriety audit looks at waste "even though the expenditure has been incurred in conformity with the existing rules". (A) fails because regularity is satisfied here. 3 (D) — SM p.15.6: Audit Boards have no separate legal entity and work under the supervision and control of the C&AG. (A), (B), (C) are SM text (p.15.7). 4 (A) — SM para 6: best price/due time = economy; cost per unit compared with others = efficiency (input-output; SM example "cost per job created"); whether impacts are due to the policy or other causes = effectiveness. 5 (B) — SM Ch 16 para 3 examples (listed company IA reports to Audit Committee, not CEO/CFO), TYK 6 answer (administrative reporting), TYU 2 answer (IA does not manage compliance framework or engage directly with regulators). (D) has no basis: s.138 appointment is by the company.
