SASQC 1 and SA 220 — Quality control

Two standards, two levels

  • SQC 1 = quality at the level of the firm; applies to all engagements (audits, reviews of historical financial information, other assurance and related services) and to all firms irrespective of constitution. Responsibility: CEO / managing partners.
  • SA 220 = quality at the level of the individual audit engagement; audits only. Responsibility: engagement partner. SA 220 is premised on the firm being subject to SQC 1 (SQC 1 is a sine qua non); engagement teams are entitled to rely on the firm's system unless information from the firm or others suggests otherwise.
  • Other facilitators: other SAs, Code of Ethics, certain provisions of the Companies Act, 2013. Review mechanisms: Peer Review Board, Quality Review Board, NFRA.
  • SQC 1 objective: reasonable assurance that (i) firm and personnel comply with professional standards and regulatory and legal requirements and (ii) reports issued by the firm or engagement partners are appropriate in the circumstances.

This SM covers SQC 1 and SA 220 only. SQM 1 components are not in the SM: answer every "elements" question with the six SQC 1 elements below.

SQC 1 — six elements Asked: Jan 2026 Q1(c)

  1. Leadership responsibilities for quality within the firm
  2. Ethical requirements
  3. Acceptance and continuance of client relationships and specific engagements
  4. Human resources
  5. Engagement performance
  6. Monitoring
  • Policies and procedures are documented and communicated to personnel; the firm encourages personnel to communicate their views or concerns on quality control matters (feedback).

2.1.1 Leadership responsibilities for quality Asked: Jan 2026 Q1(c)

  • Internal culture that quality is essential. CEO or managing partners assume ultimate responsibility for the system of quality control.
  • Persons given operational responsibility by the CEO/managing partners: sufficient and appropriate experience, ability, and the necessary authority.
  • Business strategy is subject to the overriding requirement to achieve quality in all engagements. Ensure: (a) management responsibilities assigned so that commercial considerations do not override quality; (b) policies on performance evaluation, compensation and promotion (including incentive systems) demonstrate the overriding commitment to quality; (c) sufficient resources for development, documentation and support of QC policies.

Examiner's twist: The story is about an expanding firm (partner good at bringing clients but weak on quality oversight; incentives on revenue; resources spent on marketing) and asks "which element?". Answer Leadership responsibilities, not Human resources, even though incentives and promotions appear: the SM places incentive systems under consideration (b) of leadership. The scoring line: "the firm's business strategy is subject to the overriding requirement to achieve quality in all engagements", then (a), (b), (c). TYK 1 (managing partner pushing a client who offers 25% higher fee, regulatory inquiries, auditor resigned) = same element plus integrity of client.

2.1.2 Ethical requirements and independence

  • Fundamental principles: integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality, professional behaviour. Emphasised by: actions of leadership, awareness and training, monitoring, a process for dealing with non-compliance.
  • Independence policies (firm, personnel, experts contracted by the firm, network firm personnel) enable the firm to: (a) communicate independence requirements to personnel; (b) identify and evaluate threats and eliminate or reduce them by safeguards, or withdraw.
  • Engagement partners give the firm client information; personnel promptly notify threats; all breaches promptly notified.
  • At least annually: written confirmation of compliance with independence policies from all personnel required to be independent.
  • Familiarity threat: criteria consider (a) nature of engagement, including public interest and (b) length of service of senior personnel. Safeguards: rotation of senior personnel or EQCR. Listed entity audits: engagement partner rotated after a pre-defined period, normally not more than seven years, except where the listed audit is conducted by a sole practitioner (mandatory peer review of such firms instead).

Examiner's twist: TYK 2 — a paid CA on a bank stock audit promises a clean stock report in return for the statutory audit. Asked "how does it reflect on the QC system?": QC policies must be documented and communicated to personnel; personnel are not sensitised to SQC 1. Do not stop at "violates the Code".

2.1.3 Acceptance and continuance

  • Information decides three things: integrity of client, promoters, KMP; competence (capabilities, time, resources); compliance with ethical requirements.
  • Obtain information at three points: new client; continuing an existing engagement; new engagement with an existing client. Issues identified but client accepted → document how the issues were resolved.
  • Integrity matters (7): identity and business reputation of principal owners, key management, related parties, TCWG · nature of operations and business practices · attitude to aggressive interpretation of accounting standards and internal control environment · client aggressively concerned with keeping fees as low as possible · indications of inappropriate scope limitation · indications of money laundering or other criminal activities · reasons for proposed appointment and non-reappointment of previous firm. Knowledge grows with an ongoing relationship.
  • Capability matters (6): industry/subject knowledge · experience of regulatory or reporting requirements (or ability to gain it) · sufficient personnel · experts available · individuals eligible for EQCR available · ability to meet the reporting deadline.
  • Conflict of interest resolved before accepting.
  • Information that would have caused the firm to decline, obtained later → consider (a) professional and legal responsibilities, including reporting to the appointing person(s) or regulators; (b) possibility of withdrawing from the engagement or from both engagement and client relationship.
  • Withdrawal policies (4): discuss with appropriate level of management and TCWG the appropriate action · if withdrawing, discuss withdrawal and reasons · consider professional, regulatory or legal requirement to remain in place, or to report the withdrawal and reasons to regulators · document significant issues, consultations, conclusions and basis.

2.1.4 Human resources

  • Sufficient personnel with capabilities, competence and commitment to ethical principles. Covers recruitment, compensation, training, career development, performance evaluation; emphasis on CPD.
  • Each engagement assigned to an engagement partner. Policies require: (a) identity and role of EP communicated to key members of client management and TCWG; (b) EP has appropriate capabilities, competence, authority and time; (c) EP's responsibilities clearly defined and communicated to the EP.

2.1.5 Engagement performance — consultation, EQCR, differences Asked: Sep 2025 Q1(a); May 2026 MCQ

  • Consistency through briefing teams, processes for complying with standards, supervision and training, methods of review, documentation.
  • Consultation on difficult or contentious matters, with people within or outside the firm with specialised expertise; a firm without internal resources may use advisory services of other firms or professional and regulatory bodies; document issues and results.
  • EQCR: significant judgments reviewed for an objective view before the report is issued. Extent depends on complexity and risk of an inappropriate report. Does not reduce the engagement partner's responsibility. Mandatory for all audits of financial statements of listed entities; for others the firm devises criteria.
  • EQCR for listed audits (SQC 1, 8 points): team's evaluation of firm's independence · significant risks and responses · judgments, particularly materiality and significant risks · appropriate consultation on differences of opinion / contentious matters and conclusions · significance and disposition of corrected and uncorrected misstatements · matters to be communicated to management, TCWG and, where applicable, regulators · whether working papers selected reflect work on significant judgments and support conclusions · appropriateness of the report to be issued.
  • Who can be EQCR: a partner, other person in the firm (member of ICAI), suitably qualified external person (e.g. partner or experienced employee of another firm), or a team of such individuals. For listed audits: sufficient and appropriate experience and authority to act as audit engagement partner on listed entity audits.
  • Objectivity: EQCR must not participate in the engagement or make decisions for the team. EP may consult the EQCR during the engagement without compromising eligibility; if consultations become significant and objectivity cannot be kept, another individual within the firm or a suitably qualified external person takes the role of EQCR or of the person consulted. Firm policies provide for replacement of an EQCR whose objectivity may be impaired.
  • Differences of opinion (within team, with those consulted, EP vs EQCR): report issued only after resolution. EP rejects EQCR's recommendation → firm's established procedures, e.g. consult another practitioner or firm, or a professional or regulatory body.

Examiner's twist: (1) TYK 4 — team got an ICAI expert opinion, so "EQCR lost relevance": wrong; the expert opinion addresses interpretation, EQCR is a separate mandatory requirement for listed audits. (2) Sep 2025 — a person with no listed-audit experience "signs a few working papers": fails both eligibility (experience and authority to act as EP on listed audits) and the documentation requirement. (3) EQCR "decides the discount rate" for the team: objectivity lost, replace.

Engagement documentation — assembly, ownership, retention

  • Assembly: complete final engagement files on a timely basis after reports are finalised — not more than 60 days after the date of the auditor's report for audits; other engagements: limits appropriate to the engagement. Two or more reports on the same subject matter (e.g. component report for group consolidation, later statutory report) → time limit applied to each report as a separate engagement.
  • Policies maintain confidentiality, safe custody, integrity, accessibility and retrievability.
  • EQCR documentation (SQC 1): (a) firm's EQCR procedures performed; (b) EQCR completed before the report is issued; (c) reviewer not aware of unresolved matters making significant judgments/conclusions inappropriate.
  • Ownership: documentation is the property of the firm unless law or regulation says otherwise; extracts may be given to clients if this does not undermine validity of the work or independence.
  • Retention: long enough for monitoring, or longer if law requires. Audits: ordinarily no shorter than seven years from the date of the auditor's report, or, if later, the date of the group auditor's report.
  • Scanned paper documents (TYK answer 9): copies reflect entire content including manual signatures, cross-references and annotations; integrated into files with indexing and sign-off; retrievable and printable. Working papers should show the source (client-prepared vs team-prepared).

2.1.6 Monitoring Asked: Nov 2024 Q1(a)

  • Policies are relevant, adequate, operating effectively and complied with in practice; include an ongoing consideration and evaluation of the system, including a periodic inspection of a selection of completed engagements.
  • Factors (6): whether the QC system is appropriately designed and effectively implemented · whether new developments in professional standards and legal/regulatory requirements are reflected · monitoring entrusted to a partner or other person with sufficient and appropriate experience and authority · dealing with complaints and allegations of non-compliance (from within or outside the firm) · remedial action against personnel who did not conform · action when deficiencies in design/operation or non-compliance are identified.

SASA 240 — Fraud

SA 240 — objectives, fraud, two types Asked: Jan 2026 Q5(b), Q6(c)

  • Objectives: (a) identify and assess RoMM due to fraud; (b) obtain SAAE on assessed risks through appropriate responses; (c) respond appropriately to identified or suspected fraud.
  • Fraud = intentional act by management, TCWG, employees or third parties, using deception to obtain an unjust or illegal advantage. Fraud vs error = intentional or unintentional. Auditor is concerned only with fraud causing material misstatement and does not make legal determinations.
  • Two types: fraudulent financial reporting and misappropriation of assets; both involve incentive/pressure, perceived opportunity, rationalisation.
  • FFR accomplished by: manipulation, falsification (incl. forgery) or alteration of records · misrepresentation or intentional omission of events/transactions · intentional misapplication of accounting principles. Management may want to reduce earnings (tax) or inflate them (bank financing).
  • Override techniques (6): fictitious JEs near period end · inappropriate adjustment of assumptions/judgments for estimates · omitting, advancing or delaying recognition · concealing facts · complex transactions structured to misrepresent · altering records and terms of significant unusual transactions.
  • Misappropriation (often employees, small amounts; management can conceal better): embezzling receipts · stealing physical assets or IP · paying for goods/services not received (fictitious vendors/employees, kickbacks) · personal use of assets (collateral for personal loan).

Who is responsible

  • Primary responsibility for prevention and detection: TCWG and management; emphasis on prevention (reduces opportunity) and deterrence (likelihood of detection and punishment); culture of honesty, active oversight by TCWG.
  • Auditor: reasonable assurance that FS as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether fraud or error. Risk of not detecting fraud > error (concealment, forgery, collusion — collusion makes false evidence look persuasive). Detection depends on skill of perpetrator, frequency and extent of manipulation, degree of collusion, size of amounts, seniority. Management fraud is harder to detect than employee fraud.
  • Auditor maintains skepticism, considers management override, and recognises that procedures effective for error may not detect fraud.

Skepticism, team discussion, risk assessment

  • Skepticism throughout, notwithstanding past experience of honesty and integrity of management and TCWG. Records may be accepted as genuine unless reason to believe otherwise; doubt on authenticity or undisclosed modified terms → investigate further; inconsistent responses of management/TCWG → investigate.
  • Team discussion: how and where FS are susceptible to fraud, notwithstanding beliefs that management and TCWG are honest.
  • Risk assessment: inquiries of management and others; how TCWG oversee management's fraud-risk process and controls; unexpected relationships from analytical procedures; evaluate whether fraud risk factors are present.
  • Revenue recognition: presumption of fraud risk; may be rebutted, e.g. single type of simple revenue (leasehold revenue from a single unit rental property). Rebutted → document reasons. Complex revenue or weak controls → high probability of fraud risk (TYK 14, Arihant ₹43 crore unrecorded bookings, part in CFO's account).

Responses to fraud risk

  • FS level (3): (a) assign and supervise personnel according to knowledge, skill and ability and the fraud risk; (b) evaluate whether selection and application of accounting policies (subjective measurements, complex transactions) indicate earnings management; (c) incorporate unpredictability in nature, timing and extent.
  • Assertion level: change NTE for more reliable/relevant evidence. SM example: earnings pressure → risk of inflated sales (terms precluding recognition, invoicing before delivery) → external confirmations of sales agreement details (date, rights of return, delivery terms) plus inquiries of non-financial personnel.
  • Management override: present in all entities; unpredictable → a fraud risk and a significant risk. Irrespective of assessment: (a) test journal entries and other adjustments; (b) review estimates for bias; (c) evaluate business rationale of significant transactions outside normal course or unusual. Determine whether other procedures are needed.
  • Evaluation: final analytical procedures may show an unrecognised fraud risk. Misstatement indicative of fraud → consider reliability of representations; fraud is unlikely to be an isolated occurrence. Misstatement (material or not) that may be fraud involving senior management → re-evaluate fraud risk and NTE; consider collusion with employees, management or third parties when reconsidering evidence.

Representations, communication, documentation

  • Written representations from management (and TCWG where applicable) (4): (a) responsibility for internal control to prevent and detect fraud; (b) disclosed results of management's fraud risk assessment; (c) disclosed knowledge of fraud/suspected fraud involving management, employees with significant roles in internal control, or others where material; (d) disclosed allegations of fraud communicated by employees, former employees, analysts, regulators or others.

  • TCWG (unless all TCWG manage the entity): fraud involving management, employees with significant roles in IC, or others causing material misstatement → timely. Suspected management fraud → communicate suspicions and discuss NTE of procedures needed to complete the audit. Plus any other fraud matters relevant to TCWG.
  • Outside the entity: determine whether there is a responsibility to report; legal duty may override confidentiality (e.g. bank auditor → RBI). Some entities: duty to report misstatements where management and TCWG fail to act.
  • Documentation: significant decisions from team discussion; identified and assessed fraud risks at FS and assertion level; overall responses and NTE linked to assertion-level risks; results, including management-override procedures; communications about fraud to management, TCWG, regulators and others; reasons for rebutting the revenue presumption.

SASA 250 — Laws and regulations

SA 250 — scope, two categories, objectives Asked: Sep 2025 Q2(a); May 2026 MCQ

  • Does not apply to other assurance engagements where the auditor is specifically engaged to test and report separately on compliance with laws.
  • Heavily regulated (banks, chemical companies) vs only operating laws (occupational safety and health). Non-compliance → fines, litigation, other consequences that may be material.
  • Objectives: (a) SAAE on compliance with laws having a direct effect on material amounts and disclosures; (b) specified procedures to identify non-compliance with other laws that may be material; (c) respond appropriately to identified or suspected non-compliance.
  • Responsibility for compliance: management, with oversight of TCWG. Entity policies (7): monitor legal requirements; internal control; code of conduct; training; monitor code and discipline; legal advisors; register of significant laws and record of complaints. Larger entities: internal audit, audit committee, compliance function.

  • Fundamental-effect laws may cause the entity to cease operations or question going concern: bank — capital or investment requirements; NBFC — failure to obtain certificate of registration under RBI Act or NOF below the RBI limit.
  • Auditor is not responsible for preventing non-compliance and cannot be expected to detect all. Inherent limitations are greater because: many operating laws do not affect FS and are not captured by information systems; non-compliance may be concealed (collusion, forgery, failure to record, override, misrepresentation); whether an act is non-compliance is ultimately for a court of law. The further removed from FS, the less likely the auditor is to notice.

  • When identified/suspected: obtain (a) understanding of the nature of the act and circumstances; (b) further information on possible effect on FS. Discuss with management (and TCWG). SASA 265, 315, 330 — Risk and internal control

Audit risk and its components

  • Audit risk: risk of expressing an inappropriate opinion when FS are materially misstated. AR = IR × CR × DR; AR = RMM × DR; RMM = IR × CR.
  • Combined IR and CR is inversely related to DR; materiality inversely related to audit risk. Objective: reduce AR to acceptably low level — assess RMM, limit DR.
  • Two levels: FS level (pervasive — incompetent top accountant affects many assertions) and assertion level (payables valuation low vs inventory valuation high).
  • SM TYK 7: IR 40%, controls prevent 75% → CR 25%, RMM 10%; detection satisfaction 60% → DR 40%; AR 4%.

Risk-based audit — three phases

  • Risk assessment: acceptance/continuance; planning; risk assessment procedures (inquiries, inspection, observation); identify controls and assess design and implementation; assess RMM; identify significant risks and risks where substantive procedures alone are not sufficient; communicate material weaknesses in design/implementation to management and TCWG.

Internal control — definition, tests of control, scope, limitations

  • SA 315 definition: process designed, implemented and maintained by TCWG, management and other personnel for reasonable assurance on reliability of financial reporting, effectiveness and efficiency of operations, safeguarding of assets, compliance with laws.
  • Tests of control needed to support any CR assessment less than high; the lower the CR, the more evidence. Consider how applied, consistency during the period and by whom. Observation evidence relates only to the point in time. Different controls at different times → consider each; breakdown for a portion → separate consideration.
  • Scope extends to administrative controls; auditor evaluates only those bearing on reliability of financial records.
  • Limitations: cost vs benefit; not directed at unusual transactions; human error; collusion; abuse/override by responsible person; management manipulation of transactions or estimates. Gives reasonable, not absolute assurance.

SA 450 — Evaluation of Misstatements Asked: Jan 2026 Q1(b)

  • Misstatement: difference in amount, classification, presentation or disclosure from FRF; from error or fraud. Uncorrected misstatements: accumulated and not corrected.

2 SRS 4400 Agreed-upon procedures Asked: Nov 2024 Q6(b)

  • Objective: carry out procedures of an audit nature agreed by auditor, entity and any appropriate third parties, and report factual findings. No assurance; non-assurance engagement.
  • May cover individual items (payables, receivables, related-party purchases, segment sales/profits), a balance sheet or even a complete set of FS.
  • Ethics: Code of Ethics — integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality, professional conduct, technical standards. Independence as in table.
  • Matters to agree (with entity and ordinarily other specified parties): (a) nature — not an audit or review, no assurance; (b) stated purpose; (c) financial information to which procedures apply; (d) nature, timing and extent of specific procedures; (e) limitations on distribution — if in conflict with law, do not accept.
  • Engagement letter includes: listing of procedures agreed; statement that distribution is restricted to specified parties who agreed the procedures.
  • Plan the work; carry out agreed procedures; use evidence as basis for report.

2.4 AUP report of factual findings (a)–(o)

  • Title · addressee (ordinarily appointing authority) · identification of specific financial or non-financial information · procedures were those agreed with the recipient · performed per SRS for AUP · purpose · listing of specific procedures · factual findings with sufficient detail of errors and exceptions · not an audit or review, no assurance · had additional procedures, an audit or review been performed, other matters might have come to light · restricted to parties who agreed · (when applicable) relates only to elements specified, not FS as a whole · date · place · signature.
  • The four bold "scope" statements are the answer to "precautions so that readers do not misunderstand scope" (TYK 7, CA Kamna).
  • Documentation: matters important to support the report of factual findings and evidence of compliance with SRS and terms.

3 SRS 4410 Compilation engagements Asked: May 2024 Q4(b); May 2025 Q3(c); May 2026 Q1(c)

  • Assist management with preparation and presentation of historical FI without obtaining assurance, and report. Non-historical or non-financial compilations → may use SRS 4410 with adaptation. SQC 1 applies (SRS 4410 is an Engagement Standard).
  • Not assurance; no need to verify accuracy or completeness, or gather evidence for an opinion/conclusion.
  • Purposes: mandatory periodic reporting under law; or others — internal use by management/TCWG; external parties under contract (e.g. funding body for a grant); transactional (merger, acquisition, change in ownership/financing).
  • Objectives: apply accounting and financial reporting expertise to assist management per AFRF based on management's information; report per SRS.
  • Management retains responsibility incl. judgments, policies and estimates (may have prepared a draft). Framework ranges from entity-specific basis to established standards.
  • Ethics: comply; identify and address threats. Independence not applicable; law may specify.
