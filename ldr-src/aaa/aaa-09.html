Ch 9 — Related Services (SRS 4400 & SRS 4410)

Two standards, both non-assurance. Examiners love: AUP report contents · engagement letter · compilation: ethics, SQC 1, withdrawal, documentation. Asked Nov 2024 (Q6b – AUP engagement letter, ATP / RS Ltd) Asked May 2024 (Q4b – compilation, CA Lalit, SQC 1)

1Related services — meaning (SM 1)

  • CAs are often asked for services that do not express an opinion on truth and fairness. The AASB of ICAI issued two Standards on Related Services (SRS) to standardise them: SRS 4400 Engagements to Perform Agreed-upon Procedures Regarding Financial Information · SRS 4410 Compilation Engagements.
  • Assurance engagement = practitioner expresses a conclusion designed to enhance the degree of confidence of intended users (other than the responsible party) about the outcome of evaluation / measurement of a subject matter against criteria.
  • Engagements that are not assurance: (i) preparation of tax returns where no assurance conclusion is expressed (ii) consulting / advisory (management and tax consulting) (iii) engagements under SRS — agreed-upon procedures and compilations. All of these are related services.
  • A person performing related services need not be the auditor of the entity's financial statements.
Mnemonic — 3 non-assurance engagements: T-C-S = Tax returns (no conclusion) · Consulting / advisory · SRS engagements (AUP + compilation). "TCS — they just serve, they don't assure."

"Auditor" in SRS 4400 does not mean statutory auditor of the entity. CA Kamna (TYK Q7) rightly accepted an AUP on receivables though not the company's statutory auditor. SA 501 is an audit standard — it is not the standard for such engagements (opening story).

2SRS 4400 — Agreed-upon procedures (AUP): objective (SM 2, 2.1)

  • What: auditor is engaged by the client to issue a report of factual findings, based on specified procedures performed on specified subject matter — specified elements, accounts or items of a financial statement (eg accounts payable, receivables, related-party purchases, segment sales and profits, a balance sheet, or even a complete set).
  • Objective: carry out procedures of an audit nature to which the auditor, the entity and any appropriate third parties have agreed, and report on factual findings.
  • No assurance — users assess the procedures and findings and draw their own conclusions. Report is usually restricted to parties who agreed the procedures (others, unaware of the reasons, may misinterpret).

3Audit vs AUP (SM 2.2)

AuditAgreed-upon procedures
OutputExpresses an opinionOnly a report of factual findings
AssuranceProvides assurance to usersNo assurance; users draw own conclusions
ExampleAUP to evaluate validity of accounts payable: (1) compare names of major suppliers & amounts outstanding on a date to the trial balance (2) obtain supplier statements / confirmations (3) compare them to TB amounts → report the variations as factual findings, no assurance.

4General principles of an AUP engagement (SM 2.3)

  • Auditor must comply with the Code of Ethics of ICAI. Ethical principles (6): integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality, professional conduct, technical standards.
  • Independence is NOT a requirement for an AUP engagement. But the terms or objective of the engagement may require compliance with the independence requirements of the Code. If the auditor is not independent, a statement to that effect must be made in the report of factual findings.
Mnemonic — ethical principles: I-O-P-C-P-T = "In Our Practice, Clients Place Trust" → Integrity · Objectivity · Professional competence & due care · Confidentiality · Professional conduct · Technical standards.

"Independence is not a requirement" ≠ "independence is irrelevant": if terms demand it, comply; if the auditor is not independent, say so in the report.

5Defining terms of engagement, planning, procedures & evidence (SM 2.3.1 – 2.3.3)

  • Terms (2.3.1): ensure, with entity representatives and ordinarily other specified parties who will get the report, a clear understanding of the agreed procedures and conditions. Send an engagement letter documenting key terms — it confirms acceptance and avoids misunderstanding on objectives and scope, extent of auditor's responsibilities, form of reports.
  • Planning (2.3.2): plan the work so that an effective engagement is performed.
  • Procedures & evidence (2.3.3): carry out the agreed procedures and use the evidence as the basis for the report of factual findings.

Matters to be agreed (5)

  1. Nature of engagement — procedures will not constitute an audit or review; no assurance expressed
  2. Stated purpose of the engagement
  3. Identification of the financial information to which AUP will be applied
  4. Nature, timing and extent of the specific procedures
  5. Limitations on distribution of the report. If limitation conflicts with legal requirements, auditor would not accept the engagement

Engagement letter would include

  • A listing of the procedures to be performed as agreed between the parties
  • A statement that distribution of the report of factual findings is restricted to the specified parties who have agreed to the procedures

Procedures may include (5)

Inquiry and analysis · Re-computation, comparison and other clerical accuracy checks · Observation · Inspection · Obtaining confirmations

Mnemonic — matters to be agreed: N-S-I-P-L = "Never Skip Identifying Procedures & Limits" → Nature (no audit/review) · Stated purpose · Identification of info · Procedures (nature, timing, extent) · Limitation on distribution.
Mnemonic — AUP procedures: I-R-O-I-C = "I Run Only Instructed Checks" → Inquiry & analysis · Recomputation · Observation · Inspection · Confirmations.

Answer frame — "enumerate matters to agree / include in the engagement letter" (Nov 2024, ATP Ltd for RS Ltd receivables & inventory): (1) Provision: SRS 4400 para 2.3.1 / 2.3.3 — clear understanding, engagement letter. (2) Requirement: the 5 matters (nature, purpose, identification, procedures, limits on distribution) + list of procedures + restricted distribution. (3) Application: nature = no audit/review/no assurance; purpose = ATP's investment decision; information = accounts receivable and inventory of RS Ltd; procedures = eg confirmations, inspection, observation; distribution = ATP only. (4) Conclusion: letter confirms acceptance, avoids misunderstanding on scope, responsibility and form of report.

Ch 9 · SRS 4400 — reportingREPORT OF FACTUAL FINDINGS

6Reporting — report of factual findings (SM 2.4)

Report must describe the purpose and the agreed-upon procedures in enough detail for the reader to understand the nature and extent of work, and clearly mention that no audit or review has been performed. It should contain (15):

  1. Title
  2. Addressee (ordinarily the appointing authority)
  3. Identification of specific financial / non-financial information to which AUP applied
  4. Statement that procedures performed were those agreed upon with the recipient
  5. Statement that engagement performed per the SRS applicable to AUP engagements
  6. Identification of the purpose for which AUP were performed
  7. A listing of specific procedures performed
  8. Description of factual findings incl. sufficient details of errors and exceptions found
  1. Statement that procedures do not constitute an audit or a review; no assurance expressed
  2. Statement that had the auditor performed additional procedures, an audit or a review, other matters might have come to light and been reported
  3. Statement that report is restricted to parties that agreed to the procedures
  4. Statement (when applicable) that report relates only to the elements, accounts, items or information specified and does not extend to the entity's financial statements as a whole
  5. Date of the report
  6. Place of signature
  7. Auditor's signature
Mnemonic — AUP report (15) in 4 blocks, "Name it, List it, Disclaim it, Sign it":
NAME (a–f): Title · Addressee · Information identified · procedures Agreed with recipient · Standard (SRS) · Purpose → "T-A-I-A-S-P"
LIST (g–h): Listing of procedures · Factual findings (errors & exceptions)
DISCLAIM (i–l): No audit/review-no assurance · Additional procedures might reveal more · Restricted to agreeing parties · Only specified items, not whole FS → "N-A-R-O" (narrow)
SIGN (m–o): Date · Place · Signature

Report gives findings, not an opinion or conclusion. Items (i)–(l) are the four disclaimers examiners ask for ("precautions so readers do not misunderstand scope" – TYK Q7(b) lists exactly (i), (j), (k), (l)). Findings must show errors and exceptions in sufficient detail.

7Documentation (SM 2.5)

Auditor should document matters important in providing evidence to support the report of factual findings, and evidence that the engagement was carried out in accordance with this SRS and the terms of the engagement.

8SRS 4410 — Compilation engagements: meaning & scope (SM 3, 3.1, 3.2)

  • Deals with practitioner's responsibilities when engaged to assist management in the preparation and presentation of historical financial information without obtaining any assurance on it, and to report in accordance with the SRS. Non-financial / non-historical information: can use the Standard after necessary adaptation.
  • Compilation engagement = practitioner applies accounting and financial reporting expertise to assist management in preparation and presentation of financial information per an applicable financial reporting framework, and issues a report.
  • Not an assurance engagement: no need to verify accuracy or completeness of management's information, nor to gather evidence for an audit opinion or review conclusion.
  • Purposes (SM TYK Q1): (A) to comply with mandatory periodic reporting under law / regulation; (B) purposes unrelated to mandatory reporting, eg (i) internal use by management / TCWG (ii) periodic reporting to external parties under contract, eg funding body / grant (iii) transactional purposes — change in ownership or financing, eg merger or acquisition.
  • Objectives (2): (a) apply accounting and financial reporting expertise to assist management in preparation and presentation per the framework, based on information provided by management; (b) report per the SRS.
  • Scope varies, but always: assisting management, per the framework, based on information provided by management (management may already have drafted). Management retains responsibility for the information and its basis, incl. judgments, selection / application of accounting policies, and reasonable accounting estimates. Frameworks range from a simple entity-specific basis to established reporting standards — depends on entity and intended use.
Mnemonic — purposes: L-I-C-T = "Law, then Internal, Contract (grant), Transaction (merger / acquisition)".

9Ethical requirements & independence (SM 3.3) — TYK Q2

  • Practitioner shall comply with relevant ethical requirements (Code of Ethics); threats to compliance must be identified and appropriately addressed.
  • Being non-assurance, independence requirements do not apply. However, laws or regulations may specify independence requirements or disclosure rules.

Q2 trap: "Is he required to comply with the Code of Ethics?" → Yes (relevant ethical requirements, threats addressed). "Independence?" → Not required (non-assurance), unless law or regulation says otherwise. In the case study, omitting the Rourkela unit expenditure can mislead; disregarding it violates integrity and the engagement cannot be performed in accordance with ethical requirements (answer 4a).

10Engagement acceptance & continuance (SM 3.4)

Shall not accept unless terms agreed with management (and the engaging party, if different), including (6):

  1. Intended use and distribution of the information, and any restrictions
  2. Identification of the applicable financial reporting framework
  3. Objective and scope of the compilation
  4. Responsibilities of practitioner, incl. complying with relevant ethical requirements
  5. Responsibilities of management (see box)
  6. Expected form and content of the practitioner's report

Management is responsible for (4)

  1. The financial information and its preparation / presentation per a framework acceptable for intended use and users
  2. Internal control — design, implementation, maintenance as management determines necessary for FS free from material misstatement (fraud or error)
  3. Accuracy and completeness of records, documents, explanations and other information provided
  4. Judgments needed, incl. those where practitioner assists

Record agreed terms in an engagement letter or other suitable written agreement, prior to performing. Recurring engagements: evaluate whether changed circumstances require revising the terms, and whether to remind management of existing terms.

Mnemonic — terms (6): U-F-O-P-M-R = "UFO, Please Meet Reports" → Use & distribution · Framework · Objective & scope · Practitioner's duties · Management's duties · Report form.
Mnemonic — management's 4: I-C-A-J = "I Control All Judgments" (management does) → Information & framework · Control (internal) · Accuracy & completeness of records · Judgments.

Ch 9 · SRS 4410 — performingSTEPS

11Performing the engagement (SM 3.5) & communication (3.6)

  1. Obtain an understanding sufficient to perform the engagement: entity's business and operations (incl. accounting system and records) and the applicable framework, incl. its application in the entity's industry.
  2. Compile using records, documents, explanations and information, including significant judgments, provided by management.
  3. Discuss with management / TCWG the significant judgments on which you assisted.
  4. Read the compiled information, before completion, in light of your understanding of business and the framework.
  5. Information incomplete, inaccurate or unsatisfactory → bring to management's attention and request additional / corrected information.
  6. Unable to complete because management failed to provide information → withdraw and inform management and TCWG of the reasons.
  7. Aware that compiled information (a) does not adequately refer to / describe the framework (b) needs amendments to avoid material misstatement or (c) is otherwise misleading → propose appropriate amendments to management.
  8. Management declines / does not permit amendments → withdraw and inform management and TCWG of reasons.
  9. Withdrawal not possible → determine professional and legal responsibilities applicable.
  10. Obtain acknowledgement from management / TCWG that they have taken responsibility for the final version.
  11. Communication (3.6): communicate with management / TCWG on a timely basis all matters of sufficient importance in practitioner's professional judgment.
Mnemonic — steps: U-C-D-R, then "I-W-A-W-L", then A = "Understand, Compile, Discuss, Read — Inform, Withdraw; Amend, Withdraw, Legal duty — Acknowledgement". Remember the ladder: ask → (can't finish) withdraw; propose amendment → (declined) withdraw → (can't withdraw) legal duty.

Practitioner does not verify revenue or any figure (case Q2 answer c) — but must read the information and flag errors (swapped opening balances → bring to management's knowledge, case Q1 answer b). Wrong order loses marks: propose amendments first, withdraw only if management declines. TYK 2 (₹100 lacs theft loss, claim repudiated, no appeal, shown in current assets): it is a loss; amendments needed for no material misstatement → propose amendment → if declined, withdraw and inform → if not possible, determine professional and legal responsibilities. Q8 (Kings & Co, inventory understated): "not an audit" is no defence — same ladder; partners' "certified figures" do not bypass it.

12Practitioner's report (SM 3.7)

Purpose: clearly communicate the nature of the engagement and the practitioner's role and responsibilities. It is not a vehicle to express an opinion or conclusion. Must be in writing with (13):

(a) Title · (b) Addressee(s) per terms · (c) statement that practitioner has compiled based on information provided by management · (d) management's / TCWG's responsibilities for the engagement and the information · (e) applicable framework (if special purpose, describe or refer to it in the information) · (f) identification of the information — title of each element, date or period · (g) practitioner's responsibilities incl. engagement per the SRS and compliance with relevant ethical requirements · (h) what a compilation entails · (i) explanations: (i) not assurance, so not required to verify accuracy or completeness; (ii) so no audit opinion or review conclusion that information is prepared per the framework · (j) if special purpose framework: paragraph on (i) purpose and intended users (or note reference) and (ii) attention that it may not be suitable for other purposes · (k) Date · (l) Signature · (m) Place.

Mnemonic — compilation report (13): "T-A-C | M-F-I | P-E-N | S | D-S-P" = Title · Addressee · Compiled-on-management's-info statement | Management's duties · Framework · Identify the information | Practitioner's duties & ethics · what it Entails · Not assurance (no verification, no opinion) | Special-purpose paragraph | Date · Signature · Place.
