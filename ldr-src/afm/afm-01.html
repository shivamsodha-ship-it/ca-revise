1Advanced role of the CFO incl. value creation (SM 1)

Traditional: shareholder wealth maximisation, financial health, financial controls; governance, compliance, ethics. Now (globalisation, information growth, pandemic) also strategic & operational decision-making. Post-pandemic areas:

  • (a) Risk management — oversee the whole risk-management framework
  • (b) Supply chain — financial viability of SCM for sustainable growth
  • (c) M&A and corporate restructuring — strategic; an error can collapse the business
  • (d) ESG financing — shift from traditional to sustainability financing

→ CFOs now lead value creation, on a sustainable basis over the long term.

2Strategic financial decision-making framework (SM 2)

  • Capital investment = springboard for wealth creation; capital is the limiting factor → strategic allocation of limited funds
  • 3 essentials: clear, realistic strategy · financial resources, controls & systems (finance) · right management team & processes → Strategy + Finance + Management = Fundamentals of Business
  • Strategy = long-term direction and scope to achieve competitive advantage through configuration of resources in a changing environment, fulfilling stakeholders' aspirations. Investors care about profit and its likelihood — risk-return trade-off
  • Strategic financial management = application of FM techniques to strategic decisions to achieve the decision-maker's objectives; combines backward-looking accounting with forward-looking FM; identifies strategies that maximise market value; allocates scarce capital; implements and monitors

Functions (SM 2.2) — 5

Continual search for best opportunities · selection of best profitable ones · optimal mix of funds · systems of internal control · analysis of results for future decisions. Strategy = long term (senior mgmt) · tactics = intermediate (middle) · operations = short term (line).

Key decisions — 4

Financing (equity–debt mix) · Investment (long-term projects, risk vs return) · Dividend (payout vs reinvestment) · Portfolio (judge each investment by its contribution to the aggregate performance of the corporation, not in isolation)

5Interface of financial policy & strategic management (SM 5) — TYK Q1

Starting point and end point of an organisation is money. No business can run or expand without an internally and / or externally mobilised financial base.

  1. Sources of finance & capital structure — the most important dimensions of a strategic plan. Ownership capital (equity, preference), borrowed (debentures), public deposits (short / medium term; may pay more than banks), overdraft, cash credit, bill discounting, bank loans, trade credit. D/E norms: 1 : 1 public sector, 2 : 1 private sector; higher for capital-intensive, high-cost priority-sector and backward-region projects
  2. Investment & fund allocation — policies for fixed and current assets. Proposals of 3 types: new product · more capacity for an existing product · cost reduction / efficiency. Compare within group by capital budgeting; project evaluation & selection are the key jobs; best allocation under resource constraints
  3. Dividend policy — payout vs retention; dividend = earnings that cannot be profitably used. Stable dividend → positive price impact; constant % of earnings → flexibility, signals lower risk; minimum DPS + extra in good years; weigh investment needs, shareholder preference, cash vs stock dividend

Financial policy can't be framed in isolation — investors constantly evaluate it; frame it during corporate planning, not later. Interdependence: sometimes strategy is the cause and financial policy the effect, sometimes the reverse.

6Balancing financial goals vs sustainable growth (SM 6) — TYK Q2

  • Forces managers to consider the financial consequences of sales increases; set growth goals consistent with operating & financial policies — conflict if they aren't
  • Right distribution of resources for current and future stakeholders: "save fuel" campaign = demarketing, a stable growth strategy of the oil industry under resource constraints. Variants: incremental growth, profit, pause strategies
  • Growth too fast or too slow both hurt — keep actual growth close to the sustainable growth ratio

Financially sustainable — 7 (CIVICUS)

More than one source of income · more than one way of generating income · regular strategic, action & financial planning · adequate financial systems · good public image · value clarity · financial autonomy

Sustainable organisation — 6 (CIVICUS)

Clear strategic direction · scan environment for opportunities · attract, manage, retain competent staff · adequate admin & financial infrastructure · demonstrate effectiveness & impact to leverage resources · community support & involvement

7Sustainable growth rate (SM 6.1)

SGR (Robert C. Higgins) = maximum growth in sales achievable given profitability, asset utilisation, desired payout and debt ratios — how fast a firm can grow without borrowing more (beyond it, external funds). SGR = ROE × (1 − dividend payout ratio). Variables: net margin, asset turnover, assets-to-equity, retention rate.

  • Assumes the firm wants to: (1) keep a target capital structure without issuing new equity (2) keep a target payout (3) grow sales as fast as the market allows. If willing to issue equity → no financial constraint on growth
  • Twin cornerstones: growth strategy and growth capability — neglect either and long-term growth fails
  • Sustainability: very weak — total stock of capital assets constant · weak — preserve critical resources · strong — preserve resources under primacy of ecosystem functioning
  • Useful when a borrower seeks more loans → too much debt: raise equity or slow growth to the sustainable level
  • Mature firms (actual < SGR): use surplus cash — higher dividends, buybacks, repay debt, hold liquid assets (each lowers SGR) — or acquire fast-growing companies
  • Growth from volume and inflation; inflationary asset growth must be financed like real growth → more external finance, higher historical-cost D/E → if creditors fix D/E, inflation lowers SGR
  • Mitsubishi Corporation: dropped fixed 3-year midterm plans for a ~2020 growth vision — "future pull" to double the business, clear portfolio strategy (proactive reshaping), growth with financial discipline

8External Funding Requirement (SM 6.2)

To grow, both current and non-current assets must rise; with constraints on D/E or current ratio, find how much external funding is needed and of what type. Analytical approach: project each balance-sheet item against sales under the conditions. Formula approach:

EFR = (TA ÷ S) × ΔS − (spontaneous payables & liabilities ÷ S) × ΔS − m × S1 × (1 − d)

m = net profit margin · S1 = revised sales · d = payout. Then split between short-term, long-term debt and equity using the given conditions.

SM Illustration — Sundry Ltd (₹ lakh)Working
TA 14,000; payables 2,000; sales 6,000 +20%; margin 4%; payout 50%Retained = 7,200 × 4% × 50% = 144; extra assets = (14,000 − 2,000) × 20% = 2,400 → EFR = 2,256
Sales : (ST loan + payables) = 4 : 3New = ¾ × 7,200 = 5,400; existing 2,400 + 3,000 = 5,400 → short-term nil
FA : LT loan = 1.57,200 ÷ 1.5 = 4,800 − 4,000 → long-term 800
Balance from equity2,256 − 0 − 800 = 1,456; check D/E = 4,800 ÷ (3,000 + 1,456 + 2,000 + 144) = 0.727 ≤ 1.5 ✓

Deduct only spontaneous liabilities (payables & provisions) — not loans. Retained earnings use revised sales. Always verify the stated D/E or ratio condition at the end.
