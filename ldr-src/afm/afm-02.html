2Categories of financial risk (SM 1.4)

  • Counter-party: non-honouring of obligations (goods not delivered after payment, loan / interest not repaid) — includes credit risk
  • Political: faced by overseas investors — (i) confiscation / destruction of property (ii) rationing of remittance (iii) restriction on currency conversion (iv) restriction on borrowings (v) invalidation of patents (vi) price control
  • Interest rate: rate changes alter assets & liabilities; most important for banks (earn the spread). Inherent in both fixed and floating rates — floating borrower suffers when rates rise; fixed borrower pays comparatively more when floating rates fall
  • Currency: cash flows change with exchange rates, adversely or favourably — ₹ depreciation helps an exporter (Infosys), hurts an importer (IOC)
  • Liquidity: inability to meet liabilities when due — inadequate cash or mismatch in timing; common in banks (maturity mismatch)

4Value at Risk (SM 3)

Measure of investment risk: under normal market conditions over a set period (say one day), how much an investment might lose — portfolio, capital investment or forex. Answers: (i) what is the worst case? (ii) what will be the loss? First applied 1922 at NYSE; entered finance in the 1990s; now the world's most widely used measure of financial risk.

Features (SM 3.1)

(i) Components: time period · confidence level (generally 95% / 99%) · loss in % or amount (ii) statistical tool based on standard deviation (iii) any time horizon — day, week, month (iv) probability of maximum loss, assuming normal distribution (v) risk control by setting maximum-loss limits (vi) Z score = number of σ from the mean; Z × σ = VaR

Application / significance (SM 3.2) — TYK Q1

(a) measure maximum possible loss on a portfolio or trading position (b) benchmark for performance measurement of operations / trading (c) fix limits for front-office dealers in treasury (d) help management decide trading strategies (e) tool for ALM, especially in banks

VaR = Z × σ (in ₹) × √t  ·  Z = 2.33 at 99%, 1.65 at 95% (one-tailed)  ·  σt days = σ1 day × √t

SM workingAnswer
SM 3.3: ₹2 cr in X Ltd, daily σ 2%, 252 trading days, 99%σ = ₹4 lakh → 1-day VaR = 4 × 2.33 = ₹9.32 lakh; 10-day = √10 × 9.32 = ₹29.47 lakh
TYK 1: ₹200 lakh each in XYZ & ABC, daily σ 1% each, r = 0.3, 10-day 99%Daily σ each ₹2 lakh; variance = 2² + 2² + 2 × 0.3 × 2 × 2 = 10.4 → σ = ₹3.22 lakh (or 0.80623% of ₹400 lakh); 10-day σ = 3.22 × √10 = ₹10.18 lakh; VaR = 2.33 × 10.18 = ₹23.72 lakh

Scale σ by √t, never by t. Use portfolio σ with the correlation term — two 1% shares with r = 0.3 give 0.806%, not 1%. 252 days is only needed to annualise.
