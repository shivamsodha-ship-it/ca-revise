2Inflation in capital budgeting (SM 1.1) — TYK Q3

  • Inflation raises revenues and costs; net revenues after adjusting for inflation = net revenues in current terms. Two distortions:
  • (1) Depreciation on historical cost: after-tax CF = (R − C)(1 − T) + DT — (R − C)(1 − T) moves with inflation, DT does not → inflation reduces the real rate of return. SM eg: ₹80,000, 4 yrs, revenue 60,000, costs 20,000, tax 50% → CF 30,000 p.a.; with 10% inflation nominal CF 32,000 → 39,282 but real CF 29,091 → 26,830 (falls)
  • (2) Cost of capital contains an inflation premium: RN = RR + P (SM's formula). If the rate has an inflation premium, reflect inflation in cash flows; no inflation → discount at the real rate

Illus 1: outlay 40,000, real CF 15,000 × 4 yrs, cost of capital 12% incl. 10% inflation premium. Discounting real CF at 12% is inconsistent. Either (i) nominal CF = 15,000 × 1.10t at 12% → NPV ₹17,369, or (ii) real rate = 1.12 ÷ 1.10 − 1 = 1.8% on real CF → ₹17,367 — same answer.

Different inflation for revenue and cost: NPV = Σ [{Rt Π(1 + ir) − Ct Π(1 + ic)}(1 − T) + DtT] ÷ (1 + k)t − I0. Illus 2: ₹8 lakh, revenue inflation 10/9/8/7%, cost 12/10/9/8% (compounded), tax 60%, dep 2 lakh (shield 1.2 lakh, not inflated), k 10% → NPV ₹1,06,744.

Inflate cumulatively: year-3 revenue = R × 1.10 × 1.09 × 1.08. The depreciation tax shield is never inflated.

3Technology change (SM 1.2)

Why analyse: alters production process · benefits (quality, delivery, flexibility) · may lower cost of capital · better cash inflows · may need extra capex · shorter product life cycle. How: (1) at decision time use sensitivity / scenario / simulation (2) after launch, revise estimates (3) if unviable, evaluate abandonment (4) adjust the discount rate. Review continuously.

4Government policies (SM 1.3)

  • Affect the firm directly or via suppliers, buyers, service providers; positive or negative; revise estimates, consider abandonment. Some change rarely (New Industrial Policy 1991), others within a year: fiscal policy (spending, taxation, borrowing) and monetary policy (OMO, reserve requirements, selective credit control, currency supply, repo; MPC meets at least 4 times a year)
  • Domestic: (a) interest-rate changes affect cost of debt / YTM — little effect on long-term-funded capex but big on working capital (overdraft); small changes ignored, ~100 bps calls for revision (b) fiscal policy changes tax and depreciation rates → revise cash flows
  • International: (a) bank rate and money supply move exchange rates (b) tax on foreign income / DTAA changes

5Risk in investment decisions (SM 2)

  • Decisions under (i) certainty (ii) risk — probabilities can be assigned (iii) uncertainty — they can't
  • Risk = variability of actual vs estimated returns; measured by σ and CV. Risk: distribution known; uncertainty: not known (terms used interchangeably)
  • Why adjust for risk: (1) opportunity cost — are returns proportionate to risk vs other options? (2) to know the real value of inflows — higher risk, higher premium and return

Ch 3 · Statistical & conventional techniquesTYK THEORY Q1

8Statistical techniques (SM 4.1)

ENCF = Σ NCFi × Pi · ENPV = Σ ENCFt ÷ (1 + k)t (incl. outflows) · σ = √Σ Pi(NCFi − ENCF)² · CV = σ ÷ expected return / cash flow

CV = risk per unit of expected return — compare projects of different size; lower CV preferred. SM eg: 3,00,000 × 0.3 + 2,00,000 × 0.6 + 1,20,000 × 0.1 = ₹2,22,000.

9Risk-adjusted discount rate (SM 4.2.1)

  • Investors demand more from risky projects: rate = compensation for delaying consumption + inflation (= risk-free) + risk premium (depends on perceived risk & risk aversion). RADR = Rf + risk premium
  • Illus 7: ₹100 lakh, CF 25/60/75/80/65 → at 7% NPV 144.34; at RADR 14% NPV 99.79
  • Advantages: (1) easy to understand (2) builds the premium into the discount rate. Limitations: (1) hard to find the premium / RADR (2) NPV yes, but σ of the project can't be computed

10Certainty equivalent (SM 4.2.2) — TYK Q1

CIMA: expressing risky future cash flows as the certain cash flow the decision-maker would consider equivalent — indifferent between the risky amount and the (lower) riskless amount. Lets the decision-maker build in his utility function; a set of riskless cash flows replaces the original.

  1. Step 1: αt = certain CF ÷ risky (expected) CF; multiply each CF by αt. Coin toss: win ₹10,000 or nothing; indifferent at ₹3,000 certain → CE ₹3,000, α = 0.3
  2. Step 2: discount at the risk-free rate — cost of capital would double-count risk
  3. Step 3: normal capital budgeting; for IRR compare with risk-free rate, not the required return

α between 0 and 1; α = 1 → certain or risk-neutral; industry usually risk-averse. NPV = Σ αt NCFt ÷ (1 + kf)t − I. Illus 8: ₹45 lakh; 10/15/20/25 lakh × 0.90/0.85/0.82/0.78 at 5% → ₹5,34,570.

Advantages

(1) simple to understand and apply (2) easily set for different risk levels in different years

Disadvantages

(1) no objective / mathematical way to estimate — subjective (2) reflects management's risk perception, ignores shareholders' → rarely used

RADR vs CE: CE is superior — it doesn't assume risk grows with time at a constant rate (each year's α fits its risk). But RADR is preferred in practice — easier to adjust a rate than specify a series of α.

11Sensitivity analysis (SM 4.3.1) — TYK Q2

  • CIMA: modelling and risk-assessment procedure in which changes are made to significant variables to find their effect on the planned outcome; special attention to variables of special significance
  • Change one variable at a time; the more sensitive the NPV (IRR), the more critical the variable. Steps: (1) find variables influencing NPV / IRR (2) establish mathematical relationships (3) analyse effect of change in each
  • SM Illus 9: 2.5% adverse change in each variable → selling price has the maximum effect (NPV −24.82%)
  • Advantages: (1) identifies critical issues (2) simple. Disadvantages: (1) assumes variables are independent (2) ignores probability of changes

12Scenario analysis (SM 4.3.2)

Extends sensitivity: brings in probabilities and changes several variables at once. Base (most likely) case → worst case (low sales & price, high variable cost) → best case; or mixed. Variability of outcomes across scenarios (eg boom / normal / recession) measures risk. More complex than sensitivity — all inputs change simultaneously.

13Simulation — Monte Carlo (SM 4.3.3) — TYK Q4

Exact replica of the actual situation via a model; infinite calculations for possible outcomes and probabilities; ties together sensitivities and probability distributions; named after casino gambling (from first nuclear-bomb work); gives a probability distribution of NPV, not a point estimate.

  1. Model the project — NPV vs parameters (set by decision-maker, constant across runs) and exogenous variables (stochastic, outside control)
  2. Specify parameter values and probability distributions of exogenous variables
  3. Select a value at random from each exogenous distribution
  4. Compute NPV for those values
  5. Repeat 3 & 4 many times
  6. Plot the NPV distribution; compute mean and σ

Advantages — "strength lies in variability"

(1) predict bad market situations beforehand (2) handles many exogenous variables with any distribution, complex inter-relationships beyond analytical methods, forces explicit consideration of inter-dependencies

Shortcomings (4)

(1) hard to model and specify distributions (2) inherently imprecise — misleading when a tail matters (3) built by experts, decision-maker may not understand / use it (4) NPV at risk-free rate (to avoid pre-judging risk) → hard to interpret

14Decision tree (SM 4.3.4)

  • Graphic display of the link between a present decision and future events, decisions and consequences — for sequential decisions. Decision (manager controls; acts rationally) vs event (no control; assign probabilities)
  • Steps: (1) define investment (launch local / national / international; extend factory vs outsource; dig for oil) (2) identify alternatives (3) draw the tree (4) evaluate — from the last decision (right) backwards; at each decision point keep highest NPV, truncate others; compute expected NPV at chance points
  • Rules: decision node = rectangle; chance node = circle; alternatives as straight lines; drawn left to right; number nodes; add values & probabilities

15Replacement decisions (SM 5)

  • Replace existing machine (newer version or a different machine with the same function), mostly to maintain existing levels of operation. Method (app-written): incremental NPV — cost of new less sale of old, change in operating cash flows and depreciation tax shield, terminal values
  • Optimum replacement cycle (continuous replacement): NPVs of different cycle lengths aren't comparable → EAC = PV of costs ÷ PVAF for the cycle; choose the lowest EAC. SM taxi: EAC 1 yr 3,40,044 · 2 yr 3,15,331 · 3 yr 3,17,639 → replace every 2 years. ICAI MTP machine: 4-year cycle lowest at ₹58,656

16Adjusted Present Value (SM 6)

APV = Base-case NPV + PV of tax benefit on interest. Base case at the unlevered cost of equity (all-equity firm; unlevered Ke from Ch 13). Separates investment and financing decisions; credits the project for the debt used to finance it.
