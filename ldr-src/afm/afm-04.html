1Fundamental analysis (SM 1)

  • Share price depends on expected future dividends; PV of dividends = intrinsic value. Price below intrinsic → buy; above → sell
  • Constant dividend: P0 = D ÷ k. Constant growth (g < k): P0 = D1 ÷ (k − g) = D0(1 + g) ÷ (k − g) — Gordon's dividend growth model (Myron J. Gordon); invalid if g ≥ k (infinite price) — not restrictive, long-term g unlikely to exceed k. g = 0 → back to D ÷ k
  • Link to P/E (SM 1.1): with payout fraction b, D1 = bE1 → P0 = bE1 ÷ (k − g); so P/E = b ÷ (k − g). Price / P/E above fundamental → overpriced → sell; below → buy
  • Three concentric circles: economy → industry → company analysis

Symbol trap: in SM Ch 4, b = payout ratio. In SM Ch 5 (Gordon earnings model) and Ch 1 (SGR), b = retention. Read the definition given in the question.

2Economic analysis (SM 1.2)

Past / present / expected performance of the economy and sectors; income & consumption trends drive profits, dividends, prices.

Factors (4)

(a) growth of national income — nominal GDP vs real growth (b) growth of industrial sector (by demand) (c) inflation — WPI / CPI; consumer industries fine-tune pricing (d) monsoon — forward & backward linkages

Techniques (3) — TYK Q4

(a) Anticipatory surveys — expert opinion on construction, capex, inventory, consumer spending; drawbacks: intentions may not materialise; not forecasts per se (b) Barometer / indicator approach (c) Economic model building (GNP model / sectoral analysis)

  • Indicators: leading (turn before the economy) · roughly coincidental (turn with it) · lagging (turn after it). Show direction, not magnitude; may conflict → diffusion / composite index (hard to compute; doesn't remove irregular movements). Money supply ↑ → inflation; stocks a hedge → prices rise
  • GNP model steps: (i) hypothesise total demand (GNP) from political stability, inflation, economic levels (ii) forecast components — consumption, gross private domestic investment, government purchases, net exports (iii) add them (iv) compare with an independent agency's forecast and test consistency

5Technical analysis (SM 2.1–2.3)

  • Studies price charts assuming trends repeat (investor psychology follows patterns); studies investor and price behaviour. Asks: (i) is there a trend? (ii) will it reverse? Methods: visual (charts) and statistical
  • Assumptions (4): (i) value depends on supply & demand (ii) S & D governed by rational and irrational factors (iii) prices move in trends lasting a substantial time (iv) relies on charts, not financial statements
  • Principles (3): (a) market discounts everything (b) price moves in trends (c) history repeats itself

6Dow theory (SM 2.4.1) — TYK Q2

Charles Dow (founder of Dow Jones), late 19th century; measures relative strength of the market; a business barometer. Based on DJIA and DJTA. Three simultaneous movements: primary (main bull / bear trend, 1 year to 36 months or longer) · secondary (opposite direction, 2 weeks to a month or more) · daily fluctuations (not interpreted, but studied). Tells where the market is going, not how far. Successively higher highs & higher lows → bull; lower highs & lows → bear.

Three moves up: (1) accumulation by far-sighted investors (2) first reports of good earnings (3) widespread reports of well-being + rampant speculation. Then far-sighted investors sell → first move down; non-sustainability confirmed → second; distress selling → third.

7Elliot wave theory (SM 2.4.2) — TYK Q3

Ralph Elliot, 1934, inspired by Dow theory and nature; 75 years of price data. Wave = price movement from one change in direction to the next in the same direction, from buying / selling impulses. Impulsive (basic) waves: 3 or 5 waves in the main direction (bull or bear). Corrective (reaction) waves: 3 waves against it. Waves 1, 3, 5 directional; 2 & 4 corrective. Complete cycle = 5-wave impulse + 3-wave correction (a, b, c) = 8 waves, bullish and bearish phases; then a fresh cycle.

8Random walk theory (SM 2.4.3)

Prices are statistical ups and downs, unpredictable; no link between present and future prices; successive changes independent. M. G. Kendall (SM: "Kendell") — like a roulette wheel; "drunk walking in a blind lane". (a) prices can't be predicted (b) trends are a statistical expression of past data (c) no connection between successive peaks and troughs.

9Charts (SM 2.5)

Line (closing prices joined) · Bar (vertical high-low bar; ticks for open & close) · Japanese candlestick (white = close > open = bullish; black = close < open = bearish; Doji = open ≈ close) · Point & figure (detects reversals; choose box size — smaller = more sensitive — and reversal criterion; X rising, O falling).

10Market indicators (SM 2.6)

  • Breadth index = net advances or declines ÷ issues traded; supports Dow averages = technical strength; contradicts = weakness (reversal)
  • Volume: rising price + rising volume → buy (unsatisfied demand); falling + rising volume → bear. SM also: rising market with decreasing volume indicates a bull market; falling with dwindling volume a bear market. Use with Dow theory
  • Confidence index = high-grade bond yields ÷ low-grade bond yields; rising precedes a rising market; usually (not always) a leading indicator
  • Relative strength: some securities rise faster in bull / fall slower in bear and keep that strength — measured by historical returns or ratio to industry / market
  • Odd-lot theory: contrary-opinion — average person usually wrong; predicts tops in bull markets and reversals

11Support, resistance & patterns (SM 2.7–2.8)

Peak after a fall = resistance (selling pressure); trough after a rebound = support (buying pressure); breach = new levels. SM eg: A ₹100 / ₹125; B ₹140 / ₹160.

13Evaluation of technical analysis (SM 2.10) & FA vs TA (SM 3)

For (3)

(a) crowd psychology makes trends persist; spotted early (b) demand-supply shifts are gradual (c) information is assimilated over time, so price keeps moving one way

Against (4)

(a) no convincing explanation of tools (b) evidence for random walk (c) by the time a trend is signalled it has happened (d) self-defeating — more users, less value

Efficient market → TA may not work; real markets are imperfect → useful with fundamental analysis. FA vs TA: method — macro (GDP, inflation, rates) + micro (sales, profitability, solvency) vs historical market data (price, volume, open interest) · rule — "prices discount everything" vs "price captures everything" · use — long-term vs short-term investing.

14Efficient market theory (SM 4) — TYK Q1

  • Eugene Fama (Chicago), 1960s: all price-sensitive information is fully reflected in prices → no one can consistently beat the market; higher return only by taking more risk
  • Why random: information free & instant to all · keen competition makes prices reflect intrinsic values · prices change only on new, unpredictable information
  • Misconceptions (SM 4.2): (1) EMH doesn't mean prices always equal fair value — the future is uncertain, surprises keep prices fluctuating (2) managers' failure to outperform doesn't mean incompetence — markets are efficient because managers compete well (3) random movement ≠ irrational market — rational, competitive investors make price changes random
  • Levels: weak — past prices & volumes · semi-strong — + all public information · strong — all information, public and private
  • Weak-form tests: serial correlation (none found, short-term) · run test (runs vs a random series — supports random walk) · filter rule (buy after N% rise, sell after N% fall — doesn't beat buy-and-hold after commissions)
  • Semi-strong: Fama, Fisher, Jensen & Roll — 940 NYSE splits 1957–59; Ball & Brown (SM: "Boll") — earnings announcements. Anomalies: slow adjustment to quarterly earnings surprises · small firms beat large · low P/E beats high P/E · Monday returns lower
  • Strong: insiders and exchange specialists earn superior returns; mutual fund managers do not (Burton Malkiel, A Random Walk Down Wall Street)
  • Challenges (4): (a) information inadequacy / misinformation (b) limited processing capacity (Herbert Simon; David Dreman) (c) irrational behaviour (Keynes; L. C. Gupta — "blind man firing a gun") (d) monopolistic influence of big institutions

Equity research (SM 5): buy / sell / hold calls, also used in M&A swap ratios. Tools: Bloomberg Terminal (1981), Benzinga Pro, Refinitiv Eikon, MarketXLS, Stockopedia, Koyfin, Finbox.io, GuruFocus, Business Quant, YCharts.
