Ch 5 — Security Valuation

SM order: return concepts → ERP → required return → discount-rate selection → equity (dividend / earnings / cash-flow models, EV, rights) → preference → bonds → money market → valuers → precautions. TYK theory Qs: why duration < maturity (8.8) · zero coupon bonds (8.15).

1Return concepts (SM 2)

  • Required rate of return — minimum return the investor expects over a period; also called opportunity cost / cost of capital (highest expected return forgone elsewhere at similar risk). Often used interchangeably with expected return.
  • Discount rate — rate used to find PV of future cash flows = risk-free rate + risk premium. Each cash-flow stream can carry a different rate (inflation, maturity, default probability → term structure: upward sloping = longer maturity, more inflation / liquidity / default risk).
  • IRR — rate equating PV of future cash flows to market price; average annual return assuming reinvestment at the IRR. SM eg: invest 20,000 → 3,000 (yr 1) + 23,000 (yr 2) → IRR 15%; if the 3,000 is reinvested at only 10% → (26,300/20,000)½ − 1 = 14.67%.

2Equity risk premium vs market risk premium (SM 3.2) ⚠

CAPM: Rx = Rf + βx(Rm − Rf)

Market Risk Premium = Rm − Rf  ·  Equity Risk Premium = Rx − Rf = βx(Rm − Rf)

If x = market (β = 1) → ERP = market premium.

Earlier sheet wrongly said "ERP = Rm − Rf". Per SM, that is the market risk premium; ERP of a stock is β × MRP.

3Required return on equity (SM 4)

Return on share A = Rf + β × Market Risk Premium. CAPM's insight: investors judge risk by the asset's contribution to systematic risk (not diversifiable); gives a relatively objective, widely used estimate. SM eg: 5% + 1.5 × 4.5% = 11.75%.

β from covariance (Ch 6): β = Covim ÷ σm². PYQ May 2024: 30 ÷ 24 = 1.25 → ke = 12 + 1.25 × 4 = 17%.

4Discount rate selection (SM 5)

  • Cash flows to equity → required return on equity; cash flows to all stakeholders → cost of capital (WACC)
  • Nominal CF = not adjusted for inflation; real CF = adjusted. Nominal CF ↔ nominal rate; real CF ↔ real rate. Low inflation / short term → nearly same; high inflation → nominal > real.
  • Equity valuation uses only nominal CF and nominal rate — because corporate tax is stated in nominal terms, so nominal CF reflects taxes accurately. Equity CF → nominal ke; all-provider CF → nominal after-tax WACC.

6Earnings-based models (SM 6.2)

Investors may forgo cash dividend for higher earnings on retained profits → value on earnings.

(a) Gordon's model — assumes (i) return on retained earnings constant, (ii) retention ratio constant: P = EPS1(1 − b) ÷ (Ke − br) (r = ROE, b = retention)

(b) Walter's approach: P = [D + (E − D) × r/Ke] ÷ Ke

(c) P/E (multiplier) approach: Value = EPS × P/E; P/E estimated from similar company / industry after adjusting for company-specific features

SM TYK 1: BVPS 137.8, ROE 15%, retention 60%, Ke 18% → EPS 20.67, D 8.27, g = 0.6 × 0.15 = 9% → Gordon ₹91.89; Walter = [8.27 + (12.40) × 0.15/0.18] ÷ 0.18 = ₹103.35.

9Enterprise value (SM 6.5)

EV = Market cap + LT debt + Minority interest − Cash & equivalents (also minus equity investments in affiliates / other companies and LT investments)

TypeMeaning
Total EVAll business activities: mkt cap + interest-bearing debt + minority interest − cash
Operating EVTotal EV − market value of non-operating assets (investments, shares in associates)
Core EVOperating EV − value of non-core assets

EV = true economic value; considers debt + equity, least affected by capital structure → EV multiples more reliable than equity multiples. EV/Sales — firms with negative CF / earnings (cyclical, tech); sales least manipulable. EV/EBITDA — capital-intensive firms (heavy D&A), acquisitions; unaffected by depreciation policy & capital structure; inverse = cash return on total investment.

12Bond basics, value, theorems (SM 8.1–8.7)

  • Basics: par value · coupon rate & frequency (monthly / quarterly / half-yearly / annual) · maturity period · redemption (bullet — one-shot at par or premium)
  • V = Σ I ÷ (1 + kd)t + F ÷ (1 + kd)n = I(PVIFA) + F(PVIF); kd = YTM / required return on similar bonds
  • Semi-annual: V = I/2 × PVIFA(kd/2, 2n) + F × PVIF(kd/2, 2n)
  • YTM = discount rate at which PV of the bond's future cash flows = market price

13Duration (SM 8.8)

Duration = weighted average time within which investor gets back promised principal and promised YTM; measures how quickly a bond repays its true cost; also a measure of interest-rate risk (higher duration → higher risk).

Coupon bond's duration is always less than its maturity (TYK theory Q1).

Factors: (i) time to maturity — shorter → lower duration; (ii) coupon — higher → lower; (iii) YTM — higher → lower

Macaulay = Σ t × PV(CFt) ÷ price (years to recover true cost) · Modified = Macaulay ÷ (1 + YTM/n), n = compounding periods per year

Same bond (app-computed): D = 4.1355 yrs; MD = 3.6924 → +1% yield ≈ −3.69% price.

13Immunization (SM 8.9) — contd.

  • Immunization: interest-rate risk = price risk + reinvestment risk, which move in opposite directions. Choose bonds so they offset → portfolio value at end of holding period insensitive to rate changes. Duration = holding period → immunized.
  • SM TYK 27: need ₹1 lakh in 2 yrs; Bond X duration 1, Bond Y 3.563 → 2 = x + (1 − x)3.563 → 61% X, 39% Y (≈ 52 X, 34 Y bonds)

14Yield curve / term structure (SM 8.10–8.11)

Spot rate = rate for a zero-coupon bond of that maturity (time value of a single future payment). Yield curve (term structure) = how YTM relates to term to maturity, for bonds similar in all respects except maturity. Four patterns (Saransh): A. Upward sloping · B. Downward sloping · C. Flat · D. Humped. Forward rates (rates applicable in future) are derived from spot rates.

TheorySays (SM)
ExpectationLong-term rates are unbiased estimators of future short-term rates → used to forecast them
Liquidity preferenceInvestors risk-averse; long bonds carry more interest-rate risk and sacrifice liquidity → premium; normal curve is positively sloped
Preferred habitat (market segmentation)Each investor has a preferred maturity; rates set by demand/supply in each segment; mismatch → higher rate to pull players out of habitat; curve can be upward, falling or flat (Saransh: explains almost any shape)

15Convexity adjustment (SM 8.12)

Duration assumes a linear price–yield link → good only for small Δy. Adjustment = C* × (Δy)² × 100,   C* = (V+ + V− − 2V0) ÷ [2V0(Δy)²]

16Convertibles, warrants, ZCB (SM 8.13–8.15)

  • Convertible debenture → equity after a period. Shares per debenture = conversion ratio; price paid per share = conversion price; conversion value = share price × shares per debenture
  • Warrant = right to subscribe equity during a period at a stated price; issued to sweeten a debenture issue. Value = (MP − E) × n
  • Warrant vs convertible: (i) warrant's conversion option is detachable → traded separately; (ii) warrant exercised for cash, convertible needs no cash
  • Zero coupon bond (TYK theory Q2): no coupon; issued at discount, one lump sum (face value = investment + accrued interest) at maturity; usually long maturity → long-range planning; issued by banks, government, companies — corporate ZCBs riskier (issuer strength, longer maturity) but higher return

MTP May 2025 extra terms (ICAI answer, not SM text): conversion parity = bond price ÷ ratio (5,300 ÷ 20 = 265); conversion premium; straight value (bond floor); downside risk; premium payback.

Ch 5 · Refunding, money market, precautionsFORMULA + THEORY

17Refunding of bonds (SM 8.16)

  • Refund before maturity when rates fall — issue new lower-coupon bonds; hence call feature, which costs a call premium (repay above face value)
  • A strategic decision evaluated by capital budgeting — generally NPV: refund if PV of inflows (net cash saving) > PV of outflows (call premium, interest during transition, etc.)
  • New bonds usually issued before old ones are refunded → interest on both during the overlap

Exam method (app-written, not SM text): outlay = call premium (net of tax) + new issue cost + overlap interest (net) − tax saving on unamortised old issue cost written off; savings = interest saved (1 − t) ± issue-cost amortisation shield; discount at after-tax new kd. SM TYK 25 has this set-up (₹9 m unamortised old cost, 4% call premium, 30% tax).
