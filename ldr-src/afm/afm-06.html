1Objectives & phases (SM)

Objectives (7)

Security / safety of principal · stability of income · capital growth · marketability · liquidity · diversification · favourable tax status

Phases (5)

Security analysis (risk-return of individual securities & correlation) → portfolio analysis (combine into feasible portfolios) → selection (efficient → optimal) → revision (monitor so it stays optimal) → evaluation (return & risk over the period)

Efficient portfolio: highest return among feasible portfolios with same / lower risk, or lowest risk with same / higher return.

2Theories (SM)

  • Traditional approach: concerned with the investor — study of age, health, responsibilities, needs for income / capital / liquidity, attitude to risk, tax status; objectives; investment strategy (fixed interest vs equity, high payout vs growth, tax, transaction cost vs switching gains, liquidity); diversification; selection of individual investments (intrinsic value)
  • Modern approach (Markowitz): risk of an individual asset hardly matters — what matters is its contribution to overall risk; diversify with low / negatively correlated securities; higher return needs higher risk

3Types of risk (SM)

Systematic — external, macro, uncontrollableUnsystematic — internal, micro, controllable
TypesInterest rate (prices move inversely to rates) · Purchasing power / inflation (worst for bonds & fixed income) · Market riskBusiness risk (variability of operating profit) · Financial risk (capital structure / leverage, D/E)
MeasureBeta — by correlation method (β = rimσi/σm) or regression method (Y = α + βX)Residual variance

Diversion of risk: as number of securities ↑ total risk ↓; ultimately only systematic risk remains.

4Return & risk formulas

E(R) = Σ piXi · σ² = Σ pi(Xi − X̄)² · CovAB = rABσAσB · rAB = CovAB ÷ σAσB

Two securities: σp² = x1²σ1² + x2²σ2² + 2x1x2r12σ1σ2 · n securities: σp² = ΣΣ xixjσij (variance-covariance matrix)

5Markowitz model (SM)

  • Assumptions (SM 5.1): (i) return adequately summarises outcome · (ii) investors visualise a probability distribution of returns · (iii) risk estimates proportional to variance · (iv) decisions on two criteria — expected return & variance · (v) rational & risk-averse (prefer less risk for equal return) · (vi) yield in different scenarios used as return measure
  • Efficient frontier (Mean-Variance Dominance): a portfolio is not efficient if another has (a) higher return & lower σ, (b) higher return & same σ, or (c) same return & lower σ. Investor's choice on the frontier depends on risk-return preference

6Capital market theory — CML

  • Tangent from Rf to market portfolio B → CML (Rf B Z). Rf–B = lending portfolios (part in risk-free); B–Z = borrowing portfolios
  • Slope = (RM − Rf) ÷ σM = reward per unit of risk · E(R) = Rf + [(RM − Rf) ÷ σM] × σP
  • CML uses total risk σ (efficient portfolios); SML (CAPM) uses β (all securities)

7Single index (Sharpe) model

Co-movement between stocks is due to the market index: Ri = αi + βiRm + ∈i

σi² = βi²σm² + σ∈i² · systematic risk = βi² × variance of index · unsystematic = total − systematic · Covij = βiβjσm²

Portfolio: σp² = (Σxiβi)²σm² + Σxi²σ∈i² · Rp = Σxi(αi + βiRm) · αp & βp = weighted averages

App-computed: β 1.2, σm 15%, σ 25% → systematic 0.0324, unsystematic 0.0301. MTP May 2026 (reverse): σp 25.03 → variance 626.50 = 1.73X² + 67 → σm = 17.98.

Unsystematic portfolio risk uses weights squared (Σx²σ∈²). Keep variance units consistent (15% → 225 or 0.0225).

8APT (SM)

  • Alternative to CAPM; uses risk premia of several macro factors: E(Ri) = Rf + λ1β1 + λ2β2 + … + λnβn
  • No surprises → actual = expected. With unanticipated changes: E(R) = Rf + β1(EV1 − AV1) + … (surprise factor = expected − actual value of factor)

App-computed: Rf 6%, premia 4% & 2%, sensitivities 1.2 & 0.8 → 12.4%. May 2026: target sensitivities via long / short amounts — two simultaneous equations.

9Steps (SM)

  1. Find "excess return to beta" (Ri − Rf) ÷ βi — i.e. Treynor's ratio — for each stock
  2. Rank highest to lowest
  3. Compute Ci in ranked order: Ci = σm² Σ[(Ri − Rf)βi/σei²] ÷ [1 + σm² Σ(βi²/σei²)]
  4. C* = highest Ci; select stocks whose excess-return-to-beta is above C*, reject the rest
  5. Weights: Zi = (βi/σei²) × [(Ri − Rf)/βi − C*]; Xi = Zi ÷ ΣZi

10CAPM (SM)

  • Rj = Rf + β(Rm − Rf); risk premium of a stock = β × market risk premium. Graph = SML (required return vs β); market β = 1
  • SM: only one risk-free rate; if two are given, aggressive = higher, conservative = lower, moderate = average (Illus. 5)
  • Decisions (SM 8.2): CAPM required < actual / expected return → buy (under-valued) · required > actual → sell (over-valued) · equal → hold. Price basis: actual price < CAPM price → under-valued
  • Advantages: (i) risk-adjusted return — usable as risk-adjusted discount rate; (ii) cost of equity for a no-dividend company
  • Limitations: (a) reliability of beta; (b) ignores other (unsystematic) risks; (c) information available — Rf and market return hard to estimate
  • Back-solving (Jan 2026 MCQ): two portfolios' returns & betas → solve Rf and Rm

App-computed: 6 + 1.3 × (14 − 6) = 16.4%. SM Illus. 2: β 1.40, Rm 14, Rf 10 → 15.6%; premium +2.5 points → 19.1%.

13Portfolio strategy (SM)

Ch 6 · Fixed income & alternative investmentsTHEORY BANK

17Fixed income portfolio (SM)

  • Process (5): set objective → investment policy guidelines → strategy (active / passive) → select securities → evaluate vs benchmark. Return measures: arithmetic average, time-weighted, rupee-weighted, annualised
  • Passive: buy & hold (may set duration = benchmark) · indexation (replicate bond index) · immunization (hybrid; popular with pension funds) · matching cash flows (zero-coupon bonds to meet promised payments)
  • Active: (1) forecasting returns & interest rates — bullet (all in one maturity), barbell (equal short & long), ladder (equal across maturities) · (2) bond swaps — pure yield pickup, substitution, inter-market (international) spread, tax swap · (3) interest rate swap

18Alternative investments (SM)

  • Features (6): high fees · limited historical data · illiquidity · less transparency · extensive research required · leveraged buying. Types: real estate, gold, private equity, REITs, hedge funds, ETFs, MFs, commodities, distressed securities
  • Real estate — valuation complex (inefficient market, illiquidity, only approximate comparison, high transaction cost…). 4 approaches: sales comparison (like P/E), income (perpetual net income ÷ required return), cost (replacement cost + land value, adjusted for location), discounted after-tax cash flow (NPV-like)
  • Gold forms: jewellery (making charges) · gold bars / coins (storage cost) · SGBs — government securities in grams of gold, issued by RBI on behalf of GoI, paid & redeemed in cash, quantity of gold protected (market price at redemption), no storage risk / cost, periodical interest, held with RBI or in demat · Gold ETFs (price based on gold; full transparency; low expenses) · E-gold (NSEL, 2010; 1 unit = 1 gram in demat)
  • Distressed securities: securities of firms in / near bankruptcy, bought cheap to revive; arbitrage by long debt + short equity. Risks: liquidity, event, market, human (judge's decision)

SGB scheme specifics used in May 2026 MCQ (2.5% p.a. interest, 8-year tenor, redemption gain exempt for individuals) are not stated in the SM — use only the data given in the question.
