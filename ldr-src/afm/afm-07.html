Ch 7 — Securitization

Pure theory — every list matters. SM TYK theory Qs: PTS vs PTC (para 7) · problems in securitisation, especially in India (para 6).

1Why & what (SM 1–2)

  • Firms with large receivables still face a liquidity crunch; borrowing changes D/E and adds financial risk → securitisation
  • Creation of a pool of assets from illiquid financial assets (receivables, loans) that are marketable — repackaging / rebundling illiquid assets into marketable securities (auto loans, credit-card receivables, residential mortgages, future receivables)
  • RBI: transactions where credit risks in assets are redistributed by repackaging them into tradable securities

Features (6)

(i) Creation of financial instruments backed by collateral (ii) Bundling (pooling) & unbundling (fixed-denomination instruments) (iii) Risk-management tool — on non-recourse basis, default risk shifts from originator to investor (iv) Structured finance — tailored to investors' risk-return profile (v) Tranching — pool split by risk & return (vi) Homogeneity — securities in a tranche are homogeneous, small denominations for small investors

2Benefits (SM 3)

Originator (4)

(i) Off-balance-sheet financing → frees capital, better liquidity, expansion (ii) specialisation in core business — servicing moves to SPV; non-recourse shifts default burden (iii) better financial ratios — banks / FIs manage capital-to-risk-weighted-asset ratio (iv) lower borrowing cost — rated papers (credit enhancement) issued at lower rates → originator earns a spread

Investor (3)

(1) Diversification — backed by different asset types (2) Regulatory requirement — eg buying micro-industry-backed assets meets RBI Priority Sector Lending (3) Protection against default — recourse to originator; insurance

4Mechanism (SM 5) — 7 steps

  1. Create pool — segregate similar assets (for MBS: interest rate, risk, maturity, concentration)
  2. Transfer to SPV
  3. Sale of securitised papers — SPV designs instruments by interest, risk, tenure, pool (pass-through / pay-through)
  4. Administration — sub-contracted back to the originator, who collects and passes to SPV for a fee (servicer / conduit)
  5. Recourse to originator — unless specified otherwise, defaulted receivables go back to the originator
  6. Repayment — SPV pays investors interest & principal from the pool
  7. Credit rating of instruments before sale

Stripped securities: cash flows split into Interest Only (IO) and Principal Only (PO); highly volatile → less preferred. Yields rise → borrowers postpone repayment of cheaper loans → PO price falls; IO value rises (more interest). Rates fall → borrowers prepay → IO falls. Prices driven mainly by investor perception.

Ch 7 · Pricing, risks, tokenizationTHEORY

7Pricing (SM 8)

  • Originator's angle: price at a rate whose outflow can be amortised over time by investing the amount raised
  • Investor's angle: discount best estimate of expected cash flows at the YTM of a comparable security (credit quality, average life); or use the yield curve + spread for the credit quality

8Risks (SM 9)

  • Credit / counter-party — prime risk; bankruptcy and non-performance of the servicer
  • Legal — recent concept in India; no conclusive precedent or explicit statute → disputes over ownership create pay-out uncertainty
  • Market: (a) macro-economic — eg fall in industrial production hits commercial-vehicle operators → CV loan repayments; fall in CV prices → wilful default and repossession (b) prepayment — rates fall → retail prepayments → reinvestment risk (c) interest rate — floating pool vs fixed pay-outs (or vice versa) → shortfall even at 100% collection; hedge with interest rate swaps

9Blockchain & tokenization (SM 10)

  • Blockchain / DLT: shared, peer-to-peer, decentralised, open ledger with no trusted third party; append-only — entries permanent, transactions irreversible (changes recorded as new transactions); not controlled by any bank, corporation or government. Analogy: a shared Google Doc
  • Flow: transaction initiated → broadcast → network validates using cryptography → represented as a block → block added to chain → complete

Applications (5)

(a) Financial services — automated trade lifecycle / transaction log of assets (b) Healthcare — secure, private, interoperable data (c) Government — land & vehicle registration, e-voting (d) Travel — payments, passports / IDs, reservations, insurance, loyalty (e) Economic forecasts — decentralised prediction markets, voting, trading

Risks (4)

(i) members' different risk appetites; who manages risk / accountability with no one in charge (ii) reliability depends on technology — tampered consensus mechanism makes data unreliable (iii) no central authority → hard to maintain process controls, understand general IT controls (iv) information overload; hard to find competent monitors

Tokenization = converting tangible and intangible assets into blockchain tokens (real estate, artwork). Similar to securitisation in: (i) liquidity for illiquid assets (ii) diversification (iii) tradable → wealth (iv) new fee opportunities for institutions.

10Securitisation in India (SM 11)

Pioneered by Citi Bank (auto loans); then sales-tax deferrals, aircraft receivables. SARFAESI Act 2002 — tackle NPAs without court. Now key funding for MFIs and NBFCs; CMBS emerging; dominated by few players (ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank, NHB). CRISIL: volumes may return near pre-pandemic high of ₹1.9 trillion (FY19 & FY20). SEBI allows FPIs into securitised debt of unlisted companies up to a limit.
