Basis = Spot − Futures. Normal (contango) market: futures > spot (full cost of carry) → basis negative. Backwardation / inverted: spot > futures (factors other than carry) → basis positive. Basis → 0 at expiry = convergence.

2Futures pricing — cost of carry

3Margins & mark-to-market

  • Initial margin = µ + 3σ of daily changes in contract value (SM illustration: µ ₹10,000 + 3 × ₹2,000 = ₹16,000; maintenance 75% = ₹12,000)
  • Maintenance margin (usually lower); balance falls below it → margin call to restore to initial margin
  • Daily MTM: long gains when price rises; losses debited to margin account. Cumulative MTM = (final − entry price) × lot

4Beta hedge — formulas

No. of contracts to fully hedge = (Portfolio value × βp) ÷ (Futures price × lot size)

Change beta from β1 to β2: contracts = (β2 − β1) × Portfolio value ÷ (Futures price × lot) — negative = sell, positive = buy

Long stock + short stock: hedge each leg separately (sell futures for long leg, buy for short leg) or net the beta-weighted values

ExampleWorking
Portfolio ₹1 cr, β 1.2; Nifty futures 22,000; lot 751,00,00,000 × 1.2 ÷ (22,000 × 75) = 7.27 → sell 7 contracts
Reduce β 1.2 → 0.6 (same data)(0.6 − 1.2) × 1 cr ÷ 16,50,000 = −3.64 → sell 4
RTP Jan 2026: portfolio 9,94,450, 5 Nifty contracts sold at 8,767.07 × 25 for full hedgeImplied β = 5 × 8,767.07 × 25 ÷ 9,94,450 = 1.102; 1.102 = 0.9 × 1.10 + 0.1 × β10 → β10 = 1.12

Use the futures price (not spot index) in the denominator. Lot size is whatever the question gives — don't assume. Profit on hedge = (sell price − buy-back price) × lot × contracts; overall P/L = cash position change + futures P/L.

10Black-Scholes (European, non-dividend)

C = S·N(d1) − K·e−rt·N(d2)

d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r + σ²/2)t] ÷ σ√t  ·  d2 = d1 − σ√t

P = C − S + K·e−rt (put-call parity) or P = K·e−rtN(−d2) − S·N(−d1)

N(d1) = hedge ratio / delta · N(d2) = probability call finishes ITM · N(−d) = 1 − N(d) · interpolate N() from the table

May 2026 Q6(b): S 120, K 115, 50 days, r 8%, σ 30%, d1 0.5375, e−rt 0.9891 → N(d1) 0.7045 (hedge ratio ≈ 70%); d2 = 0.5375 − 0.30√(50/365) = 0.4264; N(d2) 0.6651 (≈ 67%); C = 84.54 − 75.65 = ₹8.89; P = −120 + 8.89 + 113.75 = ₹2.63 (ICAI). Verified by code: C 8.89, P 2.64 (rounding).

6Index futures (SM 5.2)

  • Based on level of an index (Nifty 50, Sensex); used to speculate on the market or hedge a portfolio against decline
  • Not based on tangible goods → all settlements in cash; traded in number of contracts; value = index × multiplier (SM: 1 contract = 50 units × Nifty)
  • Margin is continuous — computed every business day; position squared off at closing price on expiry (SM: last Thursday of the month)
  • Purposes of futures trading (SM): speculation and hedging. Index futures hedge only systematic risk
  • SM TYK theory: reasons index futures became more popular than stock futures; short note on marking to market

16Credit derivatives (SM 8)

  • CDOs — backed by pool of bonds, ABS, REITs, other CDOs (securitisation is backed by loans / receivables); covers CBOs & CLOs. Types: Cash-flow CDO (backed by cash-market debt; relies on collateral's cash to pay SPV securities) · Synthetic CDO (credit risk transferred without transferring assets; unfunded (CDS only) / fully funded (CLN) / partially funded) · Arbitrage CDO (issuer captures spread between collateral return and borrowing cost + management fee)
  • Risks in CDOs: default (hits equity / junior tranche first), interest-rate (basis), liquidity, prepayment, reinvestment, foreign-exchange
  • CDS = Credit (loan) + Default (non-payment) + Swap (exchange of risk): buyer pays periodic premium; seller pays on default. Features: non-standardised private (forward-type) contract; not exchange-traded; ISDA guidelines; can be bought from a third party; bondholder can protect against issuer insolvency; premium rises with risk. Uses: hedging, arbitrage, speculation. Parties: reference entity, buyer (investor), seller (writer). Settlement: physical (deliver bonds, get par) or cash (par − market value)

17Commodity derivatives (SM 10)

  • Necessary conditions (SM): commodity durable & storable; units homogeneous; frequent price fluctuations with wide amplitude; supply & demand large; supply flows naturally to market and there are breakdowns in existing pattern of forward contracting
  • Investing: complements investment in commodity-using companies; invest in a country's consumption & production; no dividends, only price returns. Market advantages: managers prefer derivatives to tangibles; less hassle (delivery); indirect real-asset exposure — inflation hedge
  • Futures: easiest & cheapest way; 3 categories — agricultural (soft), energy, metals. Swaps: fixed-floating and commodity-for-interest; valued as a strip of forwards; factors — hedging cost, institutional structure, liquidity, seasonality, futures bid / offer spread, brokerage, credit risk / capital / admin costs

18Weather & electricity derivatives (SM 11–12)

  • Weather: underlying = weather measure (rainfall, temperature, humidity, wind); hedges volume risk, not price risk; first deal 1997 (Aquila Energy, OTC). vs insurance: insurance covers extreme low-probability events; weather derivatives cover normal-condition uncertainty too. Pricing issues: data reliability, weather forecasting, temperature modelling
  • Electricity: forwards (fixed quantity at forward price at maturity) · futures (standardised trading, settlement) · swaps (pay fixed vs floating price; = strip of forwards; locational basis swaps)

19Derivative mishaps & 6 lessons (SM 13)

Orange County (1994)Treasurer Robert Citron, no trading background; yield-curve play + ~$2.8 bn inverse floaters; leveraged ~$7.5 bn fund to ~$20 bn via repo; Fed raised rates 6 times in 1994 → loss ~$2 bn; Citron jailed 1 yr, fined $1,00,000
Barings (1995)Nick Leeson: arbitrage Nikkei 225 futures Singapore vs Osaka turned speculative long; also ran back office, hid losses in error account '8888'; loss $827 mn (SM also cites $1.4 bn); 233-year-old bank bankrupt; ING bought it for £1. Led to operational-risk capital in Basel II
P&G & Gibson Greetings (1994)Bankers Trust sold complex leveraged (ratio / power) swaps; P&G lost $106 mn; Gibson $3 mn then $16.7 mn; out-of-court settlements; BT taken over by Deutsche Bank (1999)

Lessons: (a) don't buy a derivative you don't understand (value in-house) · (b) due diligence before making Treasury a profit centre (don't link treasurer's pay to profit) · (c) specify risk limits · (d) separate front, middle & back offices · (e) a hedger shouldn't become a speculator · (f) stress tests, scenario analysis (VaR + scenarios, 10–20 yrs data for black swans).
