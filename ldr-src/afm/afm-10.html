1SWIFT & payment gateways (SM)

  • SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) — purely a messaging system; assigns 3-letter currency codes (USD, GBP, CNY, CAD, AUD, HKD, INR, JPY, NZD, SGD, SEK, CHF, EUR)
  • Why important (5): quick settlement (messaging in seconds) besides validation & documentation · catalyst bringing financial agencies to work together · security & reliability reduce operational risk · standardised transactions → efficiency & lower cost · full backup & recovery
  • Payment gateway — virtual equivalent of physical cash transfer; authenticates & routes payment details securely. Benefits: 24×7×365; real-time card authorisation; rapid processing; multiple payment options; encryption & verification; real-time reports; refunds; no complex software for merchants; CA-authenticated secure servers; bulk data collection; multiple host interfaces; simple admin control; customer & merchant trust. Challenges: customer may lack account with supporting banks; limited banks; reliability, delivery, limited avenues, lack of trust

3Forward points & broken period (SM)

  • Forward rate is quoted as spot + forward margin (points); user loads margin into spot. Points depend on interest rates & length; the currency with the lower interest rate is always at a premium
  • Premium (direct quote): added to both buying & selling rates · Discount: deducted from both. Points ascending (bid < ask) → add; descending → subtract
  • Broken period: forward points assumed to "grow" uniformly — interpolate proportionately
SM illustration — spot ₹66.2525 / 67.5945; 2-m points 70 / 90; 3-m 160 / 186; need 2 m 15 d (1 point = 0.0001)
Points for 15 days = (3-m − 2-m) ÷ 24548
Points for 2 m 15 d115138
Forward rate66.264067.6083
Annual premium on average rate = 0.0115 ÷ 66.2583 × 12/2.50.0833%0.0980%

Annualised premium = (F − S) ÷ S × 12 ÷ months × 100; when the question says "on average rate", divide by (S + F) ÷ 2 as above.

4Cross rates & arbitrage

  • Chain like-to-like: A/C bid = A/B bid × B/C bid; ask = ask × ask. Common base: divide bid by ask
  • SM: ₹/$ 62.22 and ¥/$ 102.34 → ₹/¥ = 62.22 ÷ 102.34 = 0.6080
  • Arbitrage test: buy via one route at ask, sell via the other at bid; gain only if buy-ask < sell-bid

9Money market hedge

SM: agreement to exchange a certain amount of one currency for a fixed amount of another at a particular date. Advantages: fixes future rate (no downside); flexible amount; usable where forwards aren't available. Disadvantages: more complicated than a forward; no benefit from favourable moves.

ReceivablePayable
1. Borrow FC = receivable ÷ (1 + FC borrowing rate × t)1. Deposit FC = payable ÷ (1 + FC deposit rate × t)
2. Convert at spot bid2. Buy FC at spot ask (borrow home currency if needed)
3. Invest home currency at deposit rate3. Cost = home borrowing × (1 + rate × t)
4. Receivable repays FC loan; compare with forward4. FC deposit pays supplier; compare with forward

12Delivery · Cancellation · Extension (SM)

EventSM treatment
Delivery on due dateAt originally agreed rate, irrespective of spot
Early deliveryBank may accept if customer bears the bank's loss: fixed charges + swap difference (loss / gain from offsetting bank's cover) + interest on outlay of funds at not less than prime lending rate (if inflow, bank may pass interest at term-deposit rate)
Late deliveryCurrent rate on delivery date — after applying automatic-cancellation provisions
Cancellation on due dateFlat charges + difference between contracted rate and cancellation rate (reverse of original) charged / paid
Early cancellationAt the opposite rate of the original contract for the date that matches the original maturity (forward rate)
Late cancellationAutomatic-cancellation provisions apply first
Extension on due dateCancel at spot (as above) and rebook at forward rate for the new period
Extension before due dateCancel at relevant forward rate and rebook at current forward rate
Automatic cancellationFEDAI Rule 6: overdue contract without instructions on / before due date is automatically cancelled within 3 working days after maturity; customer pays the exchange difference but is not entitled to any profit

SM Illustration 4 (early delivery, exporter): contract sell US$1,00,000 at 65.40 for 31 Dec; delivered 30 Nov. Bank sells spot 65.22, buys 1-m forward 65.27 + 0.15 = 65.42 → swap loss 0.20 × 1,00,000 = ₹20,000; interest on outlay 0.18 × 1,00,000 × 18% × 31/365 = ₹275 → net inflow = 65,40,000 − 20,275 = ₹65,19,725.

Cancellation = the bank's reverse action: customer's purchase contract → bank buys back (TT buying); sale contract → bank sells (TT selling). On automatic cancellation the customer bears losses but never gets gains.

13NDF & rollover (SM)

  • Non-deliverable forward: cash-settled, short-term forward on a thinly traded / non-convertible currency; P/L = (agreed rate − spot at settlement) × notional
  • Rollover = spot cancellation + new contract for a later date. Reasons: non-receipt of FC from client (exporter); shortage of local currency (importer); non-agreement of payment with clients; non-availability of longer-period forwards

14Swaps (SM)

  • Currency swap: spot exchange of principal → continuing exchange of interest → re-exchange of principal on maturity. Benefits: considerable cost savings; access currencies that would otherwise command a high premium; diversification of borrowings; treasurers can hedge currency risk
  • Commodity swap: series of futures-type settlements on a notional amount at predetermined prices / indices — mainly to hedge oil price risk
  • Equity swap: total return on equity / index (dividend + capital) exchanged for fixed or floating interest

16Strategies for exposure management (SM — 4)

Low risk : low rewardAutomatic hedging in forward market as soon as exposure arises
Low risk : reasonable rewardSelective hedging — hedge when forward rates attractive, keep open otherwise
High risk : low rewardLeave all exposures unhedged — perhaps the worst
High risk : high rewardActive trading — continuous cancellations & re-bookings of forwards

Choice depends on attitude to risk, financial strength, nature of business, vulnerability to adverse movements — no single strategy suits all.
