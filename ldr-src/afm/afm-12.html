1Interest rates & benchmarks (SM 1–2)

  • Interest = cost of borrowing + compensation for service and risk of lending (default) + protection against inflation. Factors (macro): (a) demand / supply of money (b) inflation (c) government — biggest borrower; RBI via note issue / OMO changes CRR, SLR, bank rate
  • Benchmark (reference) rate: basis for other rates — loans, overdrafts, mortgages, forwards, futures, options and especially swaps; floating loans = benchmark + bps by credit rating; decided by an independent body; also used to value bonds
  • LIBOR: manipulation news 2012 → decided 2017 to end → from 1 Jan 2022 use Alternative Reference Rates (ARRs). ARRs differ: (i) based on actual overnight transactions (secured or unsecured), LIBOR unsecured and relied on panel banks' judgment (ii) near risk-free, no term premium

Secured ARRs = SOFR and SARON (both start with S). The other three are unsecured.

3Types of interest rate risk (SM 3.1)

TypeSM meaning
Gap / mismatchAssets, liabilities, off-BS items with different principal, maturity or re-pricing dates. Positive gap = RSA > RSL → rate rise raises NII. Negative gap = RSL > RSA → NII rises when rates fall. Earnings at Risk (EaR) = gap × assumed rate change; bank fixes an EaR limit (based on last / current income) and a trigger for on- / off-BS hedging.
BasisRates on different assets / liabilities change by different magnitudes (eg assets fixed, liabilities floating); high for banks funding composite assets from composite liabilities; NII expands = favourable basis shift
Embedded optionPrepayment of loans, call / put on bonds, premature withdrawal of deposits; faster and bigger rate moves → bigger risk; adjust gap statements, charge penalties based on opportunity cost
Yield curveYield-curve movements over business cycles affect portfolio values and income
PriceAssets sold before maturity; prices and yields inverse; tied to trading book → limit size, holding period, duration, defeasance period, stop-loss, MTM
ReinvestmentUncertainty about rate at which future cash flows can be reinvested; mismatches vary NII

Gap report — 5 limitations (SM)

(1) only timing difference of re-pricing; ignores basis and embedded option risk (2) assumes all items in a time band re-price together; ignores effect on economic / market value (3) ignores timing differences in payments caused by rate changes (4) assumes parallel shift of yield curve — seldom happens (5) ignores variability in non-interest revenue & expenses

4Measuring interest rate risk (SM 3.2)

  • Must identify and quantify first; system should cover gap, basis, embedded option, yield curve, price, reinvestment and net interest position risks
  • Techniques: maturity gap analysis (sensitivity of earnings) · duration (sensitivity of capital) · simulation · VaR — often used in combination / hybrid

May 2026 Q5(b) (ICAI): re-pricing gap = RSA − RSL (negative); EaR = gap × rate change × ¼ (quarter) vs limit of 8% of NII → hedge if EaR exceeds the limit. Rates up hurts a negative gap; rates down helps.

5Hedging methods (SM 4)

Traditional: (i) Asset Liability Management (ALM) (ii) Forward Rate Agreement (FRA). Modern: (i) Interest Rate Futures (ii) Interest Rate Options (iii) Interest Rate Swaps (+ swaptions).

6ALM (SM 4.1.1)

Evolved by RBI; introduced in Indian banks w.e.f. 1 April 1999. Comprehensive, dynamic framework to measure, monitor and manage liquidity, interest rate, forex, equity and commodity price risks, integrated with business strategy; structure the balance sheet so net interest earnings are maximised within risk preference. Extends to liquidity, market and trading risk, funding & capital planning, profit planning, growth projection. First step in long-term strategic planning — an intermediate-term planning function.

7Forward Rate Agreement (SM 4.1.2) — TYK Q1

  • Borrower / lender protects against adverse rate changes; OTC (not exchange-traded); used by banks to fix cost of future deposits / revenue of variable loans indexed to SOFR, €STER, MIBOR
  • Off-balance-sheet; no transfer of principal (notional); cash-settled for the difference
  • RR > FR → seller pays buyer (buyer = borrower protected); RR < FR → buyer pays seller
  • Settled at the start of the period → difference discounted at the actual (RR) rate

Settlement = N × (RR − FR) × (dtm/DY) ÷ [1 + RR × (dtm/DY)]   (DY = 360 or 365)

SM eg: 1 × 4 FRA (deposit placed after 1 month, matures 4 months from contract) at 5%, $1 m, 90 days; SOFR 6% → 1 bp = $25 → $2,500 ÷ [1 + 0.06 × 90/360] = $2,463.05 to buyer.

TYK 1 (app-computed): ₹60 cr, 3 months in 6 months, FRA 9.30% → at 9.60% company receives ₹4,39,453; at 8.80% company pays ₹7,33,855. TYK 2: 6 × 9 FRA ₹100 cr at 5.25%, 3-month MIBOR 5.50% → +25 bp, settlement ≈ ₹6,16,523.

8Interest rate futures (SM 4.2.1)

  • Futures on an interest-bearing asset — bonds are the underlying, not the rate. Borrower fearing a rise sells futures (prices fall when rates rise → profit on buying back)
  • NSE: futures on 6, 10 and 13-year GoI securities and 91-day T-bill. Settlement: daily MTM + physical delivery any day in expiry month; final settlement only on expiry date
  • Conversion factor (published by NSE; introduced by RBI): CF × futures price = delivery price of a given bond
  • Cheapest to deliver: seller's profit = futures price × CF − quoted spot price; CTD = bond that maximises profit / minimises loss

9Interest rate options (SM 4.2.2)

Also called Interest Rate Guarantee (IRG): a right, not obligation — insurance against adverse moves while keeping favourable ones. An IRO is basically a series of FRAs exercisable at set rates each period.

OptionWho / whatPayoff (SM)
Cap (call)Buyer (borrower) pays premium; receives difference when benchmark > cap rate; no obligation if below. Evolved from IRGs on floating-rate loans (1985); LBO boom — lenders required caps. Features: notional, benchmark, cap rate (= strike), period with reset / payment datesN × max(0, r − rc) × dt/days
Floor (put)Buyer (lender / depositor) protected from fall in rates; = right to sell an FRA → European put on an FRA; multi-period floor = bundle of puts on a sequence of FRAsN × max(0, rf − r) × dt/days
CollarBuy a cap + sell a floor → rate bounded on both sides; cheaper than a cap alone (floor premium offsets); zero-cost collar = floor premium exactly offsets cap premium; gives up benefit of a big fallN × [max(0, r − rc) − max(0, rf − r)] × dt/days

SM cap eg: 1-yr cap, $1 m, 6-month SOFR cap 5%, reset 15 July (first period excluded — known at inception); SOFR 5.5% → paid next 15 Jan (184 days): 1,000,000 × 0.005 × 184/360 = $2,555.56.

10ICAI patterns

  • Jan 2026 Q4(a): borrower buys cap, depositor buys floor. Lump-sum premium converted to per-period: P = A × rp ÷ PVAF(i, n) (i = fixed rate per period). AB Ltd ₹5 m, 0.4%, PVAF(4%, 10) 8.1108 → ₹2,466; gain 2% × ½ × 5 m = 50,000 → net ₹47,534. XY Ltd ₹2 m, 0.5%, PVAF(4%, 6) 5.2421 → ₹1,908; net ₹18,092. ICAI also accepted n − 1 resets.
  • Cap to peg at 9% (MTP): pay actual rate, receive excess over 9% → net cost never above 9%; years at or below 9% → option lapses. Cap preferred to a collar here — selling the floor at 9% causes outflow when rates fall below 9%.
  • May 2026 Q4(c): zero-cost collar — value of cap = value of floor (PV of expected payoffs) → solve the floor rate.

Cap / floor is per reset period — multiply by the fraction of the year (½ for 6-month resets). The first period is usually excluded (rate already known).

11Interest rate swaps (SM 4.2.3)

  • Counterparties exchange payments indexed to two different rates on a notional principal that is never exchanged. Early swap dealers took a brokerage fee only; contract was between the end users
  • Fixed / floating swap: fixed rate · floating rate reset periodically · notional · term with reset and payment dates. Settlement 2 days after trade date. Fixed rate quoted as All-In-Cost (AIC) against a flat floating index
  • Fixed payment (semi-annual) = N × AIC × 180/360 (30-day months, 360-day year); floating receipt = N × R × dt/360
TypeSM meaning
Plain vanilla (generic / coupon)Fixed ↔ floating on notional; floating = SOFR, €STER, SONIA, MIBOR, PLR
Basis rate (non-generic)Both legs floating on different benchmarks (eg 1-month vs 3-month SOFR)
Asset swapFixed-rate investments (bonds) swapped for floating-rate investments (an index)
AmortisingNotional declines over the life — for issuers of redeemable bonds / debentures (hedges the redemption profile)

12Swaptions (SM 4.2.4) — TYK Q2

  • Option on an IRS — right, not obligation, to enter a swap at a future date, fixed rate and term. Payer (call) swaption: pay fixed, receive floating. Receiver (put) swaption: receive fixed, pay floating
  • Features: option on a forward-start IRS with terms fixed at inception · "3-month into 5-year" = option to enter a 5-yr IRS in 3 months · option period = trade date to expiry · premium in basis points · can be cash-settled (MTM off the forward curve). Pricing: model of distribution of the forward zero-coupon curve
  • Uses: (a) traders & treasurers (b) speculate / hedge swap books (c) hedge embedded optionality (d) borrowers targeting an acceptable rate (e) businesses tendering for contracts (f) protection on callable / puttable bonds
