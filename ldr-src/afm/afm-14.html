Ch 14 — Mergers, Acquisitions & Corporate Restructuring

SM TYK theory Qs: synergy (para 2) · reverse merger / takeover by reverse bid (6) · equity carve-out vs spin-off (7.2) · horizontal vs vertical merger (3). Numericals: swap ratio, post-merger EPS / MPS, true cost, restructuring.

1Conceptual framework (SM 1)

  • Corporate restructuring = expansion or contraction of operations, or changes in assets, financial or ownership structure. Internal: new plant, R&D, hiving off non-core, divestment, sell-offs, demerger. External: M&A, JVs, strategic alliances.
  • Merger = two entities unify into one (legal name in India: amalgamation). Acquisition = one buys out another; both may remain separate entities. Consolidation = both dissolve, new one created.
  • Halsbury: amalgamation = blending of undertakings, shareholders of each becoming substantially shareholders of the amalgamated company. Only one company + rights varied = reconstruction / arrangement.
  • Law: Companies Act 2013, Ch XV (s 230–240) — compromise, arrangements, amalgamations, CDR, demergers, fast-track mergers, cross-border mergers. Takeovers regulated by SEBI; also FEMA, IT Act (s 72A revival), Competition Act 2002, IDRA 1951.
  • India's first takeover attempt: Swaraj Paul – Escorts.

2Rationale for M&A (SM 2) — TYK Q1 "synergy"

Synergistic operating economics: V(AB) > V(A) + V(B). Synergy = increase in performance of the combined firm over what the two would achieve independently (Mark Sirower, "The Synergy Trap"). Sources: complementary activities (one has branch network, other efficient production) and economies of scale (shared central services). Economies may be real (less input per unit) or pecuniary (lower prices for bulk inputs).

  • Diversification — unrelated merger cuts business risk → lower discount rate → higher value; more so with independent / negatively correlated income streams
  • Taxation — set off / carry forward of losses; target's losses set off against acquirer's profits
  • Growth — faster than organic: shorter "time to market" (no delays on site, plant, hiring)
  • Consolidation of production capacity & market power — reduced competition

SM examples: growth / snuff competition — Airtel–Loop, Facebook–WhatsApp · capability — Google–Motorola (patents), Flipkart–Myntra · new market — Airtel–Zain, Cargill–Wipro · funds — Ranbaxy–Sun, Jaypee–UltraTech · tax — Burger King–Tim Hortons, DPL–WBPDCL.

4Gains from merger (SM 4.1)

Value of acquirer + stand-alone value of target + value of synergy = combined value

Cost of acquisition = premium over market value + integration costs · Net gain = synergy − premium

SM eg: VA 100, VB 50, VAB 175 → synergy 25; premium 10 → net gain 15

Value can be made from non-synergistic acquisitions too — operating improvements are a big source; managerial talent is the key; tie executive pay / equity to post-merger performance (Anslinger & Copeland, HBR 1996).

5Scheme, evaluation, finance (SM 4.2–4.4)

  • Scheme = heart of the process; no prescribed form. Must vest all assets & liabilities of transferor in transferee (else dispose / settle first — otherwise defective, court won't sanction). Define the effective date. Courts won't re-examine valuation if fair — Hindustan Lever: SC won't interfere when > 99% shareholders approved.
  • Financial evaluation asks: (a) maximum price to pay (b) principal risk areas (c) cash-flow & balance-sheet implications (d) best way to structure the deal
  • Financing: cash, stock or both (in Definitive Agreement). Pay in shares even with cash available — keep a war chest for future deals, or acquirer's shares are overpriced. LBOs: immediate funding by bridge financing, long-term funds later.

8Reverse merger (SM 6) — TYK Q2

  • Smaller company gains control of a larger one; the healthy, prosperous company's undertaking is merged into the sick, non-viable one (sick = net worth eroded). Also "takeover by reverse bid" / "back door listing".
  • 3 tests: (i) transferor's assets > transferee's (ii) equity issued by transferee > its original issued capital (iii) change of control in transferee through a minority holder / group
  • Benefits: easy access to capital market · visibility · tax benefit on carry-forward losses of the acquired public company · cheaper, easier route to become public

9Divestiture (SM 7)

Reasons (4): (i) focus on core (ii) division not contributing enough revenue (iii) firm too big to handle (iv) cash needed urgently for other opportunities. Seller's perspective: selling needs the same skill as buying; know your worth. Sell-side imperatives: competitor pressure; sale inevitable (no new technology, entry barriers, badly positioned, critical mass not reached, poor use of distribution, no new SBUs, not enough capital) · window of opportunity · focus on core competencies · best interest of shareholders.

Form (SM 7.2)SM meaning
Sell-off / partial sell-offSale of asset, factory, division, product line or subsidiary for cash / securities; unit doesn't fit core strategy; market may undervalue combined business; raises cash (raiders sold subsidiaries to service LBO debt)
Spin-offPart separated into a new firm; existing shareholders get proportionate ownership; management parted; no fresh cash. Reasons: separate identity · avoid takeover (valuable division spun off) · separate regulated & unregulated lines. Eg Future Group → Ezone
Split-upEntire firm broken into a series of spin-offs; parent ceases to exist; smaller units more manageable
Equity carve-outLike spin-off but some shares sold to the public by IPO → brings cash; parent keeps controlling stake; used when the subsidiary grows faster / has higher valuation; fails if carved out as a burden
Demerger of family business~80% of Indian private companies family-managed; hive off unprofitable units for succession; step 1 identify core / non-core, step 2 cut interest through debt restructuring + sale of surplus assets

TYK Q3 — carve-out vs spin-off: both create a separate company; spin-off shares go to existing shareholders pro rata with no cash; carve-out sells shares to the public (IPO), raises cash, parent retains control.

10Financial restructuring (SM 8) = internal reconstruction

  • Internal changes in assets & liabilities with stakeholders' consent, for firms with sizeable losses / eroded or negative net worth → fresh balance sheet free of losses and fictitious assets
  • Sacrifice order: equity shareholders most, then preference holders, debenture holders, lenders, creditors — waivers, lower coupons, debt-to-equity conversion
  • Results: (i) reduction / waiver of claims (ii) assets at real worth by revaluation (iii) gains on appreciation used to write off losses, fictitious assets, provide for doubtful debts

SM Illus 1 (XYZ): benefits = equity reduced 450 + preference 100 + debenture interest waived 26 + creditors 25% waived 75 + land revalued 50 = 701; used for P&L 485 + issue cost 5 + preliminary 10 + bad-debt provision 5 + P&M write-down 196 = 701. New BS total ₹825 lakh; cash = 130 + 150 − 74 = 206.

Ch 14 · Ownership restructuring, value, failuresTHEORY

11Ownership restructuring (SM 9)

  • Going private: listed company converted into private by buying back all outstanding shares (Essar Energy delisted from LSE, 2014); via management buyout or tender offer; usually heavy debt
  • MBO: company bought by its own management; useful to exit non-core divisions
  • LBO: financed entirely or partially (50% or more) by borrowed funds; target goes private; secured on acquired firm's physical assets; improve efficiency → cash flow repays debt → may go public again. Targets: market leaders, proven demand, strong management, steady growth. India's first: Tata Tea – Tetley (2000), ₹2,135 cr, ~77% debt; Tata Steel – Corus (7.6 bn)
  • Equity buyback: surplus cash buys shares from public (opposite of IPO); shares cease to exist; raises promoter stake

Effects of buyback (SM): (i) controlling holders' % rises (ii) EPS rises → price steps up (iii) BS: assets (cash) and equity both fall; cash outflow under financing activities and in statement of changes in equity (iv) ROA, ROE improve. SM eg: ₹1.5 cr of ₹2 cr cash, 1 L shares bought → assets 5 → 3.5 cr, ROA 4% → 5.71%, EPS 2.00 → 2.22.

12Unlocking value (SM 10)

Horizontal → optimum size, market share, curb competition, use idle capacity · vertical → save costs, avoid taxes / duties · diversification · use idle funds of another company · merge export / trading co. with industrial co. (cash flow) · merge subsidiary into holding co. (cash flow) · take over a shell with licences · nourish a sick unit of the group (image). Restructuring → more competitive, survive adverse climate, new direction.

13Premium & discount (SM 11)

Premiums: market share, controlling stake, brand value; discounts: small player, unlisted company. Work out scenarios with probabilities. Timing matters — economic cycle (GDP), stock market (multiples), global events. Synergy realisation risk (eg Satyam Infoway – India World, 2000). Use several methods; NAV / past-earnings methods may be inadequate for growing or intangible-heavy businesses.

14M&A failures (SM 12)

5 steps of a successful programme

1 Manage pre-acquisition (keep secrecy) · 2 Screen candidates · 3 Eliminate misfits, value the rest · 4 Negotiate · 5 Post-merger integration

Why mergers fail

Acquirers overpay · synergy over-estimated · poor post-merger integration · psychological barriers. Evidence: Jensen & Ruback — target holders ~20%, acquirer ~0%; McKinsey — 61% of 116 failed, 23% succeeded

To succeed: decide post-merger tasks · choose managers from both companies (and outside) · set performance yardsticks and evaluate · motivate them.

15Acquisition through shares (SM 13)

  • Steps: value acquirer's equity → value target's equity → maximum shares that can be exchanged → pessimistic & optimistic scenarios
  • Exchange ratio = acquirer's shares given per target share. SM eg: B's 1,00,000 shares at ₹230; A values itself ₹200 → 230/200 = 1.15 → 1,15,000 shares
  • Share count set by: (1) acquirer compares value per share with / without merger (2) seller compares its value with value of shares received (3) negotiation — reflects bargaining position. Fewer shares issued → acquirer better off, target worse off; both bear synergy risk
  • Impact of P/E: P/E = reciprocal of cost of equity, reflects risk; combined P/E may differ. Value = E × P/E → a lower combined P/E can offset synergy; a higher one can add value even without synergy

16Exam working (ICAI answer patterns; app-written method)

Swap ratio = target's measure ÷ acquirer's (EPS / BV / MP / intrinsic), or weighted average. Banks: CRAR (higher better), NPA (lower better → acquirer ÷ target). MTP Nov 2026: 4.0 × 0.40 + 0.8 × 0.25 + 2.0 × 0.35 = 2.5; promoters 34.50 of 57.50 L = 60%; EPS 400 ÷ 57.50 = ₹6.956.

EPSAB = (EA + EB + synergy) ÷ (NA + NB × ER) · MPSAB = EPSAB × P/EAB · equivalent EPS of target = EPSAB × ER

Max ER (acquirer not diluted): EPSAB = EPSA · Min ER (target not worse): EPSAB × ER = EPSB (Illus 3(ii)); same on MPS basis

True cost, share deal = target's % of merged firm × VAB − VB. PYQ May 2024: 20% × 2,800 − 500 = ₹60 L; cash deal = cash − VB.

17Cross-border M&A (SM 14)

Route for global growth; Tata Steel CFO: organic growth in India too slow for large capital projects, few domestic targets. Motivating factors (5): globalisation of production & distribution · integration of global economies · expansion of international trade & investment · countries reforming laws and giving investment & tax incentives · privatisation of state enterprises and banking consolidation.

18SPACs (SM 15)

  • "Blank cheque company": raises funds by IPO to acquire an unidentified target within a specific time; no operations / revenue; money in escrow; not acquired in time → delisted, money returned; holders may redeem if not interested; on approval target becomes public
  • India: no specific framework. Companies Act — RoC can strike off a company not commencing operations within one year; SPACs take 2–3 years → enabling provisions needed. Not eligible for listing under SEBI; SEBI planning a framework. IFSCA (GIFT City) released a consultation paper (offer size, sponsor holding, minimum application / subscription)
  • Advantages: capital even in volatile markets · lower fees, faster listing · negotiate with a single entity not thousands of investors · skilled professionals pick the target
  • Challenges: complex accounting / reporting; public-company readiness in 3–5 months of LOI; typically more expensive than an IPO; investors' money idle in trust up to two years
