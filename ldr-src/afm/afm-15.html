Ch 15 — Startup Finance

Pure theory — 4-mark questions. SM TYK theory Qs: innovative sources of funding a start-up (para 2) · pitch presentation (para 3). Also asked: unicorn, VC characteristics / stages / process, Startup India definition, succession planning.

1Basics of startup financing (SM 1)

  • Initial infusion of money to turn an idea into reality. Banks aren't interested — no assets to rely on, cash flows / repayment capacity can't be forecast
  • Left with: sell assets, borrow against home, family & friends — risk of bankruptcy and strained relationships → keep family loans strictly business-like
  • Speed up operations so outside investors can see the venture and the founder's own risk; bootstrapping (personal finances or the venture's operating revenue) avoids investors dictating terms; need a strong business plan and advice from experienced entrepreneurs

2Innovative ways to finance a startup (SM 2) — TYK Q1

Banks are the least likely benefactors → maximise non-bank financing.

3Pitch presentation (SM 3) — TYK Q2

Short presentation (not more than 20 minutes) to investors on the company's prospects and why they should invest; usually a PowerPoint pitch deck giving a quick overview of the business plan; face-to-face or online, to investors, customers, partners, co-founders. 9 points:

  1. Introduction — who you are, what you do; short; interesting facts, milestones
  2. Team — investors invest in the team, not only the idea; promoter's background; past joint results
  3. Problem — the problem solved. SM eg: Facebook (2004) vs Orkut — profile visible only if you are added (privacy)
  4. Solution — how it is solved. SM eg: Flipkart (2007) — cash on delivery (cards rare) + own supply chain for timely delivery
  5. Marketing / sales — market size, target customers, how to attract them, growth & revenue forecasts
  6. Projections / milestones — educated guess if no history: income statement (monthly in year 1, quarterly in year 2, annual after), cash flow (cash burn / generated, closing cash), balance sheet (annual)
  7. Competition — how you differ; details of competitors acquired (names, prices)
  8. Business model — how the company earns revenue and profit; gross profit compares models. SM eg: two movie-rental firms, revenue ₹5 cr; A goes online → COGS ₹4 cr → ₹2 cr → GP margin 20% → 60%. Show revenue streams & timeline, pricing vs competitors, customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, retention
  9. Financing — money raised, from whom, how used; if none, what was achieved with minimum funding; how much is sought and its use; investors like founders who invested own money

4Unicorn (SM 4)

  • Privately held start-up valued at US$1 billion; term coined by VC Aileen Lee in 2013; mythical animal = statistical rarity of success. Features: private · US$1 bn valuation · rarity · new ideas, disruptive innovation, consumer focus, high technology
  • Status can be lost if valuation slips below US$1 bn
  • India's first: InMobi (Sept 2011; SoftBank US$200 m; founded 2007). Fastest: Udaan (2018, just over 2 years). Then Flipkart 2012, Mu Sigma 2013, Ola & Snapdeal 2014, Paytm / Quikr / Zomato 2015 …
  • India = 3rd largest start-up ecosystem (after US, China); 59,000+ DPIIT-recognised start-ups; as of 3 Oct 2023, 111 unicorns worth US$349.67 bn. Next milestone: Decacorn (> US$10 bn)

5Bootstrapping (SM 5.1.1)

Start and build from personal finances or the company's operating revenue. More money at inception → complacency and waste; own savings → caution, curbs waste.

  • (a) Trade credit — suppliers reluctant; show a well-crafted financial plan to the owner / CFO, get a reference; maybe half on credit; sell before you pay; cuts working capital (esp. retail)
  • (b) Factoring — receivables sold to a commercial finance company, which collects and does the paperwork; can be on a non-notification basis; cuts bookkeeping, collection, credit-check costs; frees cash (esp. B2B / government with long delays)
  • (c) Leasing — lease the photocopier (₹5,000 p.m. vs ₹1,00,000) or van (vs ₹5,00,000). Lessor: depreciation tax benefit, capital appreciation, profit. Lessee: smaller payments, can walk away at end, lease rentals tax-deductible

6Angel investors (SM 5.1.2)

  • Invest in small startups, often from family & friends; one-time or ongoing; more favourable terms — invest in the entrepreneur rather than the viability of the business; focus on first steps, not profit
  • Also called informal investors, angel funders, private, seed investors, business angels; affluent individuals; take equity or convertible debt; may use crowdfunding platforms / angel networks
  • Use own money (VCs manage pooled funds); vehicle may be an LLC, business, trust or fund. Early failure = total loss → look for a defined exit (acquisition, IPO)

7Venture capital (SM 5.1.3)

Investment vehicle managing investors' funds for startups with exceptional growth potential; professionals invest alongside management in young, rapidly growing companies. VCs: finance new & rapidly growing companies · buy equity · help develop new products · add value through active participation.

Characteristics (4)

(i) Long horizon — minimum 3, maximum 10 years (ii) Lack of liquidity — adds an illiquidity premium (iii) High risk, high return (iv) Equity participation — board seat, supervise decisions

Advantages (7)

Long-term equity → solid capital base · partner sharing risk & reward · practical advice from experience · network of contacts · further funding rounds · experienced in preparing an IPO (incl. NASDAQ) · can facilitate a trade sale

9VC investment process (SM 5.2) — 6 steps

  1. Deal origination — directly or via intermediaries (mainly practising CAs); VC states sector, stage, promoter, turnover focus; company gives Investment Memorandum (business model, financial plan, exit plan, tentative valuation)
  2. Screening — committee of senior VC people
  3. Due diligence — verify documents; usually external consultants; fee paid by VC or shared
  4. Deal structuring — both win; convertible structure lets promoter buy back; tag-along clause — promoter must sell part of stake along with VC
  5. Post-investment — VC nominee on board; strong MIS, budgeting, governance, covenants, milestone reporting; professional management
  6. Exit plan — (a) sell to third parties (IPO or private placement to other VCs) (b) promoter buy-back at pre-agreed rate (IRR generally 18–25%), often as right of first refusal

10VC funds in India (SM 6)

  • Evolution: committee in the 1970s; first all-India VC funding by IDBI, ICICI, IFCI about ten years later; CCI guidelines Nov 1988 (narrow, restrictive); World Bank seminar → TDICICI, GVFL, Canbank, Pathfinder; CCI abolished; foreign finance cos allowed 1995; re-regulation 1996 favoured foreign players; boost from IT revolution 1997; dotcom bust → shift to later-stage funding
  • Formation: trust · company · LLP. Investors: FIs, banks, pension funds, corporations, HNIs
  • Structures: domestic — pooling vehicle (trust or company; trust preferred for flexibility; India doesn't recognise limited partnerships) + separate investment adviser · offshore — LLC / LP abroad invests directly, managed offshore, Indian adviser does diligence · unified — offshore vehicle invests in a local trust into which domestic investors contribute directly

Ch 15 · Startup India, succession, correlationTHEORY + LINKS

11Startup India (SM 7)

Launched 16 Jan 2016. Per GSR 127(E), 19 Feb 2019, an entity is a startup: (i) up to 10 years from incorporation, as a private limited company, registered partnership firm or LLP in India (ii) turnover ≤ ₹100 crore in every year since incorporation (iii) working on innovation / improvement of products, processes or services, or a scalable model with high employment or wealth creation. Not if formed by splitting up or reconstructing an existing business.

  • Startup India & StandUp India (2016); SIDBI-run Electronic Development Fund — government an LP for the first time; Mudra; 100% tax holiday u/s 80-IAC; exemption from angel tax
  • Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS), DPIIT, Jan 2021: ₹945 crore; ~3,600 entrepreneurs via 300 incubators over 4 years; DPIIT-recognised, incorporated ≤ 2 years before applying; up to ₹50 lakh; proof of concept, prototype, trials, market entry, commercialisation
  • Why India thrives: (i) talent pool (ii) cost-effective workforce (10 m+ join yearly) (iii) internet use (2nd largest base) (iv) technology (AI, blockchain) (v) variety of funding; 100% automatic FDI route in most sectors

12Succession planning (SM 8)

  • Identify critical positions and develop people to fill them; vital for family businesses (often non-family leader, family ownership); can be a liquidity event transferring ownership to rising employees
  • Need (5): risk mitigation (search takes 6–9 months) · cause removal (fraud, gross negligence, barred leader) · talent pipeline · conflict resolution · aligning culture, vision, values
  • Strategy (4 steps): evaluate key leadership positions → map competencies needed → identify competencies of current workforce (train / hire outside) → bridge leader (outsider who prepares young family members)
  • Challenges in startups (3): founder mindset ≠ corporate mindset · premature (processes slow growth) · founders are the face of the startup
