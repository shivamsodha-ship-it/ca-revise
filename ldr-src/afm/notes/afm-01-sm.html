1. Advanced Role of CFO in Various Matters including Value Creation

  • Traditional role: wealth maximisation for shareholders — financial health, adequate financial controls; governance, compliances, controls, business ethics.
  • Expanded by globalisation, growth in information and communications, pandemic; now also support in strategic and operational decision making.

Post-pandemic advanced areas (in addition to traditional role):

  • Risk Management: look after overall functioning of the risk management framework.
  • Supply Chain: SCM challenges sustainable growth; CFO judges its financial viability — role more critical.
  • Mergers, acquisitions and corporate restructuring: strategic decisions; any error can lead to collapse of the whole business.
  • ESG financing: shift from traditional financing to sustainability financing.

Conclusion: CFO takes a leadership role in value creation, on a sustainable basis for a longer period.

2. Strategic Financial Decision-Making Framework

  • Capital investment is the springboard for wealth creation. Investors want maximum expected return at minimum risk; management is responsible to investors → objective: maximisation of shareholders' wealth.
  • Capital is the limiting factor → problem is strategic allocation of limited funds between alternative uses.
  • Three fundamental elements of every business: a clear and realistic strategy; the financial resources, controls and systems to see it through; the right management team and processes.

Strategy + Finance + Management = Fundamentals of Business

  • Strategy = long-term direction and scope of an organisation to achieve competitive advantage through configuration of resources within a changing environment, for fulfilment of stakeholders' aspirations and expectations.
  • Investors care not only about maximum profit but its likelihood — a risk-return trade-off from a portfolio, unique for each individual.

2.1 Meaning of Strategic Financial Management

  • Definition: application of financial management techniques to strategic decisions to help achieve the decision-maker's objectives.
  • Combines backward-looking, report-focused (financial) accounting with dynamic, forward-looking financial management.
  • Identifies strategies capable of maximising market value; allocates scarce capital among competing opportunities; covers implementation and monitoring of the chosen strategy.

2.2 Functions of Strategic Financial Management

  • SFM = portfolio constituent of the corporate strategic plan: optimum investment and financing decisions to attain objectives.

  • Continual search for best investment opportunities;

  • Analysis of results for future decision-making.

Key decisions within financial strategy:

  • Investment: profitable use of funds in long-term (capital) projects; future benefits uncertain → involve risk; evaluated on expected return and risk.
  • Dividend: division of earnings between payout and reinvestment.
  • Portfolio: evaluate investments by contribution to aggregate performance of the whole corporation, not isolated characteristics.

3. Strategy at Different Hierarchy Levels

Outcomes of different planning needs. Three levels: Corporate; Business unit; Functional / departmental.

3.1–3.3 Corporate, Business Unit and Functional Level Strategy

LevelConcerned with
3.1 CorporateSelection of businesses in which to compete; development and coordination of that portfolio of businesses.
3.2 Business unit (SBU)SBU = any profit centre planned independently. Issues: practical coordination of operating units; developing and sustaining competitive advantage for its products/services.
3.3 FunctionalOperating divisions/departments; functional business processes and value chain (R&D, operations, manufacturing, marketing, finance, HR) — the resources through which SBU strategies are executed effectively and efficiently.

Corporate level strategy must answer three questions:

  • Suitability: will it work for the common objective of the company?
  • Feasibility: kind and number of resources required to formulate and implement it.
  • Acceptability: stakeholders' satisfaction — financial and non-financial.
  • Functional units give input upward (customer feedback, resources and capabilities); once higher strategy is set, they translate it into discrete action plans.
  • Finance assumes highest importance in top-down and bottom-up planning. Corporate strategy = deployment of resources; financial strategy = mobilisation and effective utilisation of money, the most critical resource — other resources are easily mobilised with an adequate monetary base.

4. Financial Planning

  • Backbone of business and corporate planning; defines the feasible area of operation and the overall planning framework.
  • Systematic approach: planner helps the customer maximise existing financial resources using financial tools to achieve financial goals.

Financial Planning = FR + FT + FG — Financial Resources + Financial Tools + Financial Goals.

  • Individual: meeting life goals (house, children's education, retirement) through specific steps — where you are now, what you need, what you must do.

Outcomes of financial planning:

  • Financial objectives — decided at the very outset; consistent with corporate mission and objectives.
  • Financial decision making — analyse financial problems faced and decide course of action.
  • Financial measures — ratio analysis, cash flow statement analysis to evaluate performance; choice depends on corporate objectives.

5. Interface of Financial Policy and Strategic Management

TYK T1 (answer: para 5).

  • Starting point and end point of an organisation is money; no business or expansion without an internally (or internally + externally) mobilised financial base.

Dimensions of the interface:

  • Sources of finance and capital structure (most important dimensions of a strategic plan):
    • Ownership capital — equity and/or preference shares; borrowed capital — debentures.
    • Public deposits for fixed period — major short/medium-term source (may offer higher interest than banks).
    • Short-term: overdraft, cash credit, bill discounting, bank loan, trade credit.
    • Debt-equity norms to minimise risk of excessive loans: public sector 1:1, private sector 2:1; ideal varies by industry and planning mode; much higher debt for capital-intensive industries, high-cost priority-sector projects, projects in underdeveloped regions.
  • Investment and fund allocation decisions: policies for investment in fixed assets and restraining current assets. Three groups of proposals:
    • addition of a new product;
    • increase level of operation of existing product (more capacity in existing plant or another plant);
    • cost reduction and efficient use of resources (new approach / closer monitoring of critical activities).
    Evaluate by within-group comparison using capital budgeting; project evaluation and project selection are the two most important jobs; best allocation under resource constraints.
  • Dividend policy: extent of earnings distributed vs retained for expansion. Dividend = part of earnings that cannot be profitably utilised by the company.
    • Stable dividend — positive impact on share price.
    • Constant % of net earnings — flexibility and ability of firm; message of lesser risk.
    • Minimum DPS + extra when earnings exceed normal.
    • Consider investment opportunities, financial needs, shareholders' preference (dividend vs capital gains), cash vs stock dividend.
  • Financial policy cannot be worked out in isolation of other functional policies; investors' awareness and stakeholders' evaluation make patronage depend on the financial policy framework.
  • Frame financial policies during corporate planning itself, not later.
  • Two-way interdependence: sometimes corporate strategy is the cause and financial policy the effect, sometimes the reverse — needs in-depth analytical modelling.

6. Balancing Financial Goals vis-à-vis Sustainable Growth Asked: Sep 2025 Q2(c) 4m

TYK T2 (answer: para 6).

  • Sustainable growth forces managers to consider the financial consequences of sales increases and set sales growth goals consistent with operating and financial policies; conflict if growth objectives exceed sustainable growth.
  • Rightness for future stakeholders too: fuel industry's save fuel campaign = demarketing → example of stable growth strategy under resource constraints. Variants of stable growth: incremental growth, profit, pause strategies.
  • Too fast or too slow growth both harm the enterprise; finance should keep growth speed close to the sustainable growth ratio.

What makes an organisation financially sustainable? It must:

  • have more than one source of income;
  • have more than one way of generating income;
  • do strategic, action and financial planning regularly;
  • have adequate financial systems;
  • have a good public image;
  • be clear about its values (value clarity);
  • have financial autonomy.

6.1 Sustainable Growth Rate Asked: May 2026 Q1(c) 4m

  • SGR (Robert C. Higgins) = maximum rate of growth in sales achievable given the firm's profitability, asset utilisation, desired dividend payout and debt (financial leverage) ratios.
  • Measures how much a firm can grow without borrowing more money; beyond it, the firm must borrow from another source.
  • Variables: net profit margin on new and existing revenues; asset turnover (sales ÷ total assets); assets to equity ratio; retention rate (fraction of earnings retained).
FormulaTerms / logic
SGR = ROE × (1 − Dividend payout ratio)ROE = return on equity; (1 − d) = retention rate. Logic: only new equity is retained earnings, and the asset to beginning-of-period equity ratio is constant (debt grows in step) → sales and assets grow no faster than retained earnings plus the debt they support.

Note (app-written): ROE itself is built from the SM's other three variables — net profit margin × asset turnover × assets to equity — so SGR moves up with any of the four.

Assumptions — the business wants to:

  • maintain a target capital structure without issuing new equity;
  • maintain a target dividend payout ratio;
  • increase sales as rapidly as market conditions allow.
  • Consistent with evidence that corporations are reluctant to issue new equity; if willing to issue equity, there is in principle no financial constraint on growth. Formula is directly predicated on ROE.
  • Twin cornerstones: growth strategy and growth capability — strategy without infrastructure to execute (or the reverse) → long-term growth impossible.
  • Concepts of sustainability: very weak — overall stock of capital assets remains constant; weak — preserve critical resources to support all over a long horizon; strong — preserve resources under primacy of ecosystem functioning.

What makes an organisation sustainable? It must:

  • have a clear strategic direction;
  • be able to scan its environment to identify opportunities;
  • be able to attract, manage and retain competent staff;
  • have adequate administrative and financial infrastructure;
  • demonstrate effectiveness and impact to leverage further resources;
  • get community support and involvement.

Use of SGR model:

  • Borrower requests additional financing (actual growth > SGR): risk of too much debt, too little equity → either raise additional equity, or reduce the rate of expansion to a level sustainable without more financial leverage.
  • Mature firms (actual growth < SGR): find productive uses of excess cash — increase dividends / buy back stock, reduce debt, or hold more lower-earning liquid assets (these lower SGR); or acquire rapidly growing companies to raise actual growth.
  • Inflation: growth = volume + inflation; inflationary asset increase must be financed like real growth → more external financing and higher historical-cost D/E → if creditors fix historical D/E, inflation lowers SGR.

Mitsubishi Corporation (SM case): dropped fixed 3-year "midterm management plan" for a long-term circa-2020 growth vision ("New Strategic Direction"); aim — stable earnings through business cycles by a portfolio diversified by business model, industry, market and geography; "Three Corporate Principles". Summary:

  • Future pull approach eyeing 2020 — double the business with a diversified but focussed portfolio;
  • Clear portfolio strategy — select winning businesses by proactive reshaping;
  • Grow and deliver returns while keeping financial discipline.

6.2 External Funding Requirement (EFR) Asked: Jan 2026 Q2(b) 4m

  • Growth needs both current and non-current assets to rise. When management also has concerns on Debt-to-Equity or Current Ratio, compute EFR and its types (short-term, long-term, equity).

6.2.1 Analytical Approach

  • Analyse each balance-sheet item in relation to change in sales, and fix amounts under prescribed conditions (specific D/E, current ratio etc.).

6.2.2 Formula based Approach

FormulaTerms / logicSM figures (Sundry Ltd)
EFR = (TA ÷ Sales) × ∆S − (Payables and Liabilities ÷ Sales) × ∆S − m × S1 × (1 − d)TA = total assets; ∆S = change in sales; S1 = revised sales; Payables and Liabilities = those that change spontaneously with sales; m = net profit after tax %; d = payout. Logic: extra assets needed − spontaneous liabilities − retained profit.(14000 ÷ 6000 − 2000 ÷ 6000) × 1200 − 0.04 × 7200 × 0.5 = ₹ 2256 lakhs
Then: split EFR by equations from given conditionsShort-term → ratio condition; long-term → FA / LTL; balance → equityNil; 800; 1456

Illus 1 (Sundry Ltd, ₹ lakhs): BS 31-03-2023: Share capital 3000, Reserves 2000, LTL 4000, STL 3000, Payables & provisions 2000 = 14000; FA 6000, Inventory 5000, Receivables 2400, Cash 600. Sales 6000, growth 20%, margin 4%, payout 50%. Conditions: Sales : (STL + payables & provisions) = 4 : 3; FA ÷ LTL = 1.5; D/E ≤ 1.5.

(i) EFR (analytical): sales 7200; profit 288; dividend 144; ploughed back (A) 144; additional funds (14000 − 2000) × 0.20 (B) 2400 (payables rise with sales); EFR = B − A = 2256.

(ii) Split: Short-term: new STL + payables = 3 × 7200 ÷ 4 = 5400; existing 2000 × 1.20 + 3000 = 5400 → Nil. Long-term: new FA 7200; new LTL 7200 ÷ 1.5 = 4800; less 4000 → 800. Equity: 2256 − 0 − 800 = 1456.

Formula approach (alt.): 6000 × 1.2 ÷ 4 = (2000 × 1.2 + X) ÷ 3 → X = 3000 (existing 3000 → 0 extra); 1.5 = 6000 × 1.2 ÷ Y → Y = 4800 (extra 800); equity 1456. Check: New DER = 4800 ÷ (3000 + 1456 + 2000 + 144) = 0.727 ≤ 1.5 → condition satisfied.

Note (app-written): ICAI's DER check takes only long-term loans (4800) as debt. Even with short-term loans (3000) added, debt = 7800 ÷ 6600 = 1.18, still within 1.5, so the conclusion stands.

Exam steps — EFR (both approaches): 1 → S1 = S × (1 + g), ∆S = S1 − S → 2 → retained profit = m × S1 × (1 − d) → 3 → asset increase = (TA − spontaneous liabilities) × g (or (TA ÷ S − P ÷ S) × ∆S) → 4 → EFR = 3 − 2 → 5 → short-term from ratio condition, long-term from FA ÷ LTL, balance = equity → 6 → check D/E (and current ratio if given).

App-written: if the question says fixed assets have spare capacity (as in Jan 2026), grow only the assets that must grow with sales before step 3.

Exam steps — SGR theory: 1 → SGR = ROE × (1 − d), growth without new equity or more leverage → 2 → target growth > SGR + loan request = too much debt, too little equity → 3 → two alternatives: raise additional equity, or cut expansion to SGR level (mature firm below SGR: dividends/buyback, repay debt, liquid assets, acquisitions).
