Asked in last 3 papers: CAPM + constant growth (Sep 25, 6m) · bond refunding NPV (Sep 25, 6m) · FCFF/FCFE (Sep 25, 7m) · repo legs MCQs (Jan 26, 6m) · H-model (May 26, 4m) · two-stage FCFE + implied growth (May 26, 5m).

1. Overview of Valuation

  • Investment = commitment of funds to earn a return that pays for (i) time funds are locked, (ii) expected inflation, (iii) risk.
  • Estimate intrinsic value and compare with market price. Returns may be earnings, cash flows, dividends, interest, interest on interest, capital gains.
  • Same core principles for all assets (real or financial); only details and uncertainty differ.

2.1–2.2 Required Rate of Return; Discount Rate

  • Required return = minimum return expected over a period = opportunity cost / cost of capital (highest return forgone on similar-risk investment). Often used interchangeably with expected return.
  • Discount rate = rate to find PV of future cash flows = risk-free rate + risk premium. Each cash flow can have its own rate (inflation, maturity, default) — term structure: upward slope = longer maturity, more inflation/liquidity/default risk.
  • Many rates can be replaced by one equivalent single rate giving the same PV.

SM Example (2.2): CF ₹100, 200, 300, 400, 500 at 2.0%, 3.2%, 3.6%, 4.8%, 5.0% → PV ₹1,278.99; single equivalent rate 4.4861%.

2.3 Internal Rate of Return

  • IRR = rate equating PV of future cash flows to market price. = average annual return, assuming reinvestment at the IRR.
  • Reinvest below IRR → realised return < IRR. Realised return = (Total cash at end ÷ Investment)1/n − 1 (app-written general form of the SM working).

SM Example (2.3): −20,000; +3,000 (Y1); +23,000 (Y2) → IRR 15%. Reinvest 3,000 at 10% → total 26,300 → (26300/20000)1/2 − 1 = 14.67%. Reinvest at 15% → 26,450 → exactly 15%.

3–3.1 Equity Risk Premium; Explanation

  • ERP = excess return of equity over a risk-free rate (e.g. tax-free government bonds) — compensation for higher risk; based on risk-reward trade-off.
  • Changes with portfolio risk and over time; theoretical — only estimated from past stock and bond market data; varies with time frame and method.

SM Example (3.1): Govt bond 7%, stock 15% → ERP 8%.

4. Required Return on Equity Asked: Sep 2025 Q2(a) 6m

  • Investors judge an asset by its contribution to systematic risk (not diversifiable). CAPM = relatively objective estimate; widely used.
  • Return on share A = Rf + β × Market risk premium.
  • β from data (used in TYK P17): β = Cov(security, market) ÷ Variance of market.

SM Example (4): Rf 5%, β 1.5, MRP 4.5% → 0.05 + 1.5(0.045) = 11.75%.

5–5.1 Discount Rate Selection; Nominal vs Real Cash Flow

  • Cash flows to equity (after debt claims and taxes) → discount at required return on equity. Cash flows to all stakeholders → cost of capital.
  • Nominal cash flow: no inflation adjustment. Real cash flow: adjusted for inflation. Low inflation/short term → almost same; high inflation → nominal > real.

5.2 Discount rate selection in Equity Valuation

  • Nominal CF ↔ nominal rate; real CF ↔ real rate.
  • Equity valuation uses only nominal cash flows and nominal rate — tax on corporate earnings is stated in nominal terms, so it reflects taxes accurately.
  • CF to equity → nominal cost of equity; CF to all capital providers → nominal after-tax WACC.

6.3–6.3.1 Cash Flow Based Models; FCFF Asked: Sep 2025 Q6(a) 7m

  • DDM ignores cash needed for long-term capex and short-term working capital → not the true free cash flow. Free cash flow models discount cash left after meeting these.
  • FCFF discounted at cost of capital (Ko) → value of the firm. FCFE discounted at Ke → value of equity (like DDM).
FCFF fromFormula
(a) Net incomeNI + Interest(1 − t) + Depreciation ∓ Capex ∓ ΔNon-cash NWC
(b) EBITEBIT(1 − t) + Depreciation ∓ Capex ∓ ΔNon-cash NWC
(c) EBITDAEBITDA(1 − t) + Depreciation × t ∓ Capex ∓ ΔNon-cash NWC (Dep × t = tax shield on depreciation)
(d) FCFEFCFE + Interest(1 − t) + Principal repaid − New debt issued + Preferred dividend (add back what went to/came from non-equity providers)
(e) Cash flowsCFO + Interest(1 − t) ∓ Capex (US GAAP; if interest already adjusted in CFO, do not add again)
  • Capex (one year) = Purchase of fixed assets − Sale of fixed assets (investing activities).
  • ΔNon-cash WC: (1) WC = CA − CL; (2) Non-cash NWC = CA − Cash & bank − CL; (3) same for previous year; (4) change = current − previous; (5) increase = more WC needed → deduct from FCFF.
  • Value of firm: one-stage = PV of stable-period FCFF; two-stage = PV explicit (or transition) period FCFF + PV stable period FCFF; three-stage = PV explicit + PV transition + PV stable.

6.3.2 Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE) Asked: Sep 2025 Q6(a) 7m Asked: May 2026 Q5(a) 5m

  • Cash for equity holders after operating expenses, interest, net debt obligations and reinvestment (WC, capex).
  • FCFE = Net income ∓ Capex + Depreciation ∓ ΔNon-cash NWC + New debt issued − Debt repayments + Net issue of preference shares − Preference dividends.
  • Or FCFE = Net profit + Dep − ΔNWC − Capex + New debt − Debt repayment + Net pref issue − Pref dividend (Capex = addition to fixed assets to sustain the base).
  • FCFE can be valued by multistage growth (two-stage, three-stage, H-model) exactly like dividends.

6.5–6.5.2 Enterprise Value; EV/Sales; EV/EBITDA

  • EV = Market capitalisation + Long-term debt + Minority interest − Cash & cash equivalents (also minus equity investments in affiliates/other companies and long-term investments). = true economic value.
  • Total EV = Market cap + interest-bearing debt + minority interest − cash. Operating EV = Total EV − market value of non-operating assets (investments, shares in associates). Core EV = Operating EV − non-core assets.
  • EV considers debt and equity, least affected by capital structure; EV multiples more reliable than equity multiples (which see only equity claim). Denominator must represent claims of all claimholders.
  • EV/Sales: for firms with negative cash flows/earnings (cyclical), technology firms; sales least manipulable, least affected by accounting policy.
  • EV/EBITDA: EBITDA = proxy of cash flow to debt + equity; for capital-intensive firms (high D&A), acquisitions; unaffected by depreciation policy and capital structure. Inverse = cash return on total investment.

8.9 Immunization

  • Rate rise → reinvestment income ↑ but bond value ↓ (and vice versa). Interest rate risk = price risk + reinvestment risk, moving in opposite directions.
  • Immunization: choose bonds so the two offset; portfolio value at end of holding period insensitive to rate changes. Duration = holding period → immunized.
  • Two bonds (TYK P27 answer): Required duration = x1 × Duration of X + (1 − x1) × Duration of Y, x1 = proportion in Bond X.

8.10 Yield Curve

  • Spot rate = rate for a zero-coupon bond of a given maturity (time value of a single future payment). ZCB price = 10,000 ÷ (1.124) = 8,897 for year 1 (SM: 8,897 = 10,000/(1 + r1)).
  • Yield curve (term structure) = YTM vs term to maturity; may slope up (long rates > short), down, flat or humped (four patterns).
  • Forward rate (f notation, to distinguish from spot r): f2 = rate for the next one year after one year. Solve bond price with r1, f2, f3… or from the spot curve as in SM: f2 = (1.1318)2/(1.124) − 1; f3 = (1.1340)3/(1.1318)2 − 1.
  • Equivalent ways to value riskless cash flows: discount at the YTM, PV[CF(t)] = CF(t)/(1 + Rt)t; or by the product of spot plus forward rates, PV[CF(t)] = CF(t)/[(1 + r1)(1 + r2)…(1 + rt)] (as printed in SM). Future spot rates are not known.

8.11 Term Structure Theories

  • Expectation theory: long-term rates are unbiased estimators of future short-term rates.
  • Liquidity preference theory: investors risk-averse; long bonds carry more interest rate risk and sacrifice liquidity → need premium → normal curve positively sloped.
  • Preferred habitat (market segmentation) theory: each investor has a preferred maturity; rates set by demand/supply in each segment; mismatch → higher rate needed to pull players out → curve may slope up, fall or be flat.

8.15 Zero Coupon Bond

  • No coupon; issued at a discount to face value; one lump sum (face) at maturity = investment + accrued interest.
  • Usually long-term → long-range planning. Issued by banks, government, private companies; corporate ZCBs riskier (issuer strength, long maturity) but higher return. (TYK T2.)

8.16 Refunding of Bonds Asked: Sep 2025 Q3(a) 6m

  • Issuer refunds before maturity when rates fall: new low-coupon bonds replace old high-coupon bonds; needs a call feature; cost = call premium (repay above face value).
  • Capital budgeting (NPV) decision: refund if PV of net cash savings > PV of outflows (call premium, interest in transition period etc.). New bonds usually issued before old are refunded → overlap interest on both.

8.17 Money Market Instruments

  • Short-term finance for industry, trade, government; outlet for banks' surplus funds.
  • Features: (a) short duration; (b) large volume; (c) de-regulated interest rates; (d) highly liquid; (e) safe (issuer strength).
  • Traditional: call and notice money, T-bills, commercial bills; new instruments give a wider choice to park funds even for a day.

8.17.1–8.17.2 Call/Notice Money; Treasury Bills

  • Call money: inter-bank, scheduled commercial banks lend/borrow overnight (1 day); notice money: 2 to 14 days — for day-to-day surpluses/deficits.
  • T-Bills: short-term promissory notes of GoI issued at a discount; interest = discount; assured yield, negligible default risk. At present RBI issues 91, 182, 364 days (earlier 14, 28, 91, 364 days on auction).

8.17.3–8.17.5 Commercial Bills; Certificate of Deposit; Commercial Paper

  • Commercial bill: arises from a genuine credit trade transaction; seller draws on buyer, buyer accepts; self-liquidating, negotiable; 3–6 months. Advantages: self-liquidating, recourse to two parties, exact date known, transparency.
  • CD: negotiable term deposit of commercial banks from bulk depositors at market rates; Demat or usance promissory note; front-ended, issued at discount, face value at maturity.
  • CP: unsecured negotiable promissory note of high-rated corporates for working capital, directly from market; 7 days to 1 year; at discount; yield market-determined; introduced Jan 1990 (Vaghul Committee); by-passes banks (securitisation); partly replaces bank WC limits — no net increase in borrowing.

9.1 Role of Valuers

  • Mergers/acquisitions/de-mergers/takeovers: Income Tax Act, 1961 (tax payable).
  • Slump sale/asset sale/IPR sale: IBC, 2016 — liquidation, sale of corporate debtor's assets (fair/liquidation value).
  • Conversion of debt/security: RBI — inbound/outbound foreign investment.
  • Capital reduction: SEBI ICDR/LODR/preferential allotment — listed securities, periodically.
  • Strategic financial restructuring: Companies Act 2013, SARFAESI 2002, Arbitration and Conciliation Act 1996; also Ind AS and court orders.

9.2 Responsibilities of Valuers

Model Code of Conduct, Rule 12(e), Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017 — 29 points under 8 heads:

  • Integrity and fairness (1–4): high integrity; honest, straightforward, forthright; true and adequate information, no misrepresentation; nothing bringing disrepute.
  • Professional competence and due care (5–9): due diligence, independent judgment; follow technical/professional standards; keep knowledge up to date; do not disclaim liability (except facts supplied by the company); carry out client instructions consistent with integrity, objectivity, independence.
  • Independence and disclosure of interest (10–17): objectivity, no bias/conflict/coercion; no assignment if he or relatives/associates not independent; independent of external influence; disclose conflicts; no dealing in securities of subject company once aware (SEBI PIT Regulations, 2015); no mandate snatching or convenience valuations, write to prior valuer; no success fee; declare past association in fairness opinions.
  • Confidentiality (18): no divulging confidential information without authority or legal/professional duty.
  • Information management (19–22): written contemporaneous records of decisions and reasons; co-operate with inspections; provide records to Registration Authority, Tribunal, Appellate Tribunal, RVO, regulators; keep working papers 3 years (or till disposal of pending case).
  • Gifts and hospitality (23–24): no gifts affecting independence; no gifts/advantage to public servants to obtain work.
  • Remuneration and costs (25–26): transparent, reasonable fees; no undisclosed fees.
  • Occupation, employability and restrictions (27–29): not too many assignments; no employment unless certificate temporarily surrendered; no business inconsistent with the profession's reputation.

10. Precautions before accepting a Valuation Assignment

  • Not a precise estimate: many assumptions; quality ∝ time spent on data and understanding the firm.
  • More than financial statements: emerging businesses valued on narrative factors — scalability, ease of replication, growth potential, cross-sell.
  • Middle ground between numbers and story — focusing on only one misses opportunities.
  • Emotions move stock prices; ignoring this is an obstacle to getting valuation right.
