Conceptual Framework (Ind AS)

The "why" behind every Ind AS. Exam answers: define the element → apply the recognition test → name the qualitative characteristic.

1Status & purpose

  • Not an Ind AS — does not override any Ind AS. Where conflict, the Ind AS prevails
  • Helps standard-setters develop Ind AS; helps preparers develop policies when no Ind AS applies (Ind AS 8 para 11(b)); helps users understand

2Objective of general purpose financial reporting

Provide information useful to existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors in making decisions about providing resources — including assessing management's stewardship of the entity's economic resources.

Information about: economic resources & claims; changes in them (financial performance — accrual basis; cash flows); efficiency & effectiveness of management.

3Qualitative characteristics

Fundamental

Relevance — predictive value, confirmatory value; materiality (entity-specific)

Faithful representation — complete, neutral, free from error. Neutrality is supported by prudence = caution under uncertainty — no deliberate under / overstatement

Enhancing

Comparability (not uniformity) · Verifiability · Timeliness · Understandability

Enhancing can't make useless info useful. Applied after fundamental ones.

Cost constraint — benefits must justify cost

  • Substance over form — faithful representation of the economic phenomenon, not just legal form
  • Measurement uncertainty doesn't stop information from being useful — but high uncertainty can affect relevance vs faithful representation trade-off

4Going concern & reporting entity

  • Underlying assumption: going concern; if not, a different basis + disclose
  • Reporting entity: one entity, part of one, or several — consolidated, unconsolidated or combined FS

5The elements

ElementDefinition
AssetA present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events. Economic resource = a right with the potential to produce economic benefits
LiabilityA present obligation to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events. Obligation = duty the entity has no practical ability to avoid
EquityResidual interest in assets after deducting liabilities
IncomeIncreases in assets / decreases in liabilities → increase in equity, other than contributions from equity holders
ExpensesDecreases in assets / increases in liabilities → decrease in equity, other than distributions to equity holders

The asset definition needs only potential to produce benefits — not "expected / probable" inflows. Probability now sits in recognition (relevance) and measurement, not in the definition.

6Recognition & derecognition

  • Recognise an item meeting an element definition only if it gives relevant information and a faithful representation — no fixed probability threshold
  • Recognition may not give relevant info if: existence uncertainty, or low probability of inflows / outflows
  • May not give faithful representation if measurement uncertainty is very high
  • Derecognition: when the entity loses control of the asset / no longer has the present obligation
  • Unit of account chosen to give relevant, faithful info (e.g., individual right vs group)
  • Executory contracts (equally unperformed) → combined right & obligation; recognised if onerous

7Measurement bases

BasisIncludes
Historical costCost incl. transaction costs; amortised cost for financial items
Current valueFair value (market participant, exit price) · Value in use (assets) / Fulfilment value (liabilities) — entity-specific · Current cost (entry value)

8Presentation & disclosure

  • Statement of profit or loss is the primary source of performance information; income / expense go to P&L by default
  • OCI only in exceptional cases, when a standard requires (e.g., current value changes that make P&L less relevant)
  • Rebuttable presumption that OCI items are recycled later — unless no clear basis (hence revaluation surplus, DB remeasurement aren't)
  • Classification, aggregation, offsetting — communicate effectively

9Capital & capital maintenance

ConceptProfit exists only if…
Financial capital (nominal money)Net assets at end (money) exceed net assets at start, after owner transactions
Financial capital (constant purchasing power)Increase exceeds the general price-level increase
Physical capital (operating capability)Productive capacity at end exceeds that at start (current cost basis)
