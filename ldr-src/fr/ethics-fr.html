Ethics — CA in Business (FR Ch 16)

FR asks ethics through an accounting scenario. Score = Ind AS treatment + principle breached + what the CA should do.

1Five fundamental principles

PrincipleMeans for a CA preparing FS
IntegrityStraightforward, honest; not associated with false / misleading information or omissions
ObjectivityNo bias, conflict of interest or undue influence overriding judgement
Professional competence & due careKeep knowledge current; act diligently per Ind AS; don't undertake beyond expertise
ConfidentialityDon't disclose / use information without authority unless legal / professional duty
Professional behaviourComply with laws; avoid conduct that discredits the profession

2Conceptual framework approach

Identify threats → Evaluate (acceptable level? "reasonable & informed third party" test) → Address (eliminate circumstances, apply safeguards, or decline / end the activity)

3The five threats — spot them

ThreatTypical FR scenario
Self-interestCFO's bonus / ESOPs tied to profit or share price; loan from entity; fear of losing job
Self-reviewReviewing a business case / valuation you prepared
AdvocacyPromoting the entity's position so strongly that objectivity suffers (e.g., to a bank)
FamiliarityClose relative is a key supplier / customer; long relationship with a director
IntimidationDirectors threaten dismissal / demotion unless numbers are "managed"

4Preparing & presenting information

  • Prepare per the applicable framework; don't use discretion to mislead — e.g., pick estimates, timing of transactions, structuring to achieve an outcome, or selective disclosure
  • Relying on others' work (internal / external) → consider their reputation, expertise, resources; be satisfied it's reasonable
  • Information is or might be misleading → consult superior / TCWG; insist on correction; consider policies, legal advice; refuse to be associated; consider resigning

5Pressure to breach principles

  • Don't allow pressure from others to make you breach, and don't pressure others
  • Respond: discuss with person exerting pressure → escalate to superior / audit committee / TCWG → use whistle-blower / ethics channel → legal advice → disassociate

6Financial interests, inducements, competence

  • Incentives linked to reported numbers → self-interest; safeguards: independent review by audit committee, remuneration policy
  • Inducements (gifts, hospitality) intended to improperly influence → refuse; inform superior
  • Lack of expertise / time / resources → obtain training / help, or decline

7NOCLAR — non-compliance with laws & regulations

CAResponse
Senior CA in business (CFO, director)Understand the matter; discuss with superior / TCWG; take steps to rectify, remediate, mitigate, deter; disclose to external auditor; determine further action — may disclose to authority, may resign; document
Other CA in businessUnderstand; inform immediate superior (or next level if superior involved); may consider further action

Confidentiality does not prevent disclosure where the Code / law permits. Check the Code edition and NOCLAR scope prescribed for your attempt.

8CA Act misconduct links

  • Professional / other misconduct under the First & Second Schedules applies to members in service too — e.g., false / misleading statements, gross negligence, disclosing confidential information, conduct bringing disrepute

10The 3-part answer template

1. Accounting position — what the Ind AS requires and why the proposed treatment is wrong (cite para).

2. Ethical issue — which principles are threatened and which threats exist (self-interest, intimidation…).

3. Action — discuss with management → escalate to audit committee / TCWG → refuse to be associated with misleading info → legal advice / whistle-blower → resign if unresolved; document.

Never stop at "it's unethical" — fix the accounting first, then name the principle, then the action.

"The proposal to record revenue on goods shipped without confirmed orders and with a right of return contravenes Ind AS 115, as control has not transferred. As a Chartered Accountant, the CFO must comply with the fundamental principles of integrity and objectivity and must not be associated with information that is materially false or misleading. He should raise the matter with the Board / Audit Committee, insist on correction, and if unresolved, consider legal advice and disassociating himself."
