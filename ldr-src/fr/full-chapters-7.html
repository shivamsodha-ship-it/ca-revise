Ind AS 33 — Earnings per Share

Asked every paper: Sep 2025 Q3(b) 6 marks (basic + diluted with options and warrants), Jan 2026 MCQ (CCDs issued in the year), May 2026 MCQ (diluted with 10% convertible bonds, key 2.25).

1Objective, scope, definitions 2.1–2.4

  • Objective: principles for determining and presenting EPS to improve comparison between entities and periods. Limitation (2.2.1): different accounting policies make EPS less comparable — so focus is on the denominator.
  • Scope: separate/individual FS of an entity whose ordinary shares are traded (or being issued) in a public market, and CFS of a group whose parent is such an entity. Voluntary EPS must follow Ind AS 33. When both separate and CFS are presented, EPS needs to be shown only on the consolidated information (separate-FS EPS only in separate FS).
  • Ordinary share = equity instrument subordinate to all other classes. Potential ordinary share = instrument that may entitle the holder to ordinary shares: convertible debt/preference shares (Example 1: preference shares convertible in 2 years), options and warrants, ESOPs (Example 3), contingently issuable shares (Example 4: more shares if price falls). Dilution = reduction in EPS (or increase in loss per share) on assumed conversion; antidilution = the opposite.

2Basic EPS 2.6

Basic EPS = profit or loss attributable to ordinary equity holders of the parent ÷ weighted average number of ordinary shares outstanding — for profit from continuing operations and for total profit.

3Diluted EPS 2.7

Diluted EPS = (basic earnings + after-tax effect of dilutive potential shares) ÷ (basic weighted shares + weighted dilutive potential shares). Potential shares deemed converted at the start of the period or the date of issue if later; if lapsed/cancelled during the year, only for the period outstanding; if converted during the year, up to the conversion date (then in basic).

4Retrospective adjustment, presentation, disclosure, two-class 2.8–2.11

  • Retrospective adjustment: bonus/split/consolidation (or rights bonus element) after the year-end but before approval → EPS of all periods presented based on new number; disclose. Prior-period errors and policy changes → restate EPS. Diluted EPS of prior periods not restated for changes in assumptions or conversions.
  • Presentation: basic and diluted EPS (continuing and total) on the face of the statement of P&L, for each class with a different right to profit, equal prominence, even if negative; discontinued operations EPS in P&L or notes.
  • Disclosure: earnings used and reconciliation to profit; weighted shares and reconciliation basic to diluted; antidilutive instruments excluded; share transactions after the year-end that would have changed EPS significantly.
  • Two-class method (participating preference shares, 2.11.1): allocate profit to dividends declared, then undistributed profit by participation rights (Illus 15: preference 7.20, ordinary 6.40 per share).
  • Ind AS 33 vs AS 20: Ind AS needs EPS on CFS only when CFS presented; options/ESOP rules more detailed; written put options; etc.
Exam layout: (1) Basic: profit to equity − pref dividend; weighted shares table (date, shares, months/12, bonus/rights factor). (2) Diluted: list each potential share → earnings increment and share increment → earnings per incremental share → rank → add one by one while EPS falls. (3) State answers for each year presented, restated.

FR · Full chapter notes · Ind AS 108 Operating SegmentsSM Ch 8 Unit 3

108Ind AS 108 — Operating Segments

3.1–3.2 Core principle and scope

  • Core principle: disclose information that lets users evaluate the nature and financial effects of the business activities and the economic environments the entity operates in — using the management approach (what the CODM sees).
  • Scope: separate/individual FS and CFS of entities whose debt or equity is traded in a public market or that are filing to issue in one. If CFS and separate FS are in one report → segment information only for CFS. Voluntary disclosure called "segment information" must comply.

3.3 Operating segment

  • Component (a) engaging in business activities that earn revenue and incur expenses (including with other components of the same entity); (b) whose operating results are regularly reviewed by the CODM to allocate resources and assess performance; (c) for which discrete financial information is available. Start-ups without revenue can be segments.
  • Not segments: corporate headquarters / functional departments that earn only incidental revenue; post-employment benefit plans (Illus 1). Functions integral to the business (3.3.1) — e.g. R&D serving segments — not segments unless they earn revenue and are reviewed separately. Discontinued operations (3.3.2) may still be a segment if reviewed.
  • CODM is a function, not a title (CEO, COO, board, committee). Where multiple sets of information are reviewed (Illus 2, 4): consider which components the CODM uses, nature of activities, existence of segment managers, information presented to the board; matrix structures → management's judgement (Illus 3). Information reviewed may be revenue only (Illus 2).
  • Segment manager — accountable to the CODM; one manager may manage several segments.

3.7–3.9 Disclosure, measurement, restatement

  • General information: factors used to identify segments (products, geography, regulatory, mix; whether aggregated) and types of products/services of each.
  • Profit or loss, assets, liabilities: a measure of profit or loss for each reportable segment; total assets and liabilities if regularly provided to the CODM; and if included in the measure reviewed: external revenue, inter-segment revenue, interest revenue, interest expense (net only if majority of revenue is interest and CODM uses net), depreciation and amortisation, material income/expense items, share of associates/JVs, income tax, other material non-cash items; investment in associates and additions to non-current assets.
  • Measurement (3.8): amounts as reported to the CODM; allocations only on a reasonable basis (Illus 10: common costs by usage, time, property value, salaries); if CODM uses more than one measure → the one closest to Ind AS. Explain bases of accounting for inter-segment transactions, differences from entity measurement (TYK 3: FIFO internally vs weighted average — disclose and reconcile), asymmetric allocations.
  • Reconciliations (3.8.1): totals of reportable segments' revenue, profit or loss (before tax and discontinued ops unless CODM uses after), assets, liabilities and other material items → entity totals; all material reconciling items separately identified.
  • Restatement (3.9): change in internal structure that changes segment composition → restate comparatives (incl. interim) unless information unavailable and cost excessive; disclose.

3.10 Entity-wide disclosures (even with one segment)

  • Products and services: external revenue per product/service group.
  • Geographical areas: external revenue and non-current assets (excluding financial instruments, deferred tax, post-employment assets) for India and all foreign countries in total, and material individual countries.
  • Major customers: revenue from a single external customer ≥ 10% of entity revenue → disclose the fact, total amount and segment(s) (identity not required). A group under common control (incl. government-controlled entities, by judgement) = single customer.

TYK 2 (coating industry): prepare the segment report — segment revenue, results, assets, liabilities, capital expenditure, depreciation, reconciliation to entity totals; comparatives needed in practice. Ind AS 108 vs AS 17: management approach vs risks-and-returns approach; no primary/secondary format; CODM concept.

FR · Full chapter notes · Ind AS 113 Fair Value MeasurementSM Ch 4 Unit 3

113Ind AS 113 — Fair Value Measurement

Asked: Jan 2026 Q6(c) 4 marks (market + income approach, discounts), May 2026 MCQ (highest and best use of vacant land) and Q6(c) 4 marks (principal vs most advantageous market).

3.1–3.4 What, objective, scope, definition

  • Fair value = price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date — an exit price, market-based, not entity-specific. A liability is transferred (not settled — Example 1: debt with face 1,00,000, market value 95,000).
  • Ind AS 113 defines FV, sets one framework and requires disclosures — it doesn't say when to use FV (other Ind AS do).
  • Out of scope (measurement and disclosure): share-based payments (Ind AS 102), leasing (Ind AS 116), measures similar to FV but not FV — NRV (Ind AS 2), value in use (Ind AS 36). Disclosure-only exclusions: plan assets (Ind AS 19), retirement benefit plan investments, assets whose recoverable amount is FV less costs of disposal (Ind AS 36).

3.5–3.6 The asset or liability; unit of account

  • Consider characteristics a market participant would: condition and location, restrictions on sale or use that attach to the asset (land legally restricted for 10 years — Example 9; car bought for private use with restriction — Example 3). Restrictions specific to the entity are ignored (Example 2: entity's own business objective).
  • Unit of account — set by the relevant Ind AS (single asset, CGU, business). No blockage factor for large holdings.

3.7–3.9 The transaction, participants, the price

ConceptRule
Principal marketMarket with the greatest volume and level of activity for the asset/liability that the entity can access. Use its price even if another market gives a better price (Example 7, 18: BSE vs NYSE — BSE if most activity). Presumed to be the market the entity normally transacts in.
Most advantageous marketOnly if no principal market: the market that maximises the amount received (asset) after transaction costs and transport costs. Export cap (Example 8: diamonds) — consider access.
Market participantsIndependent, knowledgeable, able and willing to transact; entity needn't identify specific ones — assumptions they would use.
Transaction costsNot part of FV (entity-specific) — used only to choose the most advantageous market.
Transport costsDeducted if location is a characteristic of the asset (commodities) (Example 10).

TYK 1: Market A and B, no principal market → choose the market giving the higher net (price − transaction − transport); B nets 22 → FV = 25 − transport 2 = 23. May 2026 Q6(c): principal market C → FV = 88 − 6 transport = 82; otherwise most advantageous (D, net 76). Illus 1: bond — principal market price 500 (not 600 "HBU" or NPV).

3.10 Non-financial assets — highest and best use, valuation premise

  • HBU = use by market participants that maximises value: physically possible, legally permissible, financially feasible. Current use presumed HBU unless market factors suggest otherwise. Applies even if the entity intends a different use (TYK 2: factory land vs residential development; May 2026 MCQ: vacant land used as parking — value on HBU).
  • Valuation premise: stand-alone, or in combination with other assets (then assume the other assets are available to participants). Defensive value: asset acquired to keep it from competitors still valued at HBU by participants.

3.11–3.13 Liabilities, own equity, financial assets, initial recognition

  • Liabilities and own equity: assume transfer to a participant, liability remains outstanding. If held by another party as an asset → use the holder's view (quoted price of the identical item held as asset; else other observable; else income approach). If not held as an asset (decommissioning) → value from the view of a participant that would take it on: expected cash outflows + compensation for risk (risk premium) + profit margin, discounted (PQ 3: 194.97).
  • Non-performance risk (incl. own credit) included and assumed same before and after transfer. No separate adjustment for transfer restrictions.
  • Portfolio exception: groups of financial assets and liabilities managed on net market/credit risk exposure may be measured on the net position.
  • Initial recognition: transaction price (entry) usually = FV (exit), but not when: related party, forced/duress sale, unit of account differs (bundle), market differs. Day-1 gain/loss as the relevant Ind AS says (Ind AS 109 B5.1.2A).

3.14 Valuation techniques

ApproachHow

  • Use techniques appropriate and with sufficient data; maximise observable, minimise unobservable inputs; may use several and weight them (Jan 2026 / PQ 1: market and income approaches weighted equally → enterprise value → − debt + surplus cash → equity value per share; PQ 4: maintainable earnings × P/E → ₹ 170 per share; TYK 4: DCF → ₹ 204.83). FR · Full chapter notes · Ind AS 41 AgricultureSM Ch 10 Unit 1

41Ind AS 41 — Agriculture

1.1–1.3 Objective, scope, definitions

  • Prescribes accounting for agricultural activity — management by an entity of the biological transformation and harvest of biological assets for sale or for conversion into agricultural produce or additional biological assets.
  • Applies to: biological assets (except bearer plants); agricultural produce at the point of harvest; government grants on biological assets measured at FV less costs to sell.
  • Does not apply to: land (Ind AS 16/40); bearer plants (Ind AS 16 — but their produce is in Ind AS 41); grants on bearer plants (Ind AS 20); intangibles (Ind AS 38); ROU assets from leases of land with biological assets (Ind AS 116); produce after harvest (Ind AS 2) — e.g. grapes → wine (Illus 1).
  • Biological transformation = growth, degeneration, production, procreation. Bearer plant = living plant used in production of produce, expected to bear for more than one period, remote likelihood of sale as produce (except incidental scrap) — tea bushes, vines, fruit trees, coconut palms (PQ 3). Harvest = detachment of produce or cessation of life.
  • Activities: raising livestock, forestry, annual/perennial cropping, orchards, floriculture, aquaculture incl. fish farming. Not: ocean fishing, catching fish from ponds without managing growth (PQ 2), zoo animals for recreation, deforestation of unmanaged forests (TYK 5). Leasing orchards and managing them yourself = agricultural activity (PQ 1).

1.7 Government grants

  • Unconditional grant on a biological asset at FV less costs to sell → P&L when it becomes receivable.
  • Conditional (incl. requiring the entity not to engage in an activity) → P&L only when conditions are met (Example 4: 10 cr for 5 years' cultivation, refundable if less → recognise after 5 years; if the grant is earned year by year, recognise year by year).
  • Biological asset measured at cost (FV unreliable), or grants on bearer plants / equipment → Ind AS 20 (Illus 4: incubator for poultry → reduce asset cost or deferred income over 3-year life).

1.8 Disclosure

  • Aggregate gain/loss for the period on initial recognition and change in FV less costs to sell; description of each group (consumable vs bearer; mature vs immature); reconciliation of carrying amount (purchases, sales, harvest, FV changes, business combinations, FX); restrictions/pledges; commitments; financial risk management; if at cost — why FV not reliable, range of estimates, depreciation method; grants.

Illus 5 (Moon Ltd): land (Ind AS 16), 200 dairy cows at FV less costs (Ind AS 41), milk at harvest → inventory at 72,000. PQ 3 (coconut palms): palms are bearer plants (Ind AS 16); coconuts growing on the palms are produce measured at FV less costs → total ₹ 231 crore.

1.8 DB plans — the balance sheet

  • Payment-basis accounting for gratuity with a disclosure is not Ind AS-compliant — disclosure doesn't cure non-compliance (Illus 12).
  • Net defined benefit liability (asset) = PV of DBO − FV of plan assets (deficit or surplus), with a surplus limited to the asset ceiling (PV of economic benefits as refunds or reductions in future contributions).

1.9 Measuring the DBO — PUCM, attribution, assumptions

  • Projected unit credit method: each year of service gives an additional unit of benefit; measure each unit separately; discount. Use projected (final) salary. Illus 15: 1% of final salary per year → 131 at end of year 5, PV each year 89, 98, 108, 119, 131 at 10%.
  • Attribution: to periods of service under the plan's benefit formula, but on a straight-line basis if later years give a materially higher level of benefit (back-loaded). From the date service first leads to benefits until further service leads to no material further benefit.

1.10 Plan assets and reimbursements

  • Plan assets = assets held by a long-term employee benefit fund or qualifying insurance policies, not available to the entity's creditors; at FV; exclude unpaid contributions and non-transferable entity-issued instruments.
  • Reimbursement right (insurer will pay part of the obligation) — not a plan asset → separate asset at FV; treated like plan assets in P&L/OCI.

1.12–1.13 Presentation and disclosure

  • Offset an asset of one plan against a liability of another only with a legal right and intention to settle net. Current/non-current split not required by Ind AS 19 (Ind AS 1 governs). Components may be presented in P&L as the entity chooses (service cost in employee cost, net interest in finance cost) — remeasurements in OCI.
  • Disclose: characteristics and risks of plans; reconciliation of opening to closing net liability (DBO, plan assets, ceiling) showing each component; plan assets by class (quoted/unquoted); significant actuarial assumptions and sensitivity analysis; funding arrangements, expected contributions next year, maturity profile (weighted average duration).

1.14 Other long-term benefits

  • Same measurement as DB (PUCM, discounting) but simpler: service cost, net interest and remeasurements all in P&L (no OCI). Long-term disability: if level depends on service → accrue as service given; if not → recognise when the event occurs.

1.15 Termination benefits

  • Recognise at the earlier of: (a) when the entity can no longer withdraw the offer (employee accepts; or a restriction prevents withdrawal) and (b) when it recognises restructuring costs under Ind AS 37 that involve termination benefits.
  • Measure per the nature: settled wholly within 12 months → short-term rules; otherwise → other long-term rules. Voluntary offer → based on number expected to accept.
  • Benefits conditional on future service are not termination benefits (Example 4: factory closing in 10 months; employees who stay to closure get 30,000, leavers get 10,000 → 10,000 = termination benefit recognised on announcement; extra 20,000 = short-term benefit for service, spread over the 10 months).

1.16 Appendix — limit on DB asset (asset ceiling)

  • Refund available if the entity has an unconditional right to it (during plan life, on gradual settlement, or on wind-up), measured as surplus less costs. Reduction in future contributions = future service cost less any minimum funding contributions. Minimum funding requirement for past service that won't be available as a refund/reduction once paid → recognise a liability.
Exam frame: classify (short-term / DC / DB / other long-term / termination) → for DB build the two reconciliations → P&L = CSC + PSC ± settlement + net interest → OCI = balancing figures → net liability = closing DBO − closing assets (cap a surplus at the ceiling) → entry.

36Ind AS 36 — Impairment of Assets

Asked: Sep 2025 5 marks (machine, revaluation surplus), Jan 2026 MCQ, May 2026 MCQ (CGUs + corporate asset).

4.1–4.3 Objective, scope, definitions

  • An asset may not be carried above its recoverable amount. Applies to PPE, ROU assets, investment property at cost, intangibles, goodwill, investments in subsidiaries/associates/JVs in separate FS (at cost).
  • Excluded (own rules): inventories (Ind AS 2), contract assets/costs (115), deferred tax assets (12), employee benefit assets (19), financial assets in Ind AS 109, investment property at FV, biological assets at FV less costs to sell (41), insurance contracts, non-current assets held for sale (105).
  • Recoverable amount = higher of FV less costs of disposal and value in use. CGU = smallest identifiable group of assets generating largely independent cash inflows. Corporate assets = assets other than goodwill that contribute to several CGUs (HQ, IT centre).

4.4–4.5 When to test

Any time in the year but the same time each year; different CGUs may be tested at different times; goodwill acquired this year → test before year end (Example 1).
  • Materiality: if earlier calculations show a large headroom and nothing changed, no re-estimate needed. An indicator may also mean the useful life, depreciation method or residual value needs review even if no loss.

4.6–4.7 Recoverable amount; FV less costs of disposal

  • Not always necessary to compute both — if either exceeds carrying amount, no impairment. If FVLCD can't be measured, use VIU; if VIU is not materially above FVLCD (asset held for disposal), use FVLCD. Determined for the individual asset unless it doesn't generate independent inflows → its CGU.
  • FV per Ind AS 113. Costs of disposal = incremental costs directly attributable to disposal (legal costs, stamp duty, removal costs, costs to bring to saleable condition) — not finance costs, tax, or termination benefits/reorganisation costs.

CGUs

  • Identify the CGU consistently from period to period. If an active market exists for the output (even if used internally) → separate CGU, using market prices for the internal output.
  • SM cases: magazine titles → each title (Illus 14); private railway sold only as scrap → the mine (15); 7 bus routes required by contract → the company (16); plant X selling 60% intermediate to Y with no active market → X and Y together (17); plants 1–3 with production allocated between 2 and 3 → XYZ as a whole (18); HQ building with 1/3 vacant → corporate asset, CGU = Apex as a whole (TYK 1).

Allocating a CGU loss

  1. Reduce goodwill allocated to the CGU.
  2. Then other assets pro rata to their carrying amounts.
  3. No asset below the highest of its FVLCD, VIU (if determinable) and zero — the excess is reallocated pro rata to the others.

(1) No commitment to replace → recoverable amount of the machine can't be estimated alone → no loss (review depreciation). (2) Committed to replace and sell soon → VIU ≈ FVLCD → loss recognised on the machine.

Goodwill and NCI

4.10 Reversing an impairment loss

  • At each reporting date look for indicators that a loss has decreased (mirror of the impairment indicators). Reverse only if there has been a change in the estimates used to determine recoverable amount — not because of the passage of time ("unwinding of the discount") (TYK 5: no reversal of 4.69 cr).
  • Individual asset: increase to recoverable amount but not above the carrying amount (net of depreciation) that would have existed had no loss been recognised. Reversal → P&L (revalued asset → revaluation surplus via OCI to the extent the loss went to P&L earlier in P&L). Then revise depreciation.
  • CGU: allocate reversal to assets other than goodwill pro rata, each capped at the lower of recoverable amount and the no-impairment carrying amount.
  • Goodwill impairment is never reversed (any later increase would be internally generated goodwill — Ind AS 38 prohibits).

Illus 10: Venus / Saturn — carrying 3,68,000 vs VIU 2,12,000 → loss 1,56,000 = goodwill 80,000 + other assets 76,000; when the licence is restored, reversal limited to no-impairment carrying amount 2,56,000 → reversal 68,000 (2,56,000 − 1,88,000) to P&L; depreciation from 1 April 20X5 = 2,56,000 ÷ 16 = 16,000; goodwill not reinstated. PQ 4 (Elia): reversal limited to 62,266 (9,30,000 − 8,67,734) — Machinery A capped at its own recoverable amount 4,30,000, goodwill not restored.

4.11 Disclosure

  • For each class: impairment losses and reversals in P&L (line item) and in OCI. By reportable segment if Ind AS 108 applies. For each material loss/reversal: events and circumstances, amount, nature of asset/CGU, segment, whether recoverable amount is FVLCD (with Ind AS 113 level and technique) or VIU (with discount rate). Unallocated goodwill and why.
  • For CGUs with significant goodwill/indefinite-life intangibles: carrying amounts, basis of recoverable amount, key assumptions, period of projections, growth rate, discount rate, and sensitivity if a reasonably possible change would cause impairment.
Exam frame: indicator or annual test? → asset or CGU? → recoverable = higher of FVLCD and VIU (pre-tax rate, no restructuring/enhancement, no finance/tax) → loss = carrying − recoverable → revaluation surplus first, then P&L → CGU: goodwill first, then pro rata with floor → NCI gross-up if proportionate → revised depreciation and deferred tax → reversal cap (never goodwill).

34Ind AS 34 — Interim Financial Reporting

2.1–2.4 Objective, scope, definitions

  • Prescribes the minimum content of an interim financial report and the recognition and measurement principles for complete or condensed interim statements. Ind AS 34 does not mandate who must publish or how often — SEBI LODR (quarterly results) or other regulators do; it applies if an entity publishes an interim report under Ind AS.
  • Interim period = reporting period shorter than a full financial year. Interim financial report = complete (Ind AS 1) or condensed set for an interim period.

2.5 Content of an interim report

  • Minimum components: condensed balance sheet, condensed statement of P&L (with OCI), condensed SOCE, condensed cash flows, selected explanatory notes. Condensed statements show at least each heading and subtotal of the last annual FS; basic and diluted EPS on the face if Ind AS 33 applies.
  • Selected notes (assume the user has the last annual report — explain only significant events and transactions since then, year-to-date basis): same policies or nature of changes; seasonality/cyclicality comments; unusual items; changes in estimates; issues/repurchases of debt and equity; dividends paid; segment information (Ind AS 108 subset); events after the interim period; changes in composition (business combinations, loss of control, discontinued operations); FV disclosures for financial instruments; inventory write-downs, impairment losses and reversals, restructuring provisions, PPE acquisitions/disposals, litigation settlements, prior period errors, related party transactions, transfers between FV levels, contingent liabilities.

2.6 Disclosure in annual financial statements

  • If an estimate reported in an interim period changes significantly in the final interim period but no separate report is published for it, disclose the nature and amount of the change in a note to the annual FS.

2.7 Recognition and measurement

  • Same accounting policies as the annual FS (or the changed policies to be used in the next annual FS). Frequency of reporting must not affect annual results → measurement on a year-to-date basis. Each interim period is part of the annual period ("integral view" in tax and estimates) yet assets/liabilities must meet definitions at the interim date ("discrete view").
  • Seasonal, cyclical or occasional revenues — not anticipated or deferred at the interim date if not appropriate at year end (dividend income, royalties, government grants, retailers' seasonal sales) (Illus 3: ICPL can't defer 16 crore expenses to Q3 → Q1 profit 29 crore).
  • Costs incurred unevenly — anticipated or deferred only if appropriate at year end. Planned major maintenance, year-end bonus without obligation, contingent lease payments, discretionary charitable contributions → not accrued unless an obligation exists at the interim date.

2.9 Interim reporting and impairment (Appendix A)

  • An impairment loss on goodwill recognised in an interim period is not reversed in a later interim or annual period.
