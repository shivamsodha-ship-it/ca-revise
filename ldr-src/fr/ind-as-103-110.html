Ind AS 103 — Business Combinations

Part A: Ind AS 103 (§1–32) · Part B: Ind AS 110 control & rules (§33–42) · Part C: consolidation, stake changes, practice (§43–56)

1Key definitions Appendix A

Business combination — a transaction or other event in which an acquirer obtains control of one or more businesses. "True mergers" and "mergers of equals" are also business combinations. → Two tests: control obtained + what is acquired is a business.

Acquirer — the entity that obtains control of the acquiree. Acquiree — the business(es) the acquirer obtains control of. Acquisition date — the date on which the acquirer obtains control.

Contingent consideration — usually an obligation of the acquirer to transfer additional assets or equity to the former owners if specified future events occur or conditions are met; may also be a right to the return of consideration.

Goodwill — an asset representing future economic benefits from other assets acquired that are not individually identified and separately recognised.

Identifiable — an asset is identifiable if it (a) is separable (can be sold, transferred, licensed, rented or exchanged, alone or with a related item), or (b) arises from contractual or other legal rights, whether or not transferable or separable.

Non-controlling interest (NCI) — equity in a subsidiary not attributable, directly or indirectly, to the parent. Fair value — price to sell an asset / transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date (Ind AS 113).

2How control can be obtained B5–B6

Ways (B5)

  • transferring cash, cash equivalents or other assets (incl. net assets that form a business)
  • incurring liabilities
  • issuing equity interests
  • more than one type of consideration
  • without transferring consideration — incl. by contract alone (para 43)

Legal structures (B6)

  • one or more businesses become subsidiaries of the acquirer
  • one entity's net assets are legally merged into another
  • owners of one entity transfer their interests to another / its owners
  • all combining entities transfer to a newly formed entity (roll-up / put-together)
  • a group of former owners of one entity obtains control of the combined entity

4Business App A · B7–B12D

Business — an integrated set of activities and assets that is capable of being conducted and managed for the purpose of providing goods or services to customers, generating investment income (dividends, interest) or generating other income from ordinary activities.

A business consists of inputs and processes applied to those inputs that have the ability to contribute to the creation of outputs B7:

ElementDefinition (B7)Examples
InputAny economic resource that creates outputs, or has the ability to contribute to the creation of outputs when one or more processes are applied to it.Non-current assets (incl. intangibles, right-of-use assets), intellectual property, ability to access necessary materials or rights, employees.
ProcessAny system, standard, protocol, convention or rule that, when applied to an input, creates outputs or has the ability to contribute to creating outputs.Strategic management, operational and resource management processes. Usually documented — but an organised workforce with the necessary skills and experience, following rules and conventions, can itself provide the process.
OutputThe result of inputs and processes applied to them that provides goods or services to customers, generates investment income or other income from ordinary activities.Revenue from customers, dividends, interest. (Cost savings alone are not output.)

Not processes: accounting, billing, payroll and other administrative systems — they are not processes used to create outputs B7(b).

Minimum requirement B8 · B8A · B9 · B11

  • To be a business, the set must include, at a minimum, an input and a substantive process that together significantly contribute to the ability to create output.
  • Outputs are not required — an early-stage set with no revenue can still be a business.
  • It need not include all the inputs or processes the seller used.
  • If the set has outputs, continuing revenue alone does not prove that an input and a substantive process were acquired B8A.
  • A business need not have liabilities B9.
  • Judge as a market participant would: irrelevant whether the seller ran it as a business or whether the acquirer intends to B11.

5Is the process "substantive"? B12A–B12D

SituationThe acquired process is substantive only if…
No outputs at acquisition date B12B(a) it is critical to the ability to develop or convert the acquired input(s) into outputs, and
(b) the inputs acquired include both an organised workforce with the necessary skills, knowledge or experience to perform that process and other inputs that workforce could develop or convert into outputs (e.g. IP, technology, in-process R&D, real estate, mineral interests).
Manpower is compulsory here.
Has outputs at acquisition date B12CEither (a) it is critical to the ability to continue producing outputs and the inputs include an organised workforce able to perform it; or
(b) it significantly contributes to continuing outputs and is (i) unique or scarce, or (ii) cannot be replaced without significant cost, effort or delay.
Route (b) needs no workforce.

Further points B12D

  • An acquired contract is an input, not a substantive process. But a contract (e.g. outsourced property or asset management) may give access to an organised workforce — then check whether that workforce performs a substantive process the entity controls (look at duration and renewal terms).
  • Difficulty in replacing an acquired organised workforce may show it performs a critical process.
  • A process is not critical if it is ancillary or minor in the context of all processes needed to create outputs.

6The optional concentration test B7A–B7C

  • Optional, chosen transaction by transaction. Use it first — it is a quick exit.
  • Met if substantially all of the fair value of the gross assets acquired is concentrated in a single identifiable asset or a group of similar identifiable assets → set is NOT a business → asset acquisition, no further assessment.
  • Not met (or not applied) → does not mean it is a business → do the full B8–B12D assessment (§4–5).
  • "Substantially all" has no % in the standard — judgement. In practice it is commonly read as about 90% or more (so if other assets are more than about 10% of gross assets, the test usually fails). (Practice guide, not the standard's words.)

Step 1 · Fair value of gross assets acquired B7B(a)–(b)

Gross assets = Consideration (FV) + FV of NCI + FV of previously held interest + FV of liabilities assumed (other than DTL)

then EXCLUDE: cash & cash equivalents · deferred tax assets · goodwill arising from the effect of DTL

  • Gross assets include any consideration (+ NCI + old stake) in excess of the FV of net identifiable assets — i.e. the "goodwill" element is in the denominator.
  • If the FV of gross assets is more than this total, a more precise calculation may be needed.

Step 2 · What counts as a single asset / similar assets B7B(c)–(f)

  • Single identifiable asset = anything recognised and measured as one asset in a business combination.
  • A tangible asset attached to another (or to a leased asset) that can't be removed and used separately without significant cost or loss of utility/FV → one asset (e.g. land + building, building + fittings).
  • Similar: consider the nature of each asset and the risks of managing it and creating outputs from it.
Never "similar" B7B(f)
Tangible vs intangible assets
Tangible assets of different classes (inventory, manufacturing equipment, cars) — unless attached as above
Intangibles of different classes (brand names, licences, intangibles under development)
Financial vs non-financial assets · financial assets of different classes (receivables vs equity investments)
Assets in the same class but with significantly different risk characteristics

Buy 100% of S for ₹1,000. S's liabilities ₹140 (no DTL), cash ₹60, DTA ₹20. S owns 10 rented houses (each land + building = one asset; same class, same risks → similar) FV ₹1,000, and office equipment ₹20.
Gross assets = 1,000 + 140 − 60 − 20 = 1,060 · Houses = 1,000 ÷ 1,060 = 94% → substantially all → asset acquisition. Had S also held a shopping mall of ₹300 (different risks), houses = 1,000 ÷ 1,360 = 74% → test fails → do the full assessment.

7Decision path in the exam

  1. Run the concentration test → met? → asset acquisition. Stop.
  2. Does the set have outputs? → No: apply B12B (workforce compulsory). Yes: apply B12C.
  3. Input + substantive process present? → business → Ind AS 103. Else → asset acquisition.

PYQ Sep 2025 MCQ 12: Hans buys 100% of MNO, which owns 3 investment properties (single-tenant warehouses on long leases, FV ₹700 lakh). Maintenance and security are outsourced; the part-time executive who handled leases does not transfer. → Asset purchase (C): similar properties dominate the FV, outsourced contracts are inputs, not substantive processes, and no organised workforce is acquired. (Variant of SM Example 4.)

SM Illustration 2: pharma company with skilled R&D workforce, patents, an approved plant, no revenue yet → business (inputs + processes; no revenue is irrelevant). Illustration 3: buyer takes everything except the sales force → ICAI: still a business if the missing sales force can easily be obtained (or the buyer has one).

10Reverse acquisition B19–B27

  • Legal acquirer (issues shares) = accounting acquiree. Legal acquiree = accounting acquirer. The accounting acquiree must be a business. (e.g. private company reverse-merges into a listed shell)
  • Consideration = FV of the number of shares the legal subsidiary would have had to issue to give the legal parent's owners the same % in the combined entity B20 (or the more reliable measure — e.g. legal parent's quoted price).
  • Consolidated FS are issued in the legal parent's name but are a continuation of the legal subsidiary's FS B21–B22: legal subsidiary's assets/liabilities at pre-combination carrying amounts; legal parent's at FV; retained earnings = legal subsidiary's; equity amount = legal subsidiary's issued equity + FV of the legal parent; equity structure (number and type of shares) = legal parent's; comparatives = legal subsidiary's.
  • Owners of the legal subsidiary who don't exchange their shares → NCI at their share of the legal subsidiary's pre-combination carrying amounts B23–B24.
  • EPS B25–B27: shares before acquisition date = legal subsidiary's weighted shares × exchange ratio; after = actual shares of the legal parent.

SM TYK 2: A issues 150 shares for B's 60 (2.5 : 1) → B's owners hold 150/250 = 60% → reverse acquisition. B would have issued 40 shares (60 ÷ 60% − 60) × ₹40 = ₹1,600 consideration. A's net assets at FV = 500 + 1,500 − 700 = 1,300 → goodwill ₹300.

11Step 2 — Acquisition date 8–9

  • = the date the acquirer obtains control. Usually the closing date (consideration transferred, assets acquired, liabilities assumed) — but can be earlier (written agreement gives control before closing) or later.
  • Not the "appointed date", "effective date", agreement date or payment date as such. Profits "from 1 January" by agreement don't move the date.
  • Court / NCLT schemes and regulatory approvals (CCI): control usually passes only after substantive approvals.

12Consideration transferred 37–40

Consideration = acquisition-date FV of assets transferred + liabilities incurred to former owners + equity interests issued (+ contingent consideration)

  • Shares issued at market price on the acquisition date. Deferred cash at present value; unwinding = finance cost.
  • Non-monetary assets given (land, shares of another company): remeasure to FV, gain/loss in P&L — unless they stay inside the combined entity (e.g. transferred to the acquiree) → carrying amount, no gain 38.
  • Replacement share-based awards → Ind AS 102 market-based measure (§25).

  • Expense in the period incurred: finder's fees; advisory, legal, accounting, valuation and other professional or consulting fees; general admin costs incl. an internal acquisitions department. The list is inclusive.

Ind AS 103 · Step acquisition · No consideration · Recognition principleSTEP 3

14Business combination achieved in stages 41–42

  • Acquirer held an equity interest before getting control (e.g. 30% associate, 5% FVOCI investment, joint operation interest).
  • On the acquisition date: remeasure the previously held interest to FV → gain/loss in P&L (or OCI where appropriate).
  • Amounts already in OCI for that interest: treat as if the interest were disposed of directly — FCTR from an associate → P&L; revaluation surplus → RE; FVOCI equity gains stay in OCI (never recycled; may move to RE).
  • That FV goes into the goodwill formula.

SM TYK 5: 5% held at FVOCI (FV 1,05,000; gain 5,000 in OCI), then 95% bought → 5,000 stays in OCI; 1,05,000 + consideration for 95% goes into goodwill. SM Illus. 11: 30% associate with share of P&L 700, FCTR 100, revaluation 50 → on control, FCTR 100 → P&L; revaluation 50 → RE; plus remeasurement gain to P&L.

15Control without transferring consideration 43–44

  • (a) acquiree buys back its own shares so an existing investor gets control; (b) minority veto rights lapse; (c) combination by contract alone (stapling, dual-listed company).
  • By contract alone: the acquiree's equity is attributed to NCI (may be 100%); goodwill uses the acquisition-date FV of the acquirer's interest in place of consideration 33.

Illus. 12: Sita and Beta form a dual-listed corporation; Sita has 6 of 10 board seats → Sita is acquirer. Consideration nil; Beta's FV 80 as NCI (FV method); net assets 70 → goodwill 10.

16Step 3 — Recognition principle 10–17

  • Recognise, separately from goodwill, identifiable assets acquired, liabilities assumed and NCI.
  • Condition 1 — must meet the definitions of asset and liability in the Framework for Preparation and Presentation of FS (not the 2021 Conceptual Framework). Planned restructuring/exit costs the acquirer isn't obliged to incur are not liabilities → post-combination expense.
  • Condition 2 — must be part of what was exchanged — not a separate transaction (§23).
  • Items the acquiree never recognised can be recognised: internally generated brands, patents, customer relationships.
  • Classification / designation made on acquisition-date conditions (financial asset categories, hedges, embedded derivatives) 15–16. Exceptions: leases where acquiree is lessor (operating vs finance) and insurance contracts — classify on inception terms 17.

Identifiable intangible assets B31–B34 · B37–B40

  • Recognise separately from goodwill if it meets the contractual-legal criterion (even if not separable) or the separability criterion (capable of being sold, licensed, rented, exchanged — alone or with a related contract/asset/liability; evidence of exchange for similar items is enough).
  • Not identifiable → subsumed in goodwill: assembled workforce; potential contracts under negotiation at acquisition date.
  • In-process R&D acquired in a combination: Ind AS 38 recognition criteria (probable benefits, reliable measurement) are always treated as met → recognise at FV.

Illus. 17: e-commerce company's list of 10 million users, no intention to sell → still separable (could be transferred) → recognise intangible; intention is irrelevant. Illus. 18: two drugs in development (one approved, one awaiting approval) → both recognised as intangibles at FV.

Reacquired rights 29 · B35–B36 · 55

  • A right the acquirer had granted to the acquiree (franchise, licence) → intangible, measured on the remaining contractual term only (no renewals); amortise over that term.
  • If the contract was favourable/unfavourable vs market → settlement gain/loss (pre-existing relationship rules, §23).

Illus. 19: franchise at market terms → reacquired right recognised, no gain/loss. Illus. 20: licence unfavourable to acquirer by 3,00,000 (market 4,50,000 vs 1,50,000 unamortised); settlement clause 1,80,000 → loss = lower = 1,80,000; licence recognised at 4,50,000.

17Measurement principle 18 · B41–B45

  • Measure identifiable assets and liabilities at acquisition-date fair value.
  • No separate valuation allowance for receivables/loans — credit risk is inside their FV B41.
  • Assets the acquirer doesn't intend to use (defensive brands) — still at FV by market participant use B43.
  • Acquiree is a lessor: asset subject to an operating lease measured at FV taking the lease terms into account (no separate lease asset/liability) B42.

Ind AS 103 · NCI · Goodwill · Bargain purchase · Measurement periodSTEP 4

19NCI at the acquisition date 19 · B44–B45

  • NCI that are present ownership interests entitling holders to a proportionate share of net assets on liquidation (ordinary shares): choose, combination by combination: (a) fair value → full goodwill; or (b) proportionate share of identifiable net assets → partial goodwill.
  • All other components of NCI (e.g. preference shares not entitled to a share on liquidation, equity component of convertibles, share options) → FV, unless another Ind AS requires otherwise.
  • FV per share of NCI may differ from the acquirer's (control premium / lack-of-control discount).

Illus. 29: 60% for 97.5; net assets 150; FV of 40% NCI 65 → FV method: NCI 65, goodwill 97.5 + 65 − 150 = 12.5. Proportionate: NCI 60, goodwill 7.5. SM TYK 4: 90% for 85; NCI FV 15; net assets 100 → NCI 15 or 10.

20Goodwill 32–33

Goodwill = (a) Consideration (FV) + (b) NCI + (c) FV of previously held interest − (d) Net identifiable assets (Ind AS 103 values)

  • Only equity exchanged and the acquiree's equity FV is more reliable → use it in place of consideration 33. No consideration → use FV of the acquirer's interest.
  • Not amortised. Allocated to CGUs; impairment test annually (Ind AS 36). With partial goodwill, gross up for NCI when testing.

21Bargain purchase 34–36A Indian carve-out

  1. If (d) exceeds (a) + (b) + (c): first reassess — identification of all assets and liabilities, and measurement of net assets, NCI, old stake and consideration.
  2. Gain still there and clear evidence of the reason (forced/distress sale) → OCI → accumulate in equity as capital reserve.
  3. No clear evidence → directly in equity as capital reserve.
  4. Gain belongs to the acquirer (not NCI). Never P&L (IFRS 3 takes it to P&L).

Illus. 21: 80% for ₹15 crore; net assets 20 crore; NCI (FV) 4.2 crore; sellers forced to sell by a deadline → 20 − 19.2 = ₹80 lakh to OCI → capital reserve. SM TYK 8: 400 − (300 + 84) = 16. SM TYK 6: 70% for 5 million; net assets 10 million; NCI proportionate 3 million → 2 million gain.

22Measurement period 45–50

  • Initial accounting incomplete at the reporting date → report provisional amounts.
  • Adjust them retrospectively (against goodwill; comparatives revised, e.g. depreciation) for new information about facts and circumstances that existed at the acquisition date; also recognise additional assets/liabilities.
  • Ends when the information is obtained or learned unobtainable — max 1 year from acquisition date.
  • Information about events after acquisition (new contract, default after the date) → not a measurement-period adjustment → other Ind AS / P&L. After the period → only Ind AS 8 error correction.

Illus. 22: DTA on losses not recognisable at acquisition; big contract won later → no goodwill change (new event). Illus. 23: claim provisionally 1 crore; customer's further evidence (about day-1 facts) within period shows 2 crore → adjust goodwill by 1 crore. SM TYK 3: borrower's results for a period before acquisition → adjust goodwill; borrower's later loss of a customer → P&L.

Ind AS 103 · What is part of the deal · Employees · Share awards · After day 1CASE STUDY

23What is part of the business combination 51–52 · B50

Account only for what was exchanged for the acquiree. These are separate transactions:

  • (a) settling a pre-existing relationship between acquirer and acquiree;
  • (b) remunerating employees or former owners for future services;
  • (c) reimbursing the acquiree or former owners for the acquirer's acquisition-related costs.

Factors B50: the reasons for the transaction · who initiated it · its timing. Mainly for the acquirer's benefit → separate.

Settling a pre-existing relationship B51–B53

  • Non-contractual (lawsuit) → gain/loss at fair value.
  • Contractual (supplier, licensor) → gain/loss = lower of (i) amount by which the contract is favourable/unfavourable vs current market terms and (ii) any stated settlement provision available to the counterparty to whom it is unfavourable. If (ii) < (i), the difference is part of the business combination.
  • Adjust for any asset/liability already recognised for the relationship.

Illus. 24: acquirer had recognised ₹10m for a patent suit by the acquiree; FV of settlement at acquisition ₹20m → ₹20m treated as paid to settle the suit (out of the price); ₹10m loss in P&L.

25Share-based payment awards of the acquiree B56–B62

  • Replacement awards (acquirer obliged to replace): part in consideration = market-based measure of the acquiree award × vesting period completed ÷ greater of total vesting period and original vesting period. Rest (and any excess of replacement over acquiree award) → post-combination expense. Not obliged (award would lapse) → all expense.
  • Not replaced: vested → NCI at market-based measure; unvested → pre-combination part to NCI, rest expensed over the remaining vesting period.

27What is common control App C · 2–7

Common control business combination — a business combination involving entities or businesses in which all the combining entities are ultimately controlled by the same party or parties both before and after the combination, and that control is not transitory.

  • The extent of NCI is irrelevant.
  • Examples: merger of fellow subsidiaries · subsidiary into parent · transfer of a subsidiary within the group · demerger of a division to a group company.

28Pooling of interests method App C · 8–12

  1. Assets and liabilities at their carrying amounts (in the consolidated FS of the parent, where relevant).
  2. No fair value adjustments, no new assets/liabilities, no goodwill. Only adjust to harmonise accounting policies.

34Control — the definition 5–9 · B2

An investor controls an investee when it is exposed, or has rights, to variable returns from its involvement and has the ability to affect those returns through its power over the investee. Control exists if and only if the investor has all three:

(a) power over the investee · (b) exposure, or rights, to variable returns · (c) ability to use its power to affect the amount of its returns

  • Consider all facts and circumstances; reassess when facts indicate a change in any of the three 8 · B80–B85.
  • Two or more investors that must act together to direct relevant activities → no one controls → joint control (Ind AS 111) or significant influence (Ind AS 28) or Ind AS 109 9.

Factors to assess B3

(a) purpose and design of the investee · (b) what the relevant activities are and how decisions about them are made · (c) whether the investor's rights give it the current ability to direct them · (d) whether it is exposed to variable returns · (e) whether it can use its power to affect its returns.

Ind AS 110 · Power — relevant activities, rights, substantive vs protectiveELEMENT 1

35Power 10–14 · B9–B34

An investor has power when it has existing rights that give it the current ability to direct the relevant activities. Power arises from rights; the ability need not be exercised. Evidence of past direction helps but isn't required.

Relevant activities — activities of the investee that significantly affect its returns: selling/purchasing goods or services · managing financial assets · selecting/acquiring/disposing assets · R&D · determining funding structure · decisions on operating and capital budgets · appointing/remunerating/terminating KMP B11–B12.

  • Different investors direct different relevant activities → the one directing the activities that most significantly affect returns has power 13 · B13.
  • Activities only arise on particular events (e.g. default) → the right to direct them then can be power B53.

Illus. 3: A builds a toll road SPV (2 years); B runs it thereafter and takes the residual cash → B controls from inception (operation is the most significant; A is like a contractor). Illus. 4: asset manager holding 10% units takes investment decisions → has power, but still check returns and link. Illus. 5: asset manager directs until defaults hit a threshold, then the debt investor → each must judge who directs the activities that most significantly affect returns. Illus. 15: receivables SPV — investor manages defaulted receivables (the only significant activity) → power.

Rights that can give power B15

(a) voting / potential voting rights · (b) rights to appoint, reassign or remove KMP · (c) rights to appoint or remove another entity that directs · (d) rights to direct the investee into transactions (or veto changes) for the investor's benefit · (e) other rights, e.g. management contracts.

36Substantive rights B22–B25

Only substantive rights count — the holder must have the practical ability to exercise them when decisions need to be made. Barriers that make a right non-substantive:

  • financial penalties or incentives that deter exercise; an exercise or conversion price that makes it uneconomic;
  • terms that make exercise unlikely (narrow time window); no mechanism to exercise;
  • holder can't obtain information needed to exercise;
  • operational barriers — e.g. no other manager willing/able to take over;
  • legal or regulatory barriers (e.g. foreign investor prohibited);
  • rights held by many parties who would need to agree — the more parties, the less likely substantive;
  • whether the holder would benefit from exercising them.

Illus. 6: investors gave decision rights to A for 5 years; taking them back triggers a huge penalty → not substantive. Dissenting investors lack a majority → not substantive. Illus. 7: option far out of the money; too-narrow exercise window; no information on market value → all not substantive. Illus. 8: right to remove fund manager, but no replacement manager available → not substantive.

37Protective rights B26–B28 · B29–B33

  • Designed to protect the holder's interest without giving power; usually apply to fundamental changes or exceptional circumstances. They never give power and can't prevent another party from having power.
  • Examples: lender restricting the borrower from activities that change its credit risk · NCI's right to approve capex beyond the ordinary course, or issue of equity/debt · lender's right to seize assets on default.
  • Franchise rights protecting the brand are generally protective; the franchisee may still have power.

Illus. 9: ABC (brand owner) franchise to PQR, which funds the outlet, hires staff and picks vendors → ABC's rights protect the brand → protective; PQR has power.

39Exposure to variable returns 15–16 · B55–B57

  • Returns that vary with the investee's performance — can be positive, negative or both. Judge on substance: even a "fixed" interest coupon is variable because of credit risk.
  • Examples: dividends, interest, changes in value of the investment · fees for servicing assets or liabilities · fees and exposure from credit or liquidity support · residual interests on liquidation · tax benefits · access to future liquidity · returns not available to others — synergies, economies of scale, cost savings, scarce products, proprietary knowledge.

40Link — principal or agent? 17–18 · B58–B75

A decision-maker with power that acts as an agent (on behalf of others) does not control. Weigh together:

FactorPoints to agent when…
(a) Scope of authoritynarrow, pre-set mandate; others involved in design
(b) Rights held by otherssubstantive removal (kick-out) rights held by a single party → agent, conclusively; held by many dispersed parties → less weight

41Investment entities 27–33 · B85A–B85W

An investment entity (a) obtains funds from one or more investors to provide them investment management services; (b) commits to its investors that its business purpose is to invest funds solely for capital appreciation, investment income, or both; and (c) measures and evaluates substantially all investments on a fair value basis.

  • Typical characteristics 28: more than one investment · more than one investor · investors not related parties · ownership interests in the form of equity or similar. Missing one doesn't automatically disqualify — judgement and disclosure.
  • Must have exit strategies for its equity and non-financial investments (not needed for debt with fixed maturity) B85F–B85H.
  • It and its group must not obtain other benefits from investees not available to unrelated parties (e.g. rights to acquire investees' assets/technology below market, guarantees, off-market transactions) B85I.
  • Investment-related services to investees and support are allowed if not a substantial separate business or separate substantial income B85C–B85E.
  • Accounting: does not consolidate; measures subsidiaries at FVTPL (Ind AS 109) — except a subsidiary that is not itself an investment entity and mainly provides investment-related services → consolidate it 31–32.
  • Parent of an investment entity that is not itself one → consolidates all subsidiaries, including those held through the investment entity 33.
  • Change of status (prospective) B100–B101: ceases → subsidiary deemed acquired at FV on that date (goodwill = FV of investment + NCI − net assets); becomes → stop consolidating, FV gain/loss in P&L as on loss of control.
