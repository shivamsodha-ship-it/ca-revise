Ind AS 103 — Business Combinations

Follows ICAI SM Chapter 12 heading by heading (1 Introduction → 17 Carve-in). Every Example, Illustration and TYK with ICAI's answer.

1Introduction — why Ind AS 103

  • Old Indian GAAP: AS 14 (Accounting for Amalgamations) + AS 21 (consolidation) — not in line with global standards. Ind AS 103 is in line with IFRS 3.
  • Business combination = a transaction in which the acquirer obtains control of another business (the acquiree).
  • Business = an integrated set of activities and assets capable of being conducted and managed for providing goods or services to customers, generating investment income (dividends, interest) or other income from ordinary activities.

2Scope 2

Applies to a transaction or event that meets the definition of a business combination. Does not apply to:

  • (a) the formation of a joint arrangement (in the FS of the joint arrangement itself);
  • (b) acquisition of an asset or group of assets that is not a business (asset acquisition) — identify and recognise individual identifiable assets (incl. Ind AS 38 intangibles) and liabilities, allocate cost on relative fair values at date of purchase; no goodwill;
  • (c) combinations of entities or businesses under common control → dealt with in Appendix C (SM §13);
  • (d) acquisition by an investment entity of a subsidiary required to be measured at FVTPL (Ind AS 110).

3Definition of business combination

  • Occurs when an entity obtains control of a business by acquiring its net assets or a significant equity interest.
  • Control can be obtained by contract only — the acquirer acquires neither net assets nor equity → in the balance sheet controlling interest = 0 and NCI = 100%.

Two elements, both required: (1) the acquirer obtains control of an acquiree (control as defined in Ind AS 110); and (2) the acquiree is a business.

Ways of obtaining control

  • transferring cash, cash equivalents or other assets (incl. net assets that constitute a business)
  • incurring liabilities
  • issuing equity interests
  • providing more than one type of consideration
  • without transferring consideration, incl. by contract alone

Legal structures (not limited to)

  • businesses become subsidiaries, or their net assets are legally merged into the acquirer
  • one entity transfers its net assets / its owners transfer equity to another entity or its owners
  • all entities transfer to a newly formed entity (roll-up / put-together)
  • a group of former owners of one combining entity obtains control of the combined entity

4Definition and elements of business

4.1 Definition B7

  • A business consists of inputs and processes applied to those inputs that have the ability to contribute to the creation of outputs. Businesses usually have outputs, but outputs are not required.
  • The entity must gain control of an integrated set of assets and activities that is more than a collection of assets or of assets and liabilities. A business normally carries out a continuing trade with identifiable revenue — its assets interact with each other and with the people who operate them. In some cases it needs judgement.

4.2 Elements of business

ElementDefinitionExamples (SM)
(a) InputAny economic resource that creates outputs or has the ability to contribute to the creation of outputs when one or more processes are applied to it.Non-current assets (incl. intangibles or rights to use non-current assets), intellectual property, the ability to obtain access to necessary materials or rights, and employees.
(b) ProcessAny system, standard, protocol, convention or rule that when applied to an input or inputs creates output or has the ability to contribute to the creation of outputs.Strategic management, operational and resource management processes. Typically documented — but the intellectual capacity of an organised workforce having the necessary skills and experience, following rules and conventions, may provide the processes. Accounting, billing, payroll and other administrative systems are typically NOT processes used to create outputs.
(c) OutputThe result of inputs and processes applied to those inputs that provide goods or services to customers, generate investment income (dividends, interest) or generate other income from ordinary activities.Sales to customers, dividend and interest income.

4.3 Further assessment — the minimum B8 · B12A–B12C

  • Two essential elements: inputs and processes applied to those inputs.
  • An integrated set must include, at a minimum, an input and a substantive process that together significantly contribute to the ability to create output.
Substantive process?Conditions (SM 4.3)
(1) No output at acquisition dateSubstantive only if — (a) it is critical to the ability to develop or convert an acquired input or inputs into outputs; and (b) the inputs acquired include both an organised workforce that has the necessary skills, knowledge or experience to perform that process, and other inputs that the organised workforce could develop or convert into outputs. Those other inputs could include — (i) intellectual property that could be used to develop a good or service; (ii) other economic resources that could be developed to create outputs; or (iii) rights to obtain access to necessary materials or rights that enable creation of future outputs.
(2) Has outputs at acquisition dateSubstantive if, when applied to an acquired input or inputs, it — (a) is critical to the ability to continue producing outputs, and the inputs acquired include an organised workforce with the necessary skills, knowledge or experience to perform that process; or (b) significantly contributes to the ability to continue producing outputs and — (i) is considered unique or scarce; or (ii) cannot be replaced without significant cost, effort, or delay in the ability to continue producing outputs.
Manpower link: no output → organised workforce is compulsory. Output exists → workforce needed for route (a); route (b) (unique / scarce / hard to replace) works without a workforce.

4.4 Concentration test B7A–B7C

  • An optional test that permits a simplified assessment of whether an acquired set is not a business.
  • Test met → not a business → no further assessment.   Test not met → further assessment (4.3) needed.
  • Met if substantially all of the fair value of the gross assets acquired is concentrated in a single identifiable asset or group of similar identifiable assets.
Conditions for the test (B7B)
(a)Gross assets exclude cash and cash equivalents, deferred tax assets, and goodwill resulting from the effects of deferred tax liabilities.
(b)FV of gross assets includes any consideration transferred (plus FV of any NCI and FV of any previously held interest) in excess of the FV of net identifiable assets acquired.
(c)A single identifiable asset includes any asset or group of assets that would be recognised and measured as a single identifiable asset in a business combination.
(d)A tangible asset attached to, and not physically removable and usable separately from, another tangible asset (or from an underlying asset subject to a lease, Ind AS 116) without significant cost or significant diminution in utility or FV → a single identifiable asset (e.g. land and buildings).
(e)To judge similar: consider the nature of each single identifiable asset and the risks of managing and creating outputs from it (risk characteristics).
(f)Not similar: (i) a tangible and an intangible asset; (ii) tangible assets in different classes unless a single asset under (d); (iii) identifiable intangible assets in different classes; (iv) a financial and a non-financial asset; (v) financial assets in different classes; (vi) assets in the same class but with significantly different risk characteristics.

SM notes

  1. The test is optional; the decision is made transaction by transaction.
  2. It does not prohibit a detailed assessment using the definition of business.
  3. 3-step process: (a) measure FV of gross assets acquired; (b) identify the single identifiable asset or group of similar identifiable assets; (c) if substantially all of (a) is concentrated in (b) → asset acquisition; otherwise assess the business definition.

FV of gross assets = (i) FV of consideration (incl. FV of NCI and FV of previously held interest) + (ii) FV of liabilities assumed − (iii) cash & cash equivalents, DTA and goodwill resulting from DTL

Example 1 (concentration test): A holds 20% of B; buys 50% more for ₹300 crore. FV: building 1,000; cash 200; financial liabilities 800; DTL 150. FV of B 400 → NCI 120 (30%), old 20% stake 80.
(i) 300 + 120 + 80 = 500 · (ii) liabilities excl. deferred tax = 800 · (iii) cash = 200 → gross assets = 500 + 800 − 200 = 1,100. Building 1,000 / 1,100 = 91% → substantially all in a single identifiable asset → asset acquisition. "Substantially" is not defined in the standard — judgement; ICAI treats 91% as substantial.

Summary flow chart — business or not? (SM)

  1. Inputs and processes acquired? No → asset acquisition.
  2. Outputs exist at acquisition date? Yes → is the process critical to continue producing outputs and is an organised experienced workforce acquired? Yes → substantive. If No → is the process unique/scarce or not replaceable without significant cost, effort or delay? Yes → substantive; No → asset acquisition.
  3. Outputs don't exist? → is the process critical to develop/convert inputs into outputs? No → asset acquisition. Yes → do the inputs include both an organised workforce and other inputs it could develop/convert? Yes → substantive; No → asset acquisition.
  4. Substantive process acquired → business combination.

5The acquisition method

  1. Step 1 — Identifying the acquirer. (Who is the acquirer?)
  2. Step 2 — Determining the acquisition date. (When is the business acquired?)
  3. Step 3 — Recognising and measuring the identifiable assets acquired, liabilities assumed and any NCI. (Which assets and liabilities? At what value?)
  4. Step 4 — Recognising and measuring goodwill or a gain from a bargain purchase.

6Identifying the acquiring enterprise

6.1 The acquiring enterprise

  • All business combinations in scope use the acquisition method (purchase method). The acquirer = the entity that obtains control; the acquiree's assets and liabilities are the ones measured at FV.
  • Control is determined under Ind AS 110: an investor controls if and only if it has (a) power over the investee; (b) exposure, or rights, to variable returns; (c) ability to use its power to affect its returns.
  • Control does not depend only on voting rights. Also consider: potential voting rights; rights of non-controlling shareholders; other contractual rights of the investor if substantive. (Detail: Ind AS 110 notes.)
  • Indicators of control: more than 50% voting rights · power to appoint and remove the board · currently exercisable potential voting rights.

Illustration 4 (potential voting rights): P buys 40,000 of X's 1,00,000 shares + option for 25,000 more, exercisable any time in 12 months at a small premium. Others hold 35,000 and 25,000, each with currently exercisable options for 2,000. → Options currently exercisable, no conditions; P would hold 65,000/1,25,000 (>50%); even if all options exercised, 65,000/1,29,000 = majority; small premium (slightly out of the money) still gives economic substance → P controls X.

6.2 Acquisition through cash or incurring liabilities B14

The acquirer is usually the entity that transfers the cash or other assets or incurs the liabilities.

6.3 Acquisition through issue of equity B15–B17

The acquirer is usually the entity that issues its equity — except in reverse acquisitions. Also consider:

Illustration 5: NewCo Meera issues 100 shares to Veera's and 50 to Zeera's shareholders (relative FVs), voting by shareholding → the acquirer must be one of the combining entities; Veera's owners get 67% → Veera is the acquirer.

Examples 8 & 9: (8) larger A issues shares = 20% of combined capital to B's holders; A's holders keep 80% → A is acquirer. (9) smaller A issues 10 shares per B share = 70% of combined capital to B's holders, and B's management runs the combined entity → B is the accounting acquirer; A is the legal acquirer. Identification matters because the accounting acquiree's net assets are the ones taken at FV.

Illustration 6: ABC forms Super Ltd., which borrows from a bank at commercial rates and pays cash at FV for 100% of Focus; Super can appoint/remove a majority of Focus's board and its management dominates → Super Ltd. is the acquirer (key drivers: on whose behalf the NewCo was formed, and what business is acquired).

6.4 Shell company and reverse acquisition B19–B27

  • Reverse acquisition: the entity that issues securities (legal acquirer) is the acquiree for accounting; the entity whose equity is acquired (legal acquiree) is the accounting acquirer.
  • Typical case: a private operating company wants to become public without registering its shares → arranges for a public entity to acquire its shares in exchange for the public entity's shares → public entity = legal acquirer but accounting acquiree; private entity = accounting acquirer.
  • The accounting acquiree must meet the definition of a business; all Ind AS 103 recognition and measurement principles apply, including goodwill.
  • Consideration = acquisition-date FV of the accounting acquirer's equity: the number of shares the legal subsidiary would have had to issue to give the legal parent's owners the same % of the combined entity.

Example 9 (reverse acquisition): A (30,000 shares; net assets 1,100) issues 2.5 shares per B share → 1,50,000 shares for B's 60,000. B's holders own 1,50,000/1,80,000 = 83.33% and 6 of 8 board seats → B is accounting acquirer. B's share price ₹105 (A's ₹20). B would have issued 30,000 ÷ 2.5 = 12,000 shares (12,000/72,000 same ratio) × ₹105 = ₹12,60,000. Goodwill = 12,60,000 − 11,00,000 = ₹1,60,000.

Example 11 (new parent pays cash): A spins off B and C; forms D with independent directors; D raises cash from new investors (99%; A keeps 1%) and pays cash for 100% of B and C → D is the acquirer — it paid cash and its new owners obtained control of B and C from A.

Accounting may change significantly if the accounting acquirer differs from the legal acquirer. Always test who really gets control.

7Determining the acquisition date 8–9

  • = the date on which the acquirer obtains control. Generally the closing date (consideration legally transferred, assets acquired, liabilities assumed) — but it can be earlier (written agreement gives control before closing) or later. Consider all facts.
  • It decides when consideration, assets, liabilities and NCI are measured, and from when the acquiree's results are consolidated — it materially affects post-combination earnings.

Example 12: shareholder agreement 1 Jun; appointed date 1 Apr; board control 1 Jul; payment 15 Jul; share transfer 1 Aug → acquisition date 1 July 20X1 (appointed date isn't control).

8Step acquisitions 41–42

  • Acquisition date = date control is obtained. Until then the investment follows Ind AS 109 (or Ind AS 28 for associates/JVs).
  • On control: remeasure the previously held equity interest at acquisition-date FV; gain or loss in P&L or OCI, as appropriate.
  • Amounts earlier recognised in OCI for that interest → recognised on the same basis as if the interest were disposed of directly.

SM example: Fruits Ltd buys 15% of Mango (1 Apr 20X1) → 20% more (5 Apr 20X2) → 65% more (31 Mar 20X3) → total 90% → control (acquisition date) = 31 March 20X3. Assumption: control comes with total stake unless stated otherwise.

9Determination of the purchase consideration 37–40

  • Measured at fair value = total of acquisition-date FVs of assets transferred (incl. cash) + liabilities incurred to former owners of the acquiree + equity interests issued by the acquirer (+ contingent consideration).
  • Forms: cash, other assets, a business or subsidiary of the acquirer, contingent consideration, ordinary or preference equity instruments, options, warrants, member interests of mutual entities.
  • Exception to FV: the portion of the acquirer's share-based payment awards exchanged for acquiree employees' awards that is included in consideration → measured under Ind AS 102.
  • Non-monetary assets transferred (carrying amount ≠ FV): remeasure to FV at acquisition date; gain/loss in P&L. E.g. land given to the acquiree's owners → land's FV is consideration; derecognise land; difference to P&L.
  • But if the transferred assets/liabilities remain within the combined entity (transferred to the acquiree, not its former owners) → acquirer keeps control → measure at carrying amounts, no gain/loss. They still affect NCI and goodwill.

Purchase consideration = FV of assets transferred (incl. cash) + liabilities incurred to former owners + equity interests issued + contingent consideration

9.1 Business combination achieved in stages (step acquisition)

  • Acquirer held an equity interest immediately before the acquisition date. Example 13: A holds 35% of B on 31 Dec 20X1 and buys 40% more → control → step acquisition.
  • Remeasure previously held interest at acquisition-date FV; gain/loss in P&L.
  • If the old interest was designated FVOCI (Ind AS 109 / 27): once elected, all gains/losses stay in OCI even on sale → remeasurement gain/loss goes to OCI.
  • A party to a joint operation that obtains control of the joint operation business → business combination in stages → remeasure its previously held interest at FV.

Illustration 11: PQR buys 30% of XYZ for ₹8,000 crore (equity method). Year 1: share of P&L 700, FCTR in OCI 100, revaluation reserve in OCI 50 → carrying amount 8,850. Then buys 70% for ₹25,000 crore; FV of the 30% = 9,000; FV of net assets 30,000.
Goodwill = 25,000 + 9,000 − 30,000 = 4,000. Gain on old stake = 9,000 − 8,850 = 150 + FCTR recycled 100 = 250 to P&L. Revaluation reserve 50 → retained earnings (Ind AS 16: not reclassified to P&L).
Entry: Dr Net assets 30,000 · Dr Goodwill 4,000 · Dr FCTR 100 · Dr Revaluation reserve 50 · Cr Cash 25,000 · Cr Investment in associate 8,850 · Cr Retained earnings 50 · Cr Gain (P&L) 250.

SM TYK 5: 5% held at FVOCI (cost 1,00,000; FV 1,05,000; ₹5,000 in OCI), then buys 95% → the ₹5,000 stays in OCI (not recycled under Ind AS 109); investment already at FV, so no remeasurement; FV 1,05,000 + consideration for 95% go into goodwill.

Cash 59,00,000 + 1,00,000 shares × ₹10 + contingent consideration (FV) 3,00,000 + old stake 20,00,000 = 92,00,000. Costs ₹1,00,000 → P&L. NCI at FV 7,50,000. Net assets 60,00,000 → goodwill 39,50,000. Gain on old stake 20,00,000 − 6,00,000 = 14,00,000 to P&L.

9.2 Business combination without transfer of consideration 43–44

  • (a) The acquiree repurchases enough of its own shares for an existing investor to obtain control.
  • (b) Minority veto rights lapse that had kept the majority holder from controlling.
  • (c) Acquirer and acquiree combine by contract alone — no consideration, no equity interest held now or before (e.g. stapling arrangement, dual listed corporation).
  • By contract alone: attribute the acquiree's net assets to its owners → NCI, even if all of the acquiree's equity is NCI. Goodwill uses the FV of the acquirer's interest in place of consideration.

Illustration 12 (contract alone): Sita (FV 100) and Beta (FV 80; net assets 70) form a DLC; Sita gets 6 of 10 board seats → Sita acquirer. Part A: consideration nil + interest in acquiree (NCI at FV) 80 + old interest nil; Part B: net assets 70 → goodwill ₹10 crore.

9.3 Direct cost of acquisition 53

  • Not part of consideration: finder's fees, due diligence, accounting, legal, investment banker fees, even bonuses to employees for a successful acquisition; advisory, valuation, other professional or consulting fees; general admin costs incl. an internal acquisitions department.
  • Expensed in the period the costs are incurred and services received — one exception: costs to issue debt or equity → Ind AS 32 / Ind AS 109.
  • The para 53 list is inclusive, only indicative.

Illustration 13 — stamp duty on land acquired in a business combination → acquisition-related cost, expense in the period the acquisition is completed. It is not part of the FV exchange and doesn't increase future benefits (assets are at FV). Separate PPE purchase under Ind AS 16 would capitalise it.

Illustration 14 — one-time payment to the regulator to transfer a wireless spectrum licence acquired with PQR → transaction cost, expense. Transfer of rights is integral to the business combination. (Bought separately, Ind AS 38 would capitalise it.)

9.4 Contingent consideration 39–40 · 58

  • Consideration includes any asset or liability from a contingent consideration arrangement → recognise its acquisition-date FV as part of consideration.
  • Classify an obligation to pay as liability or equity using Ind AS 32 definitions (or other Ind AS). A right to the return of previously transferred consideration → asset.
  • FV measured on the acquisition date. Direct costs → P&L, never in consideration.

Example 14: extra ₹20m cash if targets met; FV at acquisition ₹15m → financial liability (variable cash) → increases goodwill. Later targets become less likely → remeasure liability; change to P&L.

SM TYK 10: 10 lakh shares now (FV ₹20) + 2 lakh more shares after one year if PBIT > ₹1 crore; FV of contingent consideration ₹25 lakh. → fixed number of shares → equity; consideration 2,00,00,000 + 25,00,000 = 2,25,00,000; when target met and share price is ₹25, no remeasurement (equity). Variant: shares of a variable number worth ₹40 lakh → financial liability at ₹25 lakh → remeasured to ₹40 lakh → ₹15 lakh loss in P&L.

10Purchase price allocation

10.1 Recognition of assets and liabilities of the acquired entity 10–17

At the acquisition date recognise, separately from goodwill, the identifiable assets acquired, liabilities assumed and any NCI. The most important principle: recognise and measure all assets and liabilities acquired on the acquisition date.

10.1.1 Recognition conditions

  • Must meet the definitions of assets and liabilities in the Framework for the Preparation and Presentation of FS (not the 2021 Conceptual Framework) at the acquisition date. E.g. costs the acquirer expects but is not obliged to incur to exit an activity, terminate or relocate employees → not liabilities → post-combination expense under other Ind AS.
  • Record only assets and liabilities that are part of the business combination deal; other items follow other Ind AS.
  • Acquirer may record items the acquiree never recorded — internally developed brand name, patent, customer relationship.
  • Classification and designation on conditions at the acquisition date: financial assets/liabilities (FVTPL / amortised cost / FVOCI), hedge designation, separation of embedded derivatives (Ind AS 109).
  • Only exception: lease contracts where the acquiree is lessor (operating vs finance) → classified on conditions at inception, not reassessed — unless modified at that date.

10.2 Measurement principle 18–19

  • Identifiable assets and liabilities at acquisition-date fair values.
  • NCI — components that are present ownership interests entitling holders to a proportionate share of net assets on liquidation: at fair value, or at the present ownership instruments' proportionate share of the recognised identifiable net assets. All other NCI components at acquisition-date fair value, unless another Ind AS requires otherwise.

10.2.1 Exceptions to the recognition or measurement principle 21–31A

Limited to acquisition-date accounting. Result: (1) an item that otherwise would not be recorded gets recorded; or (2) items are measured at a value other than FV.

10.3 Intangible assets B31–B34

Recognise separately from goodwill if the item meets the definition of an asset and is identifiable by either criterion:

10.3.1 Contractual-legal criterion

  • Identifiable even if not transferable or separable.
  • (a) Licence to operate a nuclear power plant → meets the criterion even if it can't be sold apart from the plant; may be combined with the plant as a single asset if useful lives are similar.
  • (b) A technology patent licensed to others outside the domestic market for a % of revenue → both the patent and the licence agreement meet the criterion even if selling them separately isn't practical.

10.3.2 Separability criterion

  • Capable of being separated and sold, transferred, licensed, rented or exchanged, individually or with a related contract, asset or liability.
  • Met even if the acquirer doesn't intend to sell/license it; evidence of exchange transactions for that type of asset (even infrequent, even not involving the acquirer) is enough.
  • Example 17: customer and subscriber lists are frequently licensed → separable — unless confidentiality or other agreements prohibit selling or exchanging customer information.
  • Separable with a related item: (a) depositor relationship intangible exchanged with deposit liabilities → recognise separately; (b) registered trademark + documented unpatented technical expertise — expertise must be sold with the trademark → separable.

Sources for spotting intangibles (SM)

  • Acquiree's FS: significant R&D cost → technology intangibles · significant sales promotion / marketing cost → marketing intangibles (distributor network, collaterals) · customer acquisition cost (incentives to register first-time users / app downloads) → customer list.
  • Share purchase agreement → technical know-how, trademarks, patents named in it.
  • Purpose of acquisition → e.g. Coca-Cola bought Thums Up intending to close the brand to raise market share — still a possible intangible.

Illustration 17: FMCG A buys e-commerce E with 10 million registered users; E has no intention to sell the list → no legal/contractual right, but separable (useful to others, E can transfer it); intention irrelevant → recognise customer list.

Illustration 18: pharma XYZ has two drugs in clinical trials — one awaiting approval (expected in 2 years), one approved. Ind AS 38.33: probability and reliable-measurement criteria are always satisfied for intangibles acquired in a BC; IPR&D is recognised if it meets the asset definition and is identifiable → both recognised; the probability of approval is reflected in FV. Later expenditure → Ind AS 38 paras 43, 54–62. (Internal research could never be capitalised.)

10.3.3 Assembled workforce and other items not identifiable

  • Assembled workforce — existing collection of employees allowing the business to operate from the acquisition date → not identifiable → goodwill.
  • Potential contracts being negotiated with prospective customers → not assets at acquisition date → goodwill; don't reclassify later for post-acquisition events (but check events shortly after acquisition to see if an intangible existed at the date).
  • After initial recognition → Ind AS 38. Identifiability decides recognition only; FV uses market-participant assumptions, e.g. expected contract renewals (renewals need not themselves be identifiable).

10.4 Reacquired rights 29 · B35–B36

  • A right the acquirer had granted to the acquiree to use the acquirer's recognised or unrecognised assets (trade name under a franchise; technology under a licence) → identifiable intangible, separate from goodwill.
  • If the contract is favourable or unfavourable vs current market terms → recognise a settlement gain or loss.

Illustration 19: Vadapav buys its franchisee Efficient; licence at current market terms → reacquired right recognised as an intangible; no gain/loss.

Illustration 20: ABC pays ₹1 crore for PQR. Four years ago it gave PQR a 10-year licence for ₹2,50,000; termination costs unexpired fee + 20% = ₹1,80,000; a new 6-year licence would fetch ₹4,50,000. Licence recognised at ₹4,50,000. Loss = lower of (i) unfavourable amount 4,50,000 − 1,50,000 = 3,00,000 and (ii) settlement 1,80,000 = ₹1,80,000. 10.5 Goodwill — recognition and measurement 32–33

Goodwill = (a) [i. consideration at acquisition-date FV + ii. NCI (Ind AS 103) + iii. FV of previously held interest (step acquisition)] − (b) net identifiable assets (Ind AS 103 amounts)

  • Only equity exchanged and the acquiree's equity FV is more reliable → use the acquiree's equity FV instead.
  • No consideration → use the acquisition-date FV of the acquirer's interest in the acquiree.

10.6 Bargain purchase 34–36

  • Extremely rare: net assets acquired exceed the consideration (+ NCI + old stake).
  • Clear evidence of the reason → gain in OCI on the acquisition date, accumulated in equity as capital reserve. No clear evidence → directly in equity as capital reserve.
  • Gain attributed to the acquirer — no allocation to NCI.
  • Examples of reasons: forced sale (seller under compulsion), bankruptcy sale, sale due to a regulatory requirement.
  • Before recognising: confirm clear evidence of the reason; reassess whether all assets and liabilities are identified (recognise any found); review the measurement of (i) identifiable assets and liabilities, (ii) NCI, (iii) previously held interest, (iv) consideration — to reflect all information available at the acquisition date.

Example 18: UK competition authority orders X to sell a division of Y's UK assets within a deadline; boutique Z buys it → more likely than not a bargain purchase (seller under compulsion).

Illustration 21: A buys 80% of B for ₹15 crore; sellers had to dispose by a deadline. Net assets ₹20 crore; NCI FV ₹4.2 crore → 20 − 19.2 = ₹80 lakh → (clear evidence) OCI → capital reserve. With proportionate NCI: NCI 4 crore → gain ₹1 crore.

SM TYK 6: 70% for ₹5 million; NCI proportionate; net assets ₹10 million → NCI 3m → gain 10 − 8 = ₹2 million. SM TYK 8: Alpha buys 80% of Beta for ₹300 (seller legally compelled); assets 500, liabilities 100; NCI FV 84 → 400 − 384 = ₹16 → OCI → capital reserve.

10.7 Measurement period 45–50

  • If information is incomplete at the reporting date, do the purchase price allocation on a provisional basis.
  • During the period: retrospectively adjust provisional amounts for new information about facts and circumstances that existed at the acquisition date; also recognise additional assets/liabilities. Adjust against goodwill.
  • Ends when the information is received or learned unobtainable — never more than one year from acquisition date.
  • Covers: identifiable assets, liabilities and NCI; consideration (or other amount used for goodwill); previously held interest; resulting goodwill or bargain gain.
  • After the period: changes for information that existed at the date → error under Ind AS 8. Conditions that did not exist at the date → other Ind AS (P&L).

10.8 Determining what is part of the business combination transaction 51–53 · B50–B53

  • The parties may have a pre-existing relationship or an arrangement made during negotiations that is separate from the combination. Identify amounts that are not part of the exchange for the acquiree. Apply the acquisition method only to consideration transferred and assets/liabilities exchanged for the acquiree; account for separate transactions under the relevant Ind AS.
  • A transaction entered by or for the acquirer or primarily for the benefit of the acquirer or combined entity (not of the acquiree or its former owners) is likely separate.

Examples of separate transactions

• settling pre-existing relationships between acquirer and acquiree · • remunerating employees or former owners of the acquiree for future services · • reimbursing the acquiree or its former owners for paying the acquirer's acquisition-related costs.

10.9 Contingent payments to employees or selling shareholders B54–B55

Whether they are contingent consideration or a separate transaction depends on the nature of the arrangement — look at why the provision exists, who initiated it and when. If unclear, use these indicators:

IndicatorConsideration vs remuneration
(a) Continuing employmentPayments automatically forfeited if employment terminates → remuneration for post-combination services. Not affected by termination → may be additional consideration.
(b) Duration of continuing employmentRequired employment period coincides with or is longer than the contingent payment period → may be remuneration.
(c) Level of remunerationOther pay reasonable vs other key employees → contingent payments may be additional consideration.
(d) Incremental payments to employeesNon-employee sellers get less per share than sellers who become employees → the incremental amount is remuneration.
(e) Number of shares ownedSellers who owned substantially all shares stay as key employees → may be profit-sharing (remuneration). Stayers own few shares and all sellers get the same per share → consideration. Consider shares of related parties (family).
(f) Linkage to valuationInitial price at the low end of the valuation range and the formula relates to that valuation → consideration. Formula consistent with prior profit-sharing → remuneration.
(g) FormulaMultiple of earnings → consideration (establishes/verifies FV of acquiree). Specified % of earnings → profit sharing → remuneration.
(h) Other agreements and issuesNon-competes, executory or consulting contracts, property leases and tax treatment. E.g. lease from a selling shareholder at significantly below market → part of the contingent payments is really rent, recognised post-combination; at market rent → contingent consideration.

Illustration 26: 3 equal shareholders, 2 are senior employees who stay. 10.10 Acquirer share-based payment awards exchanged for acquiree employees' awards B56–B62

  • Replacement awards may cover vested and unvested awards; accounted as modifications under Ind AS 102.
  • If the acquirer replaces acquiree awards, all or part of the market-based measure (remeasured on acquisition date under Ind AS 102) of the replacement awards is included in consideration.
  • Acquiree awards would expire on the combination and the acquirer replaces them without being obliged → all of the market-based measure is post-combination remuneration; none in consideration. The acquirer is obliged if replacement is required by (a) the acquisition agreement, (b) the terms of the acquiree's awards, or (c) laws or regulations (i.e. the acquiree or employees can enforce it).
  • Measure both replacement and acquiree awards at the acquisition date (Ind AS 102).

Pre-combination portion (→ consideration) = market-based measure of ACQUIREE award × vesting period completed ÷ greater of (total vesting period, original vesting period) Post-combination portion (→ remuneration cost) = market-based measure of REPLACEMENT award − pre-combination portion

  • Any excess of replacement over acquiree award measure → post-combination remuneration.
  • Attribute a portion to post-combination service if post-combination service is required, even if employees had completed all service for the acquiree awards.
  • Both portions reflect the best estimate of awards expected to vest. Example 19: pre-combination measure ₹100, 95% expected to vest → ₹95 in consideration.
  • Later changes in expected vesting / forfeitures, modifications, outcomes of performance conditions → remuneration cost of that period (not consideration).
  • Same rules whether the award is liability or equity-classified; liability-award remeasurements after acquisition → post-combination FS. Income tax effects → Ind AS 12.

Illustration 27: original vesting 5 years, 2 served; replacement vests in 2 more years (total 4); original award ₹500, replacement ₹600 → pre-combination = 500 × 2 ÷ greater of (5, 4) = ₹200 → consideration. Post-combination = 600 − 200 = ₹400 → expense over 2 years.

10.11 Non-replacement awards

  • Acquiree awards not exchanged by the acquirer → settled in the acquiree's own shares → those holders become NCI.
  • Vested → part of NCI at market-based measure (the share-based payment reserve is classified as NCI).
  • Unvested → market-based measure as if acquisition date were grant date; pre-combination portion (ratio as above) → NCI; the balance → employee cost over remaining vesting, credited to NCI.

Illustration 28: Q's award vests after 5 years; 2 served; P doesn't replace it but cuts the remaining period from 3 to 2 years; measure ₹500 → ₹200 to NCI (500 × 2/5, original vesting period); ₹300 expensed over 2 years.

10.12 Non-controlling interest in an acquiree 19 · B44–B45

  • NCI may be measured at fair value — from a quoted price in an active market for the shares not held by the acquirer, or other valuation techniques.
  • Per-share FV of the acquirer's interest and of NCI may differ: control premium in the acquirer's, or discount for lack of control (NCI discount) in NCI's.

13Common control transactions including merger Appendix C

13.1 Definitions

  • Transferor — entity or business combined into another entity as a result of a business combination.
  • Transferee — entity in which the transferor is combined.
  • Reserve — portion of earnings, receipts or other surplus (capital or revenue) appropriated by management for a general or specific purpose, other than provision for depreciation.
  • Common control business combination — involving entities or businesses that are all ultimately controlled by the same party or parties both before and after the combination, and that control is not transitory.

13.2 Common control business combinations

  • Includes transfers of subsidiaries or businesses between entities within a group.
  • Extent of NCI is not relevant — a partly-owned subsidiary is still controlled by the parent.
  • Controller may be an individual or a group of individuals acting together under a contractual arrangement, who need not follow Ind AS — combining entities need not be in the same consolidated FS.
  • A group of individuals controls when, as a result of contractual arrangements, they collectively have power to govern financial and operating policies to obtain benefits, and that collective power is not transitory.
  • Usually a group reorganisation where direct ownership changes but the ultimate parent stays the same. Judgement needed on:
    • Transitory — not defined; meant to apply Ind AS 103 acquisition accounting where a substantive change in control is structured so the entity is briefly under common control before and after. Not transitory merely because the combination is done in contemplation of an IPO or sale.
    • Two or more individuals — do they control collectively under a contractual agreement?
  • Examples: merger between fellow subsidiaries · merger of subsidiary with parent · acquisition of an entity from an entity within the same group · bringing entities under common control into a corporate legal structure.

13.3 Method of accounting — pooling of interests App C · 8–12

  1. Assets and liabilities of the combining entities at their carrying amounts.
  2. No adjustments to reflect fair values, no new assets or liabilities. Only adjustment: harmonise accounting policies.
  3. Prior-period financial information restated as if the combination occurred from the beginning of the earliest period presented, irrespective of the actual date. If the combination (common control) occurred after that date, restate only from that date.
  • Consideration may be securities, cash or other assets. Securities at nominal value; assets other than cash at fair value.
  • Transferor's retained earnings are aggregated with the transferee's — or transferred to General Reserve, if any.
  • Identity of reserves preserved: transferor's General Reserve → transferee's General Reserve; Capital Reserve → Capital Reserve; Revaluation Reserve → Revaluation Reserve. So reserves distributable before stay distributable after.

Capital reserve = (Share capital issued + additional consideration in cash/other assets) − Share capital of transferor → present separately from other capital reserves; disclose nature and purpose

Acid test: the same shareholders control the entity before and after the reorganisation.

IFRS 3: bargain purchase gain → P&L. Ind AS 103: → OCI → capital reserve, or directly capital reserve if no clear evidence (same carve-out in Ind AS 28). Reason: continuation of Indian GAAP practice — gain at the time of acquiring a business is capital in nature.
