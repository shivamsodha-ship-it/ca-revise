Two tests for equity (Ind AS 32.16) — both must pass: (a) no obligation to pay cash · (b) if settled in own shares → fixed-for-fixed. Fail either → liability (or a liability component).
  • Contract needed — need not be written. Not financial: income tax (statutory), constructive obligations (Ind AS 37), PPE, inventory, ROU assets, intangibles, prepaid expenses and advances for goods/services (benefit is goods/services, not cash), deferred revenue, most warranties.

  • Rights issue carve-in: rights/options/warrants for a fixed number of own shares for a fixed amount of any currency, offered pro rata to all existing owners of that class → equity.
  • Ordinary share dividend → liability only when approved by shareholders (AGM); a board proposal creates no obligation.

Own-use exemption — contracts for non-financial items 109.2.4–2.7

  • A contract to buy/sell a non-financial item that can be settled net is treated like a derivative — unless entered into and held for the entity's expected purchase, sale or usage (own use) → executory contract, outside Ind AS 109.
  • "Settled net" includes: terms allow net settlement · practice of net-settling similar contracts · practice of taking delivery and selling quickly for trading profit · item readily convertible to cash.
  • May irrevocably designate FVTPL at inception if it removes an accounting mismatch. Written options that can be net settled are never own use.
  • Example 4: buy 100 t cocoa at 1,000 for delivery in 12 months, can't be net settled, for own use → not a derivative even though market moves to 1,500.

3Liability or equity — the decision path Ind AS 32.15–22

  1. Is there a contractual obligation? No → not a financial instrument (other Ind AS).
  2. Obligation to deliver cash / FA that the issuer can't unconditionally avoid? → Liability (even if amount tied to an index; even if indirect).
  3. Obligation to exchange on potentially unfavourable terms? → Liability. (Ex. 1: written call at ₹100 settled net; share at ₹110 → issuer pays ₹10 → liability.)
  4. Settled in own shares — is either the consideration or the number of shares variable? Yes → liability. Both fixed → equity.
  • Classify from the issuer's view, component by component, on substance — but substance never overrides the contract, and the contract can't override law in force (ignore expected future changes in law).
  • Can't avoid = liability even if: lack of funds, insufficient profits, need for regulatory/forex approval (Illus. 4).
  • Issuer's alternative is to transfer a non-financial asset worth much more (Illus. 6: a building) → still liability — it can avoid cash only by settling the non-financial obligation.

Puttable instruments & obligations only on liquidation — exception (32.16A–D)

Normally liabilities (mutual fund units, limited-life SPVs), but equity if: (1) pro rata share of net assets on liquidation (no cap — Illus. 7: Class B capped at ₹1 crore → not equity) · (2) in the most subordinate class (Illus. 8: manager's token units ranking last → equity; other units → liability) · (3) all instruments in that class have identical features (Illus. 9: different voting rights → neither is equity) · (4) no other obligation to pay cash or deliver variable shares (Illus. 10: extra conversion into variable shares → fails) · (5) cash flows based substantially on profit, change in net assets or FV of net assets · (6) issuer has no other contract with the holder that substantially fixes/restricts their return (Illus. 11: management contract for 50% of profit vs market 4–6% → fails). Puttables: 1–6. Liquidation-only: 1–3 and 6. Reclassify when conditions start/stop: liability → equity at carrying amount; equity → liability at FV, difference in equity.

4Compound instruments — split accounting Ind AS 32.28–32 · AG30–AG35

Step 1 — Liability component = PV of contractual cash flows (interest + principal / redemption premium), ignoring conversion, at the market rate for a similar instrument without the conversion option.

Step 2 — Equity component = Proceeds (FV of whole instrument) − Liability. Residual, never remeasured. No day-1 gain/loss.

Step 3 — Transaction costs → split in proportion to the allocation of proceeds; liability share goes into EIR, equity share deducted from equity.

Step 4 — Each year: finance cost = opening liability × market rate (EIR) → P&L; liability = opening + finance cost − coupon paid. It accretes to the redemption amount.

Other embedded derivatives (issuer's call / prepayment option) → included in the liability component, not equity.

6Classifying a financial asset — the 2 tests Ind AS 109.4.1

Step 1 — What is it? Derivative → FVTPL (unless hedging instrument). Equity share → FVTPL, or irrevocable FVOCI election if not held for trading. Debt → go to Step 2.

Step 2 — SPPI test (instrument level): are cash flows solely payments of principal and interest on principal outstanding? Fail → FVTPL.

Step 3 — Business model test (portfolio level, set by KMP): Hold to collect → Amortised cost · Collect and sell → FVOCI (debt, with recycling) · Other (trading, managed on FV) → FVTPL.

Step 4 — FV option: AC / FVOCI debt may be irrevocably designated FVTPL at initial recognition only if it removes or significantly reduces an accounting mismatch.

Modification, refinancing & processing fees

10% test: PV of new cash flows (incl. fees paid net of fees received, only between borrower and lender) discounted at the original EIR vs PV of remaining old cash flows (also at original EIR). Difference ≥ 10% → substantial.

Substantial → extinguishment: derecognise old (with its unamortised fees), recognise new at FV; new EIR on revised terms; costs/fees → part of gain/loss on extinguishment (P&L).

Not substantial → modification: revised cash flows discounted at original EIR; difference → P&L now; costs/fees adjust the carrying amount and amortise over remaining term.

12Derecognition of financial assets — the flowchart 109.3.2 · B3.2.1

0. Consolidate subsidiaries first (incl. SPVs/trusts) — test at group level.

1. Apply to a part only if the part is: specifically identified cash flows (interest strip) · fully proportionate share (90% of all cash flows) · proportionate share of identified flows (90% of interest). Otherwise whole asset (first 90% of collections; 90% sold with an 8% loss guarantee — Illus. 5).

2. Rights to cash flows expired? → derecognise. (Renegotiation can count: moratorium/extension, big rate cut, conversion right.)

3. Transferred the rights (sale, assignment, factoring)? — or a pass-through meeting all 3 conditions (pay only what is collected · can't sell/pledge the asset · remit without material delay, no reinvestment)? No → keep asset.

4. Transferred substantially all risks & rewards? → derecognise. Retained substantially all? → keep asset, cash received = liability.

5. Neither? → Control retained? (Can the transferee practically sell the asset unilaterally to a third party?) No → derecognise. Yes → continuing involvement.

16Ind AS 107 disclosures — what to remember Unit 7

  • Objective: users can evaluate (a) significance of financial instruments for position and performance, (b) nature and extent of risks and how they are managed.
  • Balance sheet: carrying amounts by category (FVTPL designated / mandatory, AC assets, AC liabilities, FVOCI debt, FVOCI equity designated); FVTPL-designated items → credit-risk effect; FVOCI equity → which investments, why, FV, dividends, transfers within equity, disposals (reason, FV, cumulative gain/loss); reclassifications (date, BM change explanation, amounts; EIR and interest after leaving FVTPL); offsetting (gross, set-off, net); collateral; allowance for credit losses; defaults and breaches.
  • P&L: net gains/losses per category, interest income/expense (EIR), fee income/expense, impairment.
  • Other: accounting policies, hedge accounting, fair value by class (and FV hierarchy per Ind AS 113); risks — qualitative and quantitative for credit, liquidity (maturity analysis) and market risk (sensitivity analysis).
